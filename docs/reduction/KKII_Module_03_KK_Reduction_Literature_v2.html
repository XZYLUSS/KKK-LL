Kaluza-Klein Reduction and Gauge Emergence

A Rigorous Treatment Based on Standard Mathematical Literature

We develop the theory of Kaluza-Klein dimensional reduction following the foundational works of Kaluza (1921), Klein (1926), and the modern formulation by Witten (1981) and Duff-Nilsson-Pope (1986). The emergence of Yang-Mills gauge fields from isometries of compact internal manifolds is derived rigorously using the theory of Riemannian submersions (O'Neill) and principal bundles. We carefully address global group structure (PSU vs SU), normalization conventions, consistent truncation assumptions, and cosmological constant contributions. All formulas are traced to standard references.

0. Conventions and Notation

Convention 0.1 (Lie Algebra and Gauge Field)

Throughout this module we adopt the following conventions:

  • Generators: Anti-Hermitian generators \(T_a\) satisfying \([T_a, T_b] = f_{ab}^{\;\;c} T_c\)
  • Trace normalization: \(\tr(T_a T_b) = -\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}\) for the fundamental representation of \(\SU(N)\)
  • Gauge connection: \(A = A_\mu^a T_a \, dx^\mu\) (anti-Hermitian valued)
  • Curvature: \(F = dA + A \wedge A\), so \(F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu + [A_\mu, A_\nu]\)
  • Gauge transformation: \(\delta A = D\epsilon = d\epsilon + [A, \epsilon]\), i.e., \(\delta A_\mu^a = \partial_\mu \epsilon^a + f_{bc}^{\;\;a} A_\mu^b \epsilon^c\)

With these conventions, the Yang-Mills action is:

$$S_{\mathrm{YM}} = \frac{1}{2g^2} \int \tr(F \wedge *F) = -\frac{1}{4g^2} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g}\, F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{a\mu\nu}$$
Convention 0.2 (Metric Signature and Indices)
  • Signature: \((-,+,+,\ldots,+)\) (mostly plus)
  • External indices: \(\mu, \nu, \rho, \ldots = 0, 1, \ldots, d-1\)
  • Internal indices: \(m, n, p, \ldots = 1, \ldots, n\)
  • Total indices: \(M, N, P, \ldots = 0, 1, \ldots, D-1\) where \(D = d + n\)
  • Lie algebra indices: \(a, b, c, \ldots = 1, \ldots, \dim G\)
Convention 0.3 (Killing Vector Normalization)

For a compact homogeneous space \(K = G/H\) with metric \(h\), let \(\{\xi_a\}\) be Killing vectors corresponding to Lie algebra generators \(\{T_a\}\). We normalize so that:

$$[\xi_a, \xi_b] = f_{ab}^{\;\;c} \xi_c$$

(same structure constants as the Lie algebra). The integrated Killing metric is:

$$\mathcal{G}_{ab} := \int_K d^n y \sqrt{h}\, h_{mn}(y) \xi_a^m(y) \xi_b^n(y)$$

For a symmetric space, this is proportional to \(\delta_{ab}\) by Ad-invariance and simplicity of the Lie algebra.

1. Historical Introduction

The idea of unifying gravity with gauge interactions through extra dimensions originated with Kaluza [Ka21] and Klein [Kl26].

[Ka21] T. Kaluza, "Zum Unitätsproblem der Physik," Sitzungsber. Preuss. Akad. Wiss. Berlin (Math. Phys.) (1921), 966–972.
[Kl26] O. Klein, "Quantentheorie und fünfdimensionale Relativitätstheorie," Z. Phys. 37 (1926), 895–906.

Kaluza's key insight was that a 5-dimensional metric \(G_{MN}\) on \(M_5 = M_4 \times S^1\) naturally decomposes into a 4D metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\), a vector field \(A_\mu\), and a scalar \(\phi\). Klein added the quantum-mechanical interpretation that compactness of \(S^1\) leads to charge quantization.

The modern non-Abelian generalization was developed by DeWitt [De64], Kerner [Ke68], Cho [Ch75], and especially the comprehensive treatments by Witten [Wi81] and Duff, Nilsson, and Pope [DNP86].

[Wi81] E. Witten, "Search for a Realistic Kaluza-Klein Theory," Nucl. Phys. B 186 (1981), 412–428.
[DNP86] M.J. Duff, B.E.W. Nilsson, and C.N. Pope, "Kaluza-Klein Supergravity," Phys. Rep. 130 (1986), 1–142.

2. Riemannian Submersions

The mathematical foundation for Kaluza-Klein theory is the theory of Riemannian submersions, developed by O'Neill [ON66].

[ON66] B. O'Neill, "The Fundamental Equations of a Submersion," Michigan Math. J. 13 (1966), 459–469.
Definition 2.1 (Riemannian Submersion — [ON66])

Let \((E, G)\) and \((M, g)\) be Riemannian manifolds. A smooth surjective map \(\pi: E \to M\) is a Riemannian submersion if:

  1. \(\pi\) is a submersion (i.e., \(d\pi_p: T_p E \to T_{\pi(p)}M\) is surjective for all \(p \in E\))
  2. The restriction \(d\pi|_{\mathcal{H}_p}: \mathcal{H}_p \to T_{\pi(p)}M\) is an isometry for all \(p \in E\)

where \(\mathcal{H}_p := (\ker d\pi_p)^\perp\) is the horizontal subspace (orthogonal complement with respect to \(G\)).

Remark 2.2 (Metric Dependence)

The horizontal distribution \(\mathcal{H}\) depends on the choice of the total space metric \(G\), not just on \(\pi\). Different metrics on \(E\) with the same fibers yield different horizontal distributions.

Definition 2.3 (Vertical and Horizontal Decomposition)

For a submersion \(\pi: E \to M\):

  • The vertical distribution: \(\mathcal{V} := \ker(d\pi) \subset TE\)
  • The horizontal distribution: \(\mathcal{H} := \mathcal{V}^\perp\) (w.r.t. \(G\))

We have the orthogonal decomposition \(T_p E = \mathcal{H}_p \oplus \mathcal{V}_p\) at each point. Denote the projections by \(\mathcal{H}: TE \to \mathcal{H}\) and \(\mathcal{V}: TE \to \mathcal{V}\).

Definition 2.4 (O'Neill Tensors — [ON66])

O'Neill defined two fundamental tensors that measure the geometry of a submersion:

The \(A\)-tensor (for horizontal fields): For horizontal \(X, Y\):

$$A_X Y := \mathcal{V}(\nabla_X Y)$$

This measures the non-integrability of the horizontal distribution. If \(A = 0\), the horizontal distribution is integrable (Frobenius).

The \(T\)-tensor (for vertical fields): For vertical \(U, V\):

$$T_U V := \mathcal{H}(\nabla_U V)$$

This measures the second fundamental form of the fibers. If \(T = 0\), the fibers are totally geodesic.

The full definitions extend to mixed arguments via the projection operators.

Theorem 2.5 (O'Neill's Curvature Formula — [ON66] Theorem 2)

Let \(\pi: (E, G) \to (M, g)\) be a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibers (\(T = 0\)). For horizontal fields \(X, Y, Z, W\) that are \(\pi\)-related to fields \(\bar{X}, \bar{Y}, \bar{Z}, \bar{W}\) on \(M\):

$$\langle R^E(X,Y)Z, W \rangle = \langle R^M(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})\bar{Z}, \bar{W} \rangle - 2\langle A_X Y, A_Z W \rangle + \langle A_Y Z, A_X W \rangle - \langle A_X Z, A_Y W \rangle$$

The \(A\)-tensor contributions to curvature are precisely what become the Yang-Mills \(F^2\) term in the reduced action.

Remark 2.6 (Physical Interpretation)

The key geometric insight: gauge field strength = obstruction to integrability of horizontal distribution. The \(A\)-tensor, measuring non-integrability, becomes identified with the Yang-Mills curvature \(F_{\mu\nu}\).

3. Principal Bundles and Connections

The geometric framework for non-Abelian gauge theories is the theory of principal bundles, following Kobayashi-Nomizu [KN63].

[KN63] S. Kobayashi and K. Nomizu, Foundations of Differential Geometry, Vol. I, Wiley-Interscience (1963), Chapter II.
Definition 3.1 (Principal \(G\)-Bundle — [KN63] §II.5)

A principal \(G\)-bundle is a fiber bundle \(\pi: P \to M\) with a free right action of a Lie group \(G\) on \(P\) such that \(M = P/G\) and the bundle is locally trivial.

Definition 3.2 (Principal Connection — [KN63] §II.1)

A connection on \(P \to M\) is a \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued 1-form \(\omega \in \Omega^1(P; \mathfrak{g})\) satisfying:

  1. \(\omega(A^*) = A\) for all \(A \in \mathfrak{g}\), where \(A^*\) is the fundamental vector field
  2. \(R_g^* \omega = \Ad_{g^{-1}} \omega\) for all \(g \in G\)

The connection defines a horizontal distribution \(\mathcal{H} = \ker(\omega)\).

Proposition 3.3 (KK Gauge Field as Connection)

In the Kaluza-Klein setting, the gauge fields \(A_\mu^a(x)\) are precisely the connection coefficients of a principal connection on the bundle of internal isometry orbits. Explicitly:

$$A \sim \omega(\partial_\mu^{\mathrm{hor}}), \qquad F \sim \Omega(\partial_\mu^{\mathrm{hor}}, \partial_\nu^{\mathrm{hor}})$$

where \(\Omega = d\omega + \frac{1}{2}[\omega \wedge \omega]\) is the curvature 2-form.

Remark 3.4

This identification is not merely a "coordinate trick" — it is a genuine geometric statement: the KK vector fields are connection coefficients, and their curvature is literally the curvature of the horizontal lift.

4. The Kaluza-Klein Metric Ansatz

Definition 4.1 (Product Manifold)

Consider a \(D\)-dimensional spacetime with product structure:

$$\mathcal{M}_D = M_d \times K_n, \quad D = d + n$$

where \(M_d\) is the external spacetime (typically \(d = 4\)) and \(K_n\) is a compact internal manifold of dimension \(n\).

Definition 4.2 (KK Metric Ansatz — [DNP86] Eq. (2.1))

Introduce coordinates \(x^M = (x^\mu, y^m)\). The Kaluza-Klein metric ansatz is:

$$ds_D^2 = g_{\mu\nu}(x) dx^\mu dx^\nu + h_{mn}(y)\big(dy^m + A^a_\mu(x) \xi_a^m(y) dx^\mu\big)\big(dy^n + A^b_\nu(x) \xi_b^n(y) dx^\nu\big)$$

where:

  • \(g_{\mu\nu}(x)\) is the \(d\)-dimensional external metric
  • \(h_{mn}(y)\) is a fixed background metric on \(K_n\)
  • \(\xi_a^m(y)\) are Killing vector fields on \((K_n, h)\), \(a = 1, \ldots, \dim G_{\mathrm{iso}}\)
  • \(A^a_\mu(x)\) are the gauge fields
Warning 4.3 (Truncation Assumptions)

The ansatz (Definition 4.2) involves two crucial truncations:

  1. Isometry truncation: We expand only along Killing vectors, truncating the infinite-dimensional diffeomorphism group to the finite-dimensional isometry group \(G_{\mathrm{iso}}\).
  2. Moduli freezing: We fix \(h_{mn}(y)\) as a background, ignoring scalar fields from metric deformations (breathing modes, shape moduli).

Consistent truncation: At the linearized level, this correctly identifies the massless gauge sector. However, nonlinear consistent truncation — wherein solutions of the reduced theory uplift to exact solutions of the \(D\)-dimensional theory — is highly nontrivial and fails for generic internal spaces. It is known to work for certain symmetric spaces (e.g., spheres in maximal supergravity [DNP86]), but for \(\CP^2\) in pure gravity, we work with an effective truncation focusing on the massless sector.

Remark 4.4 (Matrix Form)

In block matrix notation:

$$G_{MN} = \begin{pmatrix} g_{\mu\nu} + h_{ab} A^a_\mu A^b_\nu & h_{am} A^a_\mu \\ h_{bm} A^b_\nu & h_{mn} \end{pmatrix}$$

where \(h_{ab} := h_{mn} \xi_a^m \xi_b^n\) is the pointwise Killing metric.

5. Isometries and Killing Vector Fields

Definition 5.1 (Killing Vector Field — [KN63] §VI.3)

A vector field \(\xi\) on \((K, h)\) is a Killing vector field if it generates isometries:

$$\Lie_\xi h = 0 \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \nabla_m \xi_n + \nabla_n \xi_m = 0$$
Theorem 5.2 (Killing Vectors form a Lie Algebra — [KN63] §VI.3)

The Killing vectors on \((K, h)\) form a finite-dimensional Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g} = \mathrm{Lie}(\Isom(K))\):

$$[\xi_a, \xi_b] = f_{ab}^{\;\;c} \xi_c$$
Theorem 5.3 (Isometries of \(\CP^n\) — [KN63], [He78])

For \(\CP^n\) with the Fubini-Study metric:

$$\Isom(\CP^n) = \PSU(n+1) = \SU(n+1)/\Z_{n+1}$$

The Lie algebra is \(\mathfrak{su}(n+1)\) with \(\dim = (n+1)^2 - 1\).

Warning 5.4 (Global Gauge Group: PSU vs SU)

The gauge group emerging from pure Kaluza-Klein reduction is naturally the identity component of the isometry group, which for \(\CP^2\) is:

$$G_{\mathrm{gauge}} = \PSU(3) = \SU(3)/\Z_3$$

Not \(\SU(3)\). The distinction:

  • For adjoint fields (gauge bosons, adjoint scalars), \(\PSU(3)\) and \(\SU(3)\) are indistinguishable.
  • For matter fields in non-trivial representations (quarks in fundamental, etc.), the global structure matters for allowed representations and charge quantization.

To obtain \(\SU(3)\) as the gauge group, one typically needs additional structure (spin connections, matter fields, or orbifold identifications).

[He78] S. Helgason, Differential Geometry, Lie Groups, and Symmetric Spaces, Academic Press (1978).

6. Gauge Fields from Internal Diffeomorphisms

Theorem 6.1 (Gauge Transformation from Diffeomorphism — [Wi81], [DNP86])

Consider an infinitesimal diffeomorphism along a Killing vector with \(x\)-dependent parameter:

$$y^m \to y^m + \epsilon^a(x) \xi_a^m(y)$$

Under this transformation, the gauge fields transform as:

$$\boxed{\delta A^a_\mu = \partial_\mu \epsilon^a + f_{bc}^{\;\;a} A^b_\mu \epsilon^c = (D_\mu \epsilon)^a}$$

This is precisely the infinitesimal Yang-Mills gauge transformation (with our conventions from §0).

Proof sketch.

Under \(y^m \to y^m + \epsilon^a(x) \xi_a^m(y)\), the 1-form \(\theta^m := dy^m + A^a_\mu \xi_a^m dx^\mu\) transforms. Using the Killing algebra \([\xi_a, \xi_b] = f_{ab}^{\;\;c}\xi_c\), one shows:

$$\theta^m \to \theta^m + (\partial_\mu \epsilon^a)\xi_a^m dx^\mu + \epsilon^a \partial_n \xi_a^m \theta^n + O(\epsilon^2)$$

Requiring form-invariance of the metric yields the gauge transformation law.

∎
Theorem 6.2 (Field Strength = Curvature of Horizontal Distribution)

The Yang-Mills field strength:

$$F^a_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A^a_\nu - \partial_\nu A^a_\mu + f_{bc}^{\;\;a} A^b_\mu A^c_\nu$$

measures the failure of the horizontal distribution to be integrable. In the language of O'Neill tensors:

$$F^a_{\mu\nu} \xi_a \sim 2 A_{\partial_\mu^{\mathrm{hor}}} \partial_\nu^{\mathrm{hor}}$$

and in principal bundle language:

$$F^a_{\mu\nu} T_a = \Omega(\partial_\mu^{\mathrm{hor}}, \partial_\nu^{\mathrm{hor}})$$

7. Curvature Decomposition

Theorem 7.1 (Scalar Curvature Decomposition — [DNP86] Eq. (2.27))

For the KK metric ansatz with fixed internal metric (totally geodesic fibers):

$$R_D = R_d + R_K - \frac{1}{4} \kappa_{ab}(y) F^a_{\mu\nu} F^{b\mu\nu} + \text{(total derivatives)}$$

where \(\kappa_{ab}(y) = h_{mn}(y) \xi_a^m(y) \xi_b^n(y)\) is the pointwise Killing metric.

Definition 7.2 (Pointwise and Integrated Killing Metrics)

Pointwise Killing metric:

$$\kappa_{ab}(y) = h_{mn}(y) \xi_a^m(y) \xi_b^n(y)$$

This is not the Lie algebra Killing form; it is the metric contraction of Killing vectors at each point.

Integrated Killing metric:

$$\mathcal{G}_{ab} = \int_K d^n y \sqrt{h}\, \kappa_{ab}(y) = \int_K d^n y \sqrt{h}\, h(\xi_a, \xi_b)$$

This is an \(L^2\) inner product on Killing vectors, and determines the gauge kinetic matrix.

Theorem 7.3 (Integrated Killing Metric on Symmetric Spaces)

For a symmetric space \(K = G/H\), the integrated Killing metric \(\mathcal{G}_{ab}\) is Ad-invariant (since the Killing vectors transform under Ad). By Schur's lemma applied to simple Lie algebras:

$$\mathcal{G}_{ab} = C \cdot \delta_{ab}$$

where \(C\) depends on \(\vol(K)\) and the average norm-squared of Killing vectors.

Remark 7.4 (Terminology Clarification)

On a homogeneous space, \(\mathcal{G}_{ab}\) defines an Ad-invariant bilinear form on \(\mathfrak{g}\). By simplicity, any such form is proportional to the Cartan-Killing form. Thus while \(\kappa_{ab}(y)\) is not the Killing form, its integral \(\mathcal{G}_{ab}\) is proportional to it.

8. Dimensional Reduction of the Action

Theorem 8.1 (Reduction of Einstein-Hilbert Action — [DNP86] §2.3)

The \(D\)-dimensional Einstein-Hilbert action:

$$S_D = \frac{1}{2\kappa_D^2} \int_{\mathcal{M}_D} d^D X \sqrt{-G}\, R_D$$

reduces upon integration over \(K\) to:

$$S_d = \frac{\vol(K)}{2\kappa_D^2} \int_{M_d} d^d x \sqrt{-g}\, R_d - \frac{1}{2\kappa_D^2} \int_{M_d} d^d x \sqrt{-g}\, \frac{1}{4}\mathcal{G}_{ab} F^a_{\mu\nu} F^{b\mu\nu} + \ldots$$
Proposition 8.2 (Effective Couplings)

The effective \(d\)-dimensional gravitational and gauge couplings are:

$$\frac{1}{2\kappa_d^2} = \frac{\vol(K)}{2\kappa_D^2}$$

The gauge kinetic term, written as an \(L^2\) norm on Killing vectors:

$$S_{\mathrm{gauge}} = -\frac{1}{4} \int d^d x \sqrt{-g} \left(\frac{\mathcal{G}_{ab}}{2\kappa_D^2}\right) F^a_{\mu\nu} F^{b\mu\nu}$$

Reading off the standard form \(-\frac{1}{4g^2} F^a F^a\), we identify:

$$\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} = \frac{\mathcal{G}_{aa}}{2\kappa_D^2} = \frac{1}{2\kappa_D^2} \int_K d^n y \sqrt{h}\, \|\xi_a\|^2$$

(no sum on \(a\); assuming \(\mathcal{G}_{ab} \propto \delta_{ab}\)).

Remark 8.3 (No Extra Coupling Constant)

The gauge coupling emerges directly from the geometry — there is no arbitrary \(g\) parameter inserted by hand. The \(L^2\) norm of Killing vectors determines everything.

9. The 4D Effective Theory and Vacuum Energy

Master Formula: 4D Effective Action (Massless Sector)
$$S_{\mathrm{eff}} = \int d^d x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{1}{2\kappa_d^2} R_d - \frac{1}{4g^2} F^a_{\mu\nu} F^{a\mu\nu} - V_{\mathrm{eff}} \right]$$

where the gauge group \(G_{\mathrm{gauge}} \subseteq \Isom_0(K)\).

Warning 9.1 (Cosmological Constant from Internal Curvature)

For an internal space with \(R_K \neq 0\), dimensional reduction produces a contribution to the 4D effective potential:

$$V_{\mathrm{eff}} \supset -\frac{\vol(K)}{2\kappa_D^2} R_K$$

For \(\CP^2\) with \(R_K = 24/b^2 > 0\), this acts as a negative 4D cosmological constant (with our sign conventions), i.e., anti-de Sitter.

Consequence: Pure gravity on \(M_4 \times \CP^2\) does not naturally yield 4D Minkowski space. One requires:

  • A positive \(D\)-dimensional cosmological constant \(\Lambda_D > 0\) to cancel, or
  • Form-field fluxes, or
  • Higher-curvature (Lovelock) terms
Theorem 9.2 (Spontaneous Compactification — [CR76], [DNP86])

A product vacuum \(M_d \times K_n\) solves the \(D\)-dimensional Einstein equations with cosmological constant \(\Lambda_D\) if both factors are Einstein:

$$R^{(K)}_{mn} = \lambda_K h_{mn}, \qquad R^{(M)}_{\mu\nu} = \lambda_M g_{\mu\nu}$$

The block-diagonal Einstein equations then give two constraints:

$$\lambda_M \frac{d-2}{2} + \lambda_K \frac{n}{2} = \Lambda_D$$ $$\lambda_M \frac{d}{2} + \lambda_K \frac{n-2}{2} = \Lambda_D$$

Solving simultaneously:

$$\lambda_M = \frac{2\Lambda_D}{D-2}, \qquad \lambda_K = \frac{2\Lambda_D}{D-2}$$

i.e., both factors have the same Einstein constant, determined by \(\Lambda_D\).

[CR76] E. Cremmer and J. Scherk, "Spontaneous Compactification of Space in an Einstein-Yang-Mills-Higgs Model," Nucl. Phys. B 108 (1976), 409–416.
Remark 9.3 (Breathing Mode and Einstein Frame)

If the overall scale \(b(x)\) of the internal space is promoted to a dynamical field (breathing mode), the effective action acquires scalar kinetic and potential terms. To obtain canonical Einstein gravity, one must perform a Weyl rescaling to Einstein frame. The relation \(\kappa_d^{-2} = \vol(K) \kappa_D^{-2}\) then becomes field-dependent before the rescaling.

10. Application to \(\CP^2\)

Proposition 10.1 (Geometric Data of \(\CP^2\))

For \(\CP^2\) with Fubini-Study metric scaled so that the holomorphic sectional curvature is \(4/b^2\):

Quantity Value Reference
Real dimension \(n = 4\)
Isometry group \(\PSU(3) = \SU(3)/\Z_3\) [He78]
Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{su}(3)\), dim = 8
Volume \(\displaystyle\vol(\CP^2) = \frac{\pi^2 b^4}{2}\)
Scalar curvature \(\displaystyle R_{\CP^2} = \frac{24}{b^2}\)
Einstein constant \(\displaystyle\Ric = \frac{6}{b^2} g_{FS}\)
Theorem 10.2 (Gauge Coupling from \(\CP^2\))

The integrated Killing metric on \(\CP^2 = \SU(3)/\U(2)\) is:

$$\mathcal{G}_{ab} = C \cdot b^6 \cdot \delta_{ab}$$

where \(C\) is a numerical constant depending on normalization conventions. The scaling is:

  • \(\vol(\CP^2) \sim b^4\)
  • \(\|\xi_a\|^2 \sim b^2\) (Killing vectors scale with the metric)
  • Hence \(\mathcal{G}_{ab} \sim b^6\)

The gauge coupling therefore scales as:

$$\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2} = \frac{C \cdot b^6}{2\kappa_8^2}$$
Remark 10.3 (Normalization Statement)

With the conventions of §0 (\(\tr(T_a T_b) = -\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}\), Killing vectors normalized so \([\xi_a, \xi_b] = f_{ab}^c \xi_c\)), one has:

$$\int_{\CP^2} d^4 y \sqrt{h}\, h(\xi_a, \xi_b) = \frac{3\pi^2 b^6}{8} \delta_{ab}$$

leading to \(g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{-2} = \frac{3\pi^2 b^6}{16\kappa_8^2}\). The numerical coefficient is derivable and checkable given explicit Killing vector expressions.

Example 10.4 (Killing Vectors in Local Coordinates)

In the affine chart \(\{[1: z^1: z^2]\} \subset \CP^2\), the Cartan generators are:

$$\xi_3 = -iz^1 \partial_{z^1} + i\bar{z}^1 \partial_{\bar{z}^1} - \frac{i}{2}z^2 \partial_{z^2} + \frac{i}{2}\bar{z}^2 \partial_{\bar{z}^2}$$ $$\xi_8 = -\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}\left(z^1 \partial_{z^1} - \bar{z}^1 \partial_{\bar{z}^1} + z^2 \partial_{z^2} - \bar{z}^2 \partial_{\bar{z}^2}\right)$$

Caveat: These are local representatives valid only on this coordinate patch. Globally, each Killing field extends smoothly over all of \(\CP^2\) (since it comes from the global \(\SU(3)\) action), but any explicit coordinate formula is patchwise.

11. Product Manifolds and \((\CP^2)^3\)

Proposition 11.1 (Isometry of Product)

For the product \(Y = K_1 \times K_2 \times \cdots \times K_r\):

$$\Isom_0(Y) \supseteq \Isom_0(K_1) \times \Isom_0(K_2) \times \cdots \times \Isom_0(K_r)$$

with equality when the factors are pairwise non-isometric.

Theorem 11.2 (Trinification from \((\CP^2)^3\))

For \(Y = \CP^2_c \times \CP^2_L \times \CP^2_R\):

$$\Isom_0(Y) = \frac{\SU(3)_c \times \SU(3)_L \times \SU(3)_R}{(\Z_3)^3}$$

providing a geometric realization of the trinification gauge group (at the Lie algebra level).

Warning 11.3 (Discrete Enhancement)

Because the three \(\CP^2\) factors are isometric to each other, the full isometry group includes a discrete permutation symmetry:

$$\Isom((\CP^2)^3) \supset \Isom_0((\CP^2)^3) \rtimes S_3$$

where \(S_3\) permutes the three factors. This is not essential for gauge boson physics but becomes important for:

  • Discrete gauge symmetries and orbifold identifications
  • "Family symmetry" if generations are identified with the three factors
Proposition 11.4 (Volume and Planck Mass)

With radii \(b_1, b_2, b_3\):

$$\vol(Y) = \frac{\pi^6}{8} b_1^4 b_2^4 b_3^4$$

The 4D Planck mass is related to the 16D Planck mass by:

$$M_{\mathrm{Pl},4}^2 = M_{\mathrm{Pl},16}^{14} \cdot \vol(Y)$$

(using \([M_{\mathrm{Pl},D}] = [\mathrm{mass}]^{(D-2)/2}\) and \(D - 2 = 14\)).

16D → 4D Reduction Summary

For \(\mathcal{M}_{16} = M_4 \times (\CP^2)^3\):

  • Connected gauge group: \([\SU(3)_c \times \SU(3)_L \times \SU(3)_R]/(\Z_3)^3\)
  • Gauge bosons: \(8 + 8 + 8 = 24\) vectors
  • Gauge couplings: \(g_i^{-2} \propto b_i^6 / \kappa_{16}^2\)
  • Cosmological constant: Requires tuning \(\Lambda_{16}\) or additional ingredients for Minkowski
  • Discrete symmetry: \(S_3\) permuting the three \(\CP^2\) factors

12. Bridge to Fermions

Warning 12.1 (Spin Structure Obstruction)

The manifold \(\CP^2\) is not spin: its second Stiefel-Whitney class is non-trivial:

$$w_2(T\CP^2) = c_1(T\CP^2) \mod 2 = 3H \mod 2 = H \neq 0$$

Consequently, spinor fields cannot be defined on \(\CP^2\) or \((\CP^2)^3\) in the ordinary sense.

Proposition 12.2 (Spin\(^c\) Resolution)

Although \(\CP^2\) is not spin, it admits a canonical Spin\(^c\) structure. Every Kähler manifold does [LM89]. The Spin\(^c\) Dirac operator can then be used to define fermionic fields.

The key geometric data:

  • Determinant line bundle: \(L = K^{-1} = \mathcal{O}(3)\)
  • Spin\(^c\) spinor bundle: \(S^c \cong \Lambda^{0,*}T^*\CP^2\)
