§1.2 Intrinsic Invariants on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (Fubini–Study, Cohomology-Normalized)

We use the standard cohomology normalization for the Kähler form $\omega$ on each $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factor:

$$\boxed{\;\;\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\frac{\omega\wedge\omega}{2} \;=\; \frac{(2\pi)^2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}H^2 \;=\; 2\pi^2.\;\;}$$

This fixes the overall volume convention and removes the common factor-of-$4$ ambiguity that appears when $\omega$ is rescaled.

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathrm{Ric}(g_{\mathrm{FS}})=6\,g_{\mathrm{FS}},\qquad R(g_{\mathrm{FS}})=24.\;\;}$$

$$R(b^2g_{\mathrm{FS}})=\frac{24}{b^2}.$$

§1.5 Existence Proof: Stabilized $b_\star$ from Lovelock Alone

Field Equations

External equation (variation w.r.t. $g_{\mu\nu}$):

$$M_4^2(b)\,G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b)\,g_{\mu\nu} = 0$$

Breathing mode equation (variation w.r.t. $b$):

$$\frac{1}{2}M_4^{2\prime}(b)\,R_4 - \Lambda_{\text{grav}}'(b) = 0$$

Einstein Frame Formulation

Define the Einstein frame potential:

$$V_E(b) = \frac{\Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b)}{M_4^4(b)}$$

The extremum condition becomes:

$$\frac{dV_E}{db} = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Lambda_{\text{grav}}'(b)\,M_4^2(b) = 4\Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b)\,M_4^{2\prime}(b)$$

Existence Theorem

Theorem (Pure Lovelock Stabilization):

For generic Lovelock couplings $\{\alpha_k\}$, the Einstein frame potential $V_E(b)$ is a ratio of polynomials in $b^2$. If:

  1. $V_E(b) \to +\infty$ as $b \to 0^+$ (internal space collapse)
  2. $V_E(b) \to +\infty$ as $b \to +\infty$ (decompactification)
  3. $M_4^2(b) > 0$ in some interval $(b_{\min}, b_{\max})$

then $V_E(b)$ has at least one minimum at some $b_\star \in (b_{\min}, b_{\max})$.

Physical interpretation: The competition between different Lovelock terms (with different $b$-scalings) creates a potential well that stabilizes the breathing mode without requiring any flux.

Example: Cubic Truncation Stabilization

Consider $A_0, A_1, A_2, A_3 \neq 0$ with $A_{k\geq 4} = 0$.

The potential is:

$$\mathcal{L}(b) = \frac{A_0}{8}b^{12} + 9A_1 b^{10} + 504A_2 b^8 + 20736A_3 b^6$$

Extremum condition $\mathcal{L}'(b) = 0$:

$$\frac{12A_0}{8}b^{11} + 90A_1 b^9 + 4032A_2 b^7 + 124416A_3 b^5 = 0$$

Dividing by $b^5$ (assuming $b > 0$):

$$\frac{3A_0}{2}b^6 + 90A_1 b^4 + 4032A_2 b^2 + 124416A_3 = 0$$

This is a cubic in $x = b^2$:

$$\frac{3A_0}{2}x^3 + 90A_1 x^2 + 4032A_2 x + 124416A_3 = 0$$

For appropriate sign choices of $\{A_0, A_1, A_2, A_3\}$, this cubic has a real positive root $x_\star = b_\star^2 > 0$, giving a stabilized breathing mode.

§1.6 Stability Analysis and Mass Matrix

Second Derivative Test

At a critical point $b_\star$ where $V_E'(b_\star) = 0$:

$$V_E''(b_\star) = \frac{\Lambda_{\text{grav}}''(b_\star)}{M_4^4(b_\star)} - \frac{8\Lambda_{\text{grav}}'(b_\star)M_4^{2\prime}(b_\star)}{M_4^6(b_\star)} + \ldots$$

$$V_E''(b_\star) = \frac{1}{M_4^4(b_\star)}\left[\Lambda_{\text{grav}}''(b_\star) - \frac{32\Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b_\star)(M_4^{2\prime}(b_\star))^2}{M_4^4(b_\star)} + \ldots\right]$$

Stability Condition

$$\boxed{V_E''(b_\star) > 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \text{minimum (stable)}}$$

Modulus Mass

The canonically normalized mass of the breathing mode fluctuation:

$$\boxed{m_b^2 = \frac{V_E''(b_\star)}{K(b_\star)}}$$

where $K(b_\star)$ is the kinetic term normalization from the dimensional reduction.

§2.2 Flux Quantization — Period and $L^2$ Normalization (Correct Convention)

$$\alpha_6:=H_c\wedge H_L\wedge H_R,\qquad \int_{\Sigma_6}\alpha_6=1.$$

Dirac quantization for a 6-form field strength takes the standard form

$$\boxed{\;\int_{\Sigma_6}G_6 = 2\pi\,N,\qquad N\in\mathbb{Z}.\;}$$

$L^2$ Normalization (Energy Computation)

For the kinetic term, it is convenient to also impose the global normalization on $Y_{12}$:

$$\boxed{\;\int_{Y_{12}}\alpha_6\wedge\star\alpha_6=1.\;}$$

Then set

$$\boxed{\;G_6 = 2\pi N\,\alpha_6.\;}$$

$$S_{G_6}=-\frac{1}{2\cdot 6!}\int d^{16}x\sqrt{-g}\,G_6^2,$$

independent of $b$ at this stage (because $p=d/2$ with $p=6$, $d=12$).

Translation note: If you use a different normalization (e.g. absorb $(2\pi)^k$ factors into $\alpha_6$), all formulas are identical after redefining $N$ accordingly. The physical input is the quantized period and the $L^2$ norm used in the kinetic term.

§2.3 Why $G_6$ is "Clean": $b$-Independence of $\Lambda_{\text{flux}}$

Key Property: In the $L^2$-normalized convention, $\Lambda_{\text{flux}}$ is independent of the breathing mode $b$.

Derivation

Step 1: The $G_6$ flux energy is:

$$\rho_{G_6} = \frac{1}{2 \cdot 6!}|G_6|^2$$

Step 2: For $L^2$-normalized flux:

$$|G_6|^2 = \frac{(2\pi N)^2}{\text{Vol}(Y_{12})} = \frac{(2\pi N)^2}{(\pi^6/8)b^{12}} \cdot b^{12} \cdot (\text{internal metric factors})$$

The $b$-dependence from the volume cancels with the $b$-dependence from raising indices.

Step 3: After integrating over $Y_{12}$:

$$\Lambda_{\text{flux}} = \frac{\pi^2 N^2}{360}$$

No $b$-dependence!

Consequences for Stabilization

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} \Lambda_{\text{tot}}(b) &= \Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b) + \Lambda_{\text{flux}}\\[8pt] \Lambda_{\text{tot}}'(b) &= \Lambda_{\text{grav}}'(b)\\[8pt] \Lambda_{\text{tot}}''(b) &= \Lambda_{\text{grav}}''(b) \end{aligned}}$$

The $G_6$ flux does not affect the location of the extremum $b_\star$!

It only shifts the value of the vacuum energy at that extremum.

Physical intuition: The $G_6$ flux acts like a true cosmological constant — it contributes equally to all directions and doesn't create any pressure gradients that would push the internal space to expand or contract.

§2.4 Vacuum Energy Tuning: Minkowski/dS/AdS Targets (and when $b_\star$ shifts)

Total Jordan-Frame Cosmological Term

After Step 1, add the $G_6$ contribution:

$$\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b)=\Lambda_{\mathrm{grav}}(b)+\Lambda_{\mathrm{flux}},\qquad \Lambda_{\mathrm{flux}}=\frac{\pi^2}{360}N^2.$$

The 4D Jordan-frame action is then

$$S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\int d^4x\sqrt{-g_4}\left[\frac12\,M_4^2(b)\,R_4-\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b)\right].$$

Coupled Equations for Constant $b$ and Maximally Symmetric $X_4$

For a maximally symmetric $X_4$ one has $R_{\mu\nu}=\kappa_4 g_{\mu\nu}$, so $R_4=4\kappa_4$. The Einstein equation gives

$$\boxed{\;\kappa_4=\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b)}{3\,M_4^2(b)}.\;}$$

Varying the action w.r.t. $b$ (and then taking $b=\text{const}$) gives

$$\boxed{\;\frac12\,M_4^{2\,\prime}(b)\,R_4-\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}'(b)=0.\;}$$

Eliminating $R_4$ yields an equivalent Einstein-frame extremum condition for

$$\boxed{\;V_E(b)=\frac{\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b)}{M_4^4(b)}.\;}$$

Thus $b_\star$ is determined by $V_E'(b_\star)=0$ and stability by $V_E''(b_\star)>0$.

When the “Step 2” Tuning Does (Not) Move $b_\star$

  • Minkowski target: If you enforce $\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b_\star)=0$, then $V_E'(b_\star)=0\iff \Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}'(b_\star)=0$ and $V_E''(b_\star)>0\iff \Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}''(b_\star)>0$. Since $\Lambda_{\mathrm{flux}}$ is constant, the derivative conditions are unaffected, so tuning $N$ to achieve $\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b_\star)=0$ does not spoil the stabilized $b_\star$.
  • dS/AdS targets: If $\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}(b_\star)\neq 0$, the extremum equation depends on the absolute value of $\Lambda_{\mathrm{tot}}$ and a constant shift generally moves $b_\star$. In practice, if the Lovelock well is stiff (large $V_E''$), the shift can be parametrically small for moderate tuning.

Direct Tuning Formula at a Fixed $b_\star$

If (by design) you want to keep a chosen stabilized $b_\star$ and simply tune the 4D vacuum energy, solve:

$$\boxed{\;\Lambda_{\mathrm{grav}}(b_\star)+\frac{\pi^2}{360}N^2=\Lambda_{\mathrm{target}}.\;}$$

In particular, for Minkowski $\Lambda_{\mathrm{target}}=0$:

$$\boxed{\;N^2=-\frac{360}{\pi^2}\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{grav}}(b_\star)\;\;\;\;(\text{requires }\Lambda_{\mathrm{grav}}(b_\star)\le 0).\;}$$

§3.2 Yang-Mills Flux $F^{(i)} = f_i \omega_i$ — Corrected Contractions

The YM Action

$$S_{\text{YM}} = -\frac{1}{4g_{16}^2}\int d^{16}x\sqrt{-g}\,\text{tr}(F_{MN}F^{MN})$$

Kähler Flux Ansatz

A symmetry-preserving flux on each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$:

$$F^{(i)} = f_i\,\omega_i$$

where $\omega_i$ is the Kähler form on $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$.

Critical Correction: Kähler Form Contraction

IMPORTANT

A common sign error occurs in the literature. The correct identity is:

$$\boxed{\omega_{ac}\omega_b{}^c = +g_{ab}}$$

NOT $-g_{ab}$!

Derivation of Correct Identity

Step 1: In an orthonormal frame on a Kähler 4-manifold:

$$\omega = e^1 \wedge e^2 + e^3 \wedge e^4$$

Components: $\omega_{12} = 1$, $\omega_{21} = -1$, $\omega_{34} = 1$, $\omega_{43} = -1$ (others zero).

Step 2: Compute $\omega_{ac}\omega_b{}^c = \omega_{ac}\omega_{bc}$ (orthonormal frame):

For $a = b = 1$:

$$\omega_{1c}\omega_{1c} = \omega_{12}\omega_{12} = (1)(1) = 1 = g_{11}$$

For $a = b = 2$:

$$\omega_{2c}\omega_{2c} = \omega_{21}\omega_{21} = (-1)(-1) = 1 = g_{22}$$

Similarly for $a = b = 3, 4$. Cross terms ($a \neq b$) vanish.

Step 3: Therefore:

$$\omega_{ac}\omega_b{}^c = +\delta_{ab} = +g_{ab} \quad \checkmark$$
Source of confusion: The almost complex structure $J$ satisfies $J^a{}_c J^c{}_b = -\delta^a_b$. But the Kähler form $\omega_{ab} = g_{ac}J^c{}_b$ gives $\omega_{ac}\omega_b{}^c = +g_{ab}$.

Field Strength Squared

For $F^{(i)} = f_i\omega_i$:

Contraction (on $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$):

$$F^{(i)}_{ac}F^{(i)c}{}_b = f_i^2\omega_{ac}\omega_b{}^c = f_i^2\,g_{ab}$$

Trace:

$$F^{(i)}_{ab}F^{(i)ab} = f_i^2\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab} = f_i^2 \cdot 4 = 4f_i^2$$
$$\boxed{|F^{(i)}|^2 = F^{(i)}_{ab}F^{(i)ab} = 4f_i^2}$$

Yang-Mills Stress-Energy Tensor

General Formula

$$T_{MN}^{(F)} = \frac{1}{g_{16}^2}\left(F_{MP}F_N{}^P - \frac{1}{4}g_{MN}F_{PQ}F^{PQ}\right)$$

On the Supporting $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ ("Self" Block)

$$T^{(i)}_{ab}\big|_{\mathbb{CP}^2_i} = \frac{1}{g_{16}^2}\left(F^{(i)}_{ac}F^{(i)c}{}_b - \frac{1}{4}g_{ab}|F^{(i)}|^2\right)$$ $$= \frac{1}{g_{16}^2}\left(f_i^2 g_{ab} - \frac{1}{4}g_{ab} \cdot 4f_i^2\right)$$ $$= \frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2}(g_{ab} - g_{ab}) = 0$$

External Space

$$T^{(i)}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{g_{16}^2}\left(0 - \frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu} \cdot 4f_i^2\right) = -\frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2}g_{\mu\nu}$$

Other Internal Blocks ($\mathbb{CP}^2_j$, $j \neq i$)

$$T^{(i)}_{ab}\big|_{\mathbb{CP}^2_{j\neq i}} = -\frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2}g_{ab}$$

YM Stress-Energy Summary

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} T^{(i)}_{ab}\big|_{\mathbb{CP}^2_i} &= 0 &&\text{(self block)}\\[8pt] T^{(i)}_{\mu\nu} &= -\frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2}g_{\mu\nu} &&\text{(external)}\\[8pt] T^{(i)}_{ab}\big|_{\mathbb{CP}^2_{j\neq i}} &= -\frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2}g_{ab} &&\text{(other internal)} \end{aligned}}$$
Key observation: The "self" block sees zero stress, but the external space and other internal blocks see negative pressure (effective cosmological constant). This creates anisotropic effects.

Flux Quantization

Dirac quantization on $\mathbb{CP}^1 \subset \mathbb{CP}^2_i$:

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}F^{(i)} = \frac{f_i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega_i = \frac{f_i \cdot \pi}{2\pi} = \frac{f_i}{2} = n_i \in \mathbb{Z}$$
$$\boxed{f_i = 2n_i, \qquad n_i \in \mathbb{Z}}$$

$b$-Dependence of YM Contribution

For metric $g^{(i)}_{ab} = b^2\hat{g}^{(i)}_{ab}$:

$$|F^{(i)}|^2 = F^{(i)}_{ab}F^{(i)ab} = f_i^2\omega_{ab}g^{ac}g^{bd}\omega_{cd}$$ $$= f_i^2 \cdot b^{-4}\hat{\omega}_{ab}\hat{g}^{ac}\hat{g}^{bd}\hat{\omega}_{cd} = \frac{4f_i^2}{b^4}$$

The YM energy density scales as:

$$\rho_{\text{YM}} \sim \frac{f_i^2}{b^4}$$

YM Contribution to Effective Potential

$$\boxed{\Lambda_{\text{YM}}(b) \sim \sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{f_i^2}{g_{16}^2 b^4}}$$

This is $b$-dependent and affects the location of $b_\star$!

§3.3 Freund-Rubin $G_4$ — External vs. Internal Threading

The 4-Form Action

$$S_{G_4} = -\frac{1}{2 \cdot 4!}\int d^{16}x\sqrt{-g}\,G_{MNPQ}G^{MNPQ}$$

Ansatz Options

Option A: External Threading

$G_4$ fills the external $X_4$:

$$G_4 = g_0\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}dx^\mu \wedge dx^\nu \wedge dx^\rho \wedge dx^\sigma = g_0\,\text{vol}_{X_4}$$

Option B: Internal Threading

$G_4$ fills a 4-cycle in $Y_{12}$ (e.g., one $\mathbb{CP}^2$):

$$G_4 = h_i\,\omega_i \wedge \omega_i = h_i\,\text{vol}_{\mathbb{CP}^2_i}$$

External $G_4$ (Freund-Rubin)

Field Strength Squared

$$G_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}G^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = g_0^2\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$$

In Lorentzian signature:

$$\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = -4! = -24$$ $$|G_4|^2 = -24g_0^2$$

Stress-Energy Tensor

$$T_{MN}^{(G_4)} = \frac{1}{2 \cdot 3!}\left(G_{MPQR}G_N{}^{PQR} - \frac{1}{8}g_{MN}|G_4|^2\right)$$

External components:

$$G_{\mu PQR}G_\nu{}^{PQR} = g_0^2\epsilon_{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\epsilon_\nu{}^{\rho\sigma\tau} = -6g_0^2\,g_{\mu\nu}$$ $$T^{(G_4)}_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{12}\left(-6g_0^2 g_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{8}g_{\mu\nu}(-24g_0^2)\right)$$ $$= \frac{1}{12}(-6g_0^2 + 3g_0^2)g_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{g_0^2}{4}g_{\mu\nu}$$

Internal components:

$$G_{m PQR}G_n{}^{PQR} = 0 \quad \text{(no internal legs)}$$ $$T^{(G_4)}_{mn} = \frac{1}{12}\left(0 - \frac{1}{8}g_{mn}(-24g_0^2)\right) = +\frac{g_0^2}{4}g_{mn}$$

Freund-Rubin Stress-Energy

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned} T^{(G_4)}_{\mu\nu} &= -\frac{g_0^2}{4}g_{\mu\nu} &&\text{(negative pressure externally)}\\[8pt] T^{(G_4)}_{mn} &= +\frac{g_0^2}{4}g_{mn} &&\text{(positive pressure internally)} \end{aligned}}$$
Physical effect: External $G_4$ acts like a positive cosmological constant in $X_4$ and a negative cosmological constant in $Y_{12}$. This tends to expand $X_4$ and contract $Y_{12}$ — useful for achieving AdS external space.

§3.4 Anisotropic Moduli and Phenomenological Applications

Breaking the Symmetric Ansatz

So far we used a single breathing mode $b$ for all three $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors. In reality, we can have:

$$g^{(i)}_{a_i b_i} = b_i^2\,\hat{g}^{(i)}_{a_i b_i}$$

with independent $b_1, b_2, b_3$.

The YM fluxes $F^{(i)}$ naturally break this symmetry because they act differently on different factors.

Applications

  • Moduli stabilization: YM fluxes can stabilize anisotropic moduli $b_i/b_j$.
  • Gauge symmetry breaking: Non-abelian gauge fields on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ can break to subgroups.
  • Chiral fermions: The index theorem on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with flux gives chiral zero modes.
  • Generations: Multiple $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors can give multiple fermion generations.

Complete Stress-Energy Tensor

External Equations

$$M_4^2(b)\,G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\text{tot}}(b)\,g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa_4^2\,T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{matter}}$$

where:

$$\Lambda_{\text{tot}}(b) = \Lambda_{\text{grav}}(b) + \Lambda_{\text{flux}}^{(G_6)} + \Lambda_{\text{YM}}(b) + \Lambda_{G_4}$$

Internal Equations

For each $\mathbb{CP}^2_k$:

$$G_{a_k b_k}^{(\text{Lovelock})} + \Lambda\,g_{a_k b_k} = \kappa_{16}^2\left(\sum_{j \neq k}\frac{f_j^2}{g_{16}^2}g_{a_k b_k} + T^{(G_4)}_{a_k b_k} + \ldots\right)$$
Key point: The internal equations become more complicated with YM and $G_4$ fluxes. The symmetric $b_1 = b_2 = b_3 = b$ ansatz may no longer be consistent, requiring a more general analysis.
