§ AThe Complex Projective Plane: Topology and Atlas

A.1 — Definition as a Quotient

Definition A.1 — $\mathbb{CP}^2$ as a Complex Manifold

The complex projective plane $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the quotient $$\mathbb{CP}^2 \;\equiv\; (\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\})/\sim,\qquad (z^0,z^1,z^2)\sim (\lambda z^0,\lambda z^1,\lambda z^2)\quad\forall\,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^*\equiv\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.$$ Equivalence classes are denoted by homogeneous coordinates $[z^0:z^1:z^2]$. The natural projection $\pi:\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{CP}^2$, $\pi(z)=[z^0:z^1:z^2]$, equips $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with the quotient topology and a unique complex manifold structure of complex dimension $\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{CP}^2=2$ (real dimension $4$) such that $\pi$ is holomorphic [We80, Ch.~I, §1; KN69, Ch.~IX, Ex.~2.4].

The quotient is taken with respect to the holomorphic free action $\mathbb{C}^*\curvearrowright\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}$ of the multiplicative group on the complement of the origin: freeness follows from the fact that $\lambda z=z$ for some nonzero $z$ forces $\lambda=1$, and the orbit through $z$ is the punctured complex line $\mathbb{C}^* z$. The topological space $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the space of such complex lines through the origin.

Equivalent Definitions

Three other definitions of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ that agree with Definition A.1 as complex manifolds are:

  • (real) $\mathbb{CP}^2=S^5/\mathrm{U}(1)$, where $S^5=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^3:|z|^2=1\}$ and $\mathrm{U}(1)$ acts by $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$. This is the unit-sphere model; it manifests the Hopf fibration of §A.5.
  • (coset, Hermitian symmetric) $\mathbb{CP}^2=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1))$ (Helgason Type AIII; [He78, Ch.~X, Table V]; cf.~§D.6 below).
  • (algebraic) $\mathbb{CP}^2=\mathrm{Proj}\,\mathbb{C}[z^0,z^1,z^2]$, the projective scheme associated to the graded polynomial ring in three variables [Ha77, Ch.~I, Ex.~2.13 + Ch.~II, §2].

A.2 — The Three Standard Affine Charts

The natural atlas on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ consists of three holomorphic charts $\{U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha\}_{\alpha=0,1,2}$ defined by

Charts
$$U_\alpha \;=\; \bigl\{[z^0:z^1:z^2]\in\mathbb{CP}^2 \;:\; z^\alpha\neq 0\bigr\},\qquad \alpha=0,1,2,$$

with chart maps $\varphi_\alpha:U_\alpha\to\mathbb{C}^2$ defined by dividing through by the non-vanishing coordinate:

$$\varphi_0([z^0:z^1:z^2]) = \left(\tfrac{z^1}{z^0},\tfrac{z^2}{z^0}\right),\quad \varphi_1([z^0:z^1:z^2]) = \left(\tfrac{z^0}{z^1},\tfrac{z^2}{z^1}\right),\quad \varphi_2([z^0:z^1:z^2]) = \left(\tfrac{z^0}{z^2},\tfrac{z^1}{z^2}\right).$$

We write $(w^1,w^2)\in\mathbb{C}^2$ for the affine coordinates in chart $U_0$, so that $w^a=z^a/z^0$ for $a=1,2$; the inverse map sends $(w^1,w^2)\in\mathbb{C}^2$ to the equivalence class $[1:w^1:w^2]\in U_0$. The three charts cover $\mathbb{CP}^2$ since at least one homogeneous coordinate is nonzero at every point: $U_0\cup U_1\cup U_2=\mathbb{CP}^2$. Throughout this notebook we work primarily in $U_0$; all computations have direct analogues in $U_1$ and $U_2$ by relabelling.

A.3 — Transition Functions and Holomorphic Structure

Holomorphic Atlas

On the overlap $U_0\cap U_1=\{[z^0:z^1:z^2]:z^0z^1\neq 0\}$, the transition function $\varphi_1\circ\varphi_0^{-1}:\mathbb{C}^2\setminus\{w^1=0\}\to\mathbb{C}^2\setminus\{u^1=0\}$ is

$$\bigl(w^1,w^2\bigr)\;\longmapsto\;\bigl(u^1,u^2\bigr)\;=\;\left(\frac{1}{w^1},\,\frac{w^2}{w^1}\right),$$

which is biholomorphic on its domain (Jacobian determinant $\det J = -1/(w^1)^3\neq 0$). The analogous transition functions on $U_1\cap U_2$ and $U_0\cap U_2$ are biholomorphic on their domains by the same argument. Hence $\{U_\alpha,\varphi_\alpha\}$ is a holomorphic atlas, equipping $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with a unique compatible complex-manifold structure [GH78, Ch.~0, §3].

A.4 — Topology of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

Theorem A.1 — Basic Topological Invariants of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The topological space $\mathbb{CP}^2$ has the following invariants:

  1. Compact and Hausdorff (continuous image of the compact $S^5$ under $\mathrm{U}(1)$-quotient).
  2. Connected and simply connected: $\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2)=0$. Indeed, the Hopf bundle $\mathrm{U}(1)\hookrightarrow S^5\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ gives the long exact homotopy sequence with $\pi_1(S^5)=0=\pi_2(S^5)$ and $\pi_0(\mathrm{U}(1))=0$, hence $\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2)=0$.
  3. Cell complex: $\mathbb{CP}^2\simeq\mathrm{pt}\cup e^2\cup e^4$ as a CW complex with one cell in each even dimension $0,2,4$.
  4. Cohomology ring: $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}[H]/H^3$, where $H\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ is the hyperplane class (Poincaré dual to a projective line $\mathbb{CP}^1\hookrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^2$).
  5. Euler characteristic $\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\sum_k(-1)^k b_k=1+0+1+0+1=3$.
  6. Hirzebruch signature $\sigma(\mathbb{CP}^2)=+1$ (the intersection form on $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ is $(+1)$, a rank-1 positive form).
  7. Total Pontryagin class $p(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=1+3H^2$; total Chern class $c(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=(1+H)^3=1+3H+3H^2$ (Euler sequence; cf.~§C.8).
  8. Stiefel–Whitney classes: $w_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=0$ (orientability), $w_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=3H\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=H\neq 0$ — so $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not spin. (See §J.1.)

Standard references: [Hat02, Ex.~1.43 + Cor.~3.40 + Ex.~4.45]; [MS74, Ch.~14–15]; [BT82, §9, §20]; [GH78, Ch.~0–1].

A.5 — The Hopf Bundle and the Tautological Line Bundle

The complex projective plane carries a canonical $\mathrm{U}(1)$-principal bundle — the Hopf bundle — obtained by restricting the projection $\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ to the unit sphere $S^5\subset\mathbb{C}^3$:

Hopf Bundle
$$\boxed{\;\;\mathrm{U}(1)\;\hookrightarrow\;S^5\;\xrightarrow{\;\pi\;}\;\mathbb{CP}^2\;\;}$$

The structure group $\mathrm{U}(1)$ acts on $z\in S^5$ by $z\mapsto e^{i\theta}z$. This is the (higher-dimensional analogue of the) original Hopf fibration $\mathrm{U}(1)\hookrightarrow S^3\to\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2$ [Ho31; St51, §20.4].

This Hopf bundle is the unit-circle bundle of the tautological line bundle $\mathcal{O}(-1)\to\mathbb{CP}^2$, whose fibre over a point $[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the complex line in $\mathbb{C}^3$ through $z$ (and through the origin). The dual bundle $\mathcal{O}(1)\to\mathbb{CP}^2$, the hyperplane bundle, has first Chern class $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))=H$, the generator of $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$. Powers $\mathcal{O}(k)\equiv\mathcal{O}(1)^{\otimes k}$ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$ exhaust the isomorphism classes of holomorphic line bundles on $\mathbb{CP}^2$: $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, generated by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ [Ha77, Ch.~II, §6, Ex.~6.5; GH78, Ch.~1, §1.1].

Physical Rôle in KKK — CORR-003 applied

The Hopf bundle $\mathrm{U}(1)\hookrightarrow S^5\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the canonical $\mathrm{U}(1)$-principal bundle over $\mathbb{CP}^2$; it is the bookkeeping device for line bundles $\mathcal{O}(k)$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and for the canonical Spin$^c$ structure of §J. This is a different geometric object from the KKK spacetime fibration. The latter, $Y^{12}\hookrightarrow E^{16}\to M^{1,3}$, has $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ as fibre, not as base; its structure group is the much larger non-abelian $\mathrm{SU}(3)^3$ (or its central quotient $\mathrm{PSU}(3)^3$) acting on the fibre by isometries. The two bundles are linked only through line-bundle book-keeping: powers $\mathcal{O}(k)$ of the Hopf bundle on each $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factor become candidate twists for the internal Spin$^c$–Dirac index of Pillar III, and the canonical line bundle $K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=\mathcal{O}(-3)$ is the structure-group line bundle determining the canonical Spin$^c$ structure on the fibre — not on the total space $E^{16}$.

Scale independence and prequantisation matching (R4-003 applied; cross-checked against B.8). Each holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{O}(k)$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a topological/holomorphic object: it labels an element of $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{CP}^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}$, independent of the continuous metric scale $b$. The Fubini–Study Kähler form, however, depends on $b$ through its de~Rham class $$\bigl[\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)\bigr]\;=\;\tfrac{b^2}{2}\,H\;\in\;H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{R}),$$ which is a real class for generic $b$. The matching condition is:

$$\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)\;\text{represents}\;c_1(\mathcal{O}(k))\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\frac{b^2}{2}\;=\;k\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}.$$

That is, $\omega_{\rm FS}$ is the curvature of $\mathcal{O}(k)$ only at the discrete values $b^2=2k$; for generic continuous $b$, the Kähler class is real (not integral) and there is no associated holomorphic line bundle of which $\omega_{\rm FS}$ is the Chern-Weil curvature. The canonical Spin$^c$ structure $S^c=\Lambda^{0,*}T^{*(0,1)}\mathbb{CP}^2$ and its auxiliary line $L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}=K^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(3)$ are determined by the holomorphic structure and are scale-independent in $b$. Without imposing prequantisation (cf.~B.8), $b$ is a continuous metric modulus and $\omega_{\rm FS}$ need not represent any integral line-bundle class.

§ BKähler Structure and the Fubini–Study Metric

We now construct on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ the unique (up to scale and to $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{CP}^2)$) Kähler–Einstein metric of positive scalar curvature: the Fubini–Study metric $g_{\rm FS}$ [Fu03, Stu05]. The construction proceeds via the Kähler potential.

B.1 — Homogeneous Kähler Potential

Definition B.1 — The Homogeneous Fubini–Study Potential

On $\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}$ define the smooth real function

$$\boxed{\;\;\Phi(z,\bar z)\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\log\bigl(\rho^2\bigr),\qquad\rho^2(z,\bar z)\equiv|z^0|^2+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2.\;\;}$$

The real parameter $b>0$ is the Kähler-class radius (the “size” modulus of $\mathbb{CP}^2$) inherited from the convention of KKK-LL-1, §F.2. We henceforth refer to $b$ as the breathing modulus of $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

Descent of $\partial\bar\partial\Phi$ to $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The function $\Phi$ itself does not descend to $\mathbb{CP}^2$ because under the $\mathbb{C}^*$ scaling $z\mapsto\lambda z$ one has

$$\Phi(\lambda z,\bar\lambda\bar z)\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\log\bigl(|\lambda|^2\rho^2\bigr)\;=\;\Phi(z,\bar z)\;+\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\log|\lambda|^2.$$

The added term $(b^2/2)\log|\lambda|^2=(b^2/2)(\log\lambda+\log\bar\lambda)$ is the real part of a holomorphic function on the orbit, hence is annihilated by the operator $\partial\bar\partial$. Therefore the closed (1,1)-form $$\omega_{\rm FS}\;\equiv\;i\,\partial\bar\partial\,\Phi$$ descends to a well-defined closed real (1,1)-form on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ — the Fubini–Study Kähler form.

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathcal{K}(w,\bar w)\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\log\bigl(1+|w|^2\bigr),\qquad |w|^2\equiv|w^1|^2+|w^2|^2.\;\;}$$

Differing local representatives of the same global $(1,1)$-form $\omega_{\rm FS}$ on chart overlaps differ by a Kähler transformation $\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}+f(w)+\overline{f(w)}$ with $f$ holomorphic; this is the standard global Kähler-potential ambiguity [KN69, Ch.~IX, Prop.~5.5].

B.3 — Hermitian Metric Components

The Hermitian metric components in the Kähler-potential representation are $g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}\mathcal{K}$. We compute:

Step 1 — First derivative
$$\partial_a\mathcal{K}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\bar w^a}{1+|w|^2}.$$
Step 2 — Mixed second derivative
$$\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}\mathcal{K}\;=\;\partial_{\bar b}\!\left[\frac{b^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\bar w^a}{1+|w|^2}\right]\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\left[\frac{\delta^a_{\;b}}{1+|w|^2}\;-\;\frac{\bar w^a\,w^b}{(1+|w|^2)^2}\right].$$
Step 3 — Compact form
$$\boxed{\;\;g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}(w,\bar w)\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\frac{(1+|w|^2)\,\delta^a_{\;b}\;-\;\bar w^a w^b}{(1+|w|^2)^2}.\;\;}$$
Index Notation Reminder (consistent with KKK-LL-1, §C)

Here $a,b\in\{1,2\}$ are holomorphic indices on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (complex dimension 2); the anti-holomorphic conjugate indices are $\bar a,\bar b\in\{\bar 1,\bar 2\}$. We write $\delta^a_{\;b}=\delta_{ab}$ for the Kronecker delta on the complex index set. A real-coordinate index $m\in\{1,2,3,4\}$ on the underlying real 4-manifold of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ runs in the relations $z^a=x^a+iy^a$, $\bar z^{\bar a}=x^a-iy^a$; the relation between the complex and real metric components is recalled in B.4 below.

B.4 — The Real Line Element and the $2\times 2$ Matrix Form

The full real line element on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ associated with the Hermitian metric of B.3 is obtained from the standard relation, with the antisymmetrisation convention adopted in KKK-LL-1, Convention C.2,

Line Element on $\mathbb{CP}^2$
$$ds^2\;=\;2\,g_{a\bar b}\,dz^a\,d\bar z^{\bar b}\;=\;b^2\,\frac{(1+|w|^2)\,\delta_{a\bar b}\,dw^a\,d\bar w^{\bar b}\;-\;(\bar w\cdot dw)(w\cdot d\bar w)}{(1+|w|^2)^2}.$$

The factor of $2$ in $ds^2=2g_{a\bar b}dz^a d\bar z^{\bar b}$ converts the Hermitian metric to the real Riemannian metric on the underlying $4$-real-manifold (KKK-LL-1, Convention C.2). Explicitly, if we set $\rho^2=|w|^2$ and write the two complex coordinates as $w^1=w$ and $w^2=z$, the $2\times 2$ matrix of complex components is

Matrix Form — $2\times 2$
$$g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2(1+\rho^2)^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1+|z|^2 & -\bar w\,z \\[3pt] -w\,\bar z & 1+|w|^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho^2=|w|^2+|z|^2.$$

The determinant works out to

$$\det\bigl(g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{(b^2/2)^2}{(1+\rho^2)^4}\bigl[(1+|w|^2)(1+|z|^2)-|w|^2|z|^2\bigr]\;=\;\frac{b^4/4}{(1+\rho^2)^3}.$$

For the real-coordinate volume element of the underlying 4-manifold (which equals the Kähler-form top power up to a factor) we use the standard relation $\sqrt{g_{\rm real}}=2^n\det g_{a\bar b}$ with $n=2$ (cf.~[GH78, Ch.~0, §7]):

Volume Density
$$\sqrt{g_{\rm real}}\;=\;4\,\det\bigl(g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}\bigr)\;=\;\frac{b^4}{(1+\rho^2)^3}.$$

B.5 — The Kähler Form and Closedness

The Kähler form is the imaginary part of the Hermitian metric:

Kähler Form
$$\omega_{\rm FS}\;=\;i\,g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}\,dw^a\wedge d\bar w^{\bar b}\;=\;i\,\partial\bar\partial\,\mathcal{K}\;=\;\frac{i b^2}{2}\,\partial\bar\partial\log\bigl(1+|w|^2\bigr).$$
Closedness

By construction $d\omega_{\rm FS}=(\partial+\bar\partial)(i\partial\bar\partial\mathcal{K})=i\partial\bar\partial\partial\mathcal{K}+i\bar\partial\partial\bar\partial\mathcal{K}=0$, since $\partial^2=\bar\partial^2=0$ and $\partial\bar\partial=-\bar\partial\partial$. Hence $(g^{\rm FS},\omega_{\rm FS})$ is a Kähler structure on $\mathbb{CP}^2$. The fact that the Kähler form is the imaginary part of a closed Hermitian metric is the defining property of the Kähler class [KN69, Ch.~IX, Th.~5.4].

B.6 — Volume Form and Total Volume of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The Riemannian volume form on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is $d\mathrm{vol}_g=\sqrt{g_{\rm real}}\,d^4y$, which by §B.4 equals

Volume Form
$$d\mathrm{vol}_g\;=\;\frac{b^4}{(1+\rho^2)^3}\,d^4y\;=\;\frac{\omega_{\rm FS}\wedge\omega_{\rm FS}}{2!}\quad\text{(K\"ahler top form)}.$$
Theorem B.1 — Volume of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The total volume of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with respect to the Fubini–Study metric $g^{\rm FS}=b^2\,g^{\rm FS}_{(b=1)}$ is

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathrm{Vol}\bigl(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS}\bigr)\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\frac{\omega_{\rm FS}\wedge\omega_{\rm FS}}{2!}\;=\;\frac{\pi^2 b^4}{2}.\;\;}$$
Proof of Theorem B.1 — CORR-004 applied

Integrate the volume density over $\mathbb{C}^2\simeq\mathbb{R}^4$ in polar coordinates. The Euclidean volume form is $d^4y=r^3\,dr\,d\Omega_3$, where $d\Omega_3$ is the round volume form on the unit $3$-sphere and $\int_{S^3}d\Omega_3=2\pi^2$. Then

$$\mathrm{Vol}\;=\;\int_{\mathbb{C}^2}\frac{b^4}{(1+r^2)^3}\,r^3\,dr\,d\Omega_3\;=\;b^4\cdot 2\pi^2\cdot\int_0^\infty\frac{r^3\,dr}{(1+r^2)^3}.$$

Substituting $u=1+r^2$, $du=2r\,dr$, $r^2=u-1$, the radial integral becomes $$\int_0^\infty\frac{r^3\,dr}{(1+r^2)^3}\;=\;\frac12\int_1^\infty\frac{(u-1)\,du}{u^3}\;=\;\frac12\bigl[-u^{-1}+\tfrac12 u^{-2}\bigr]_1^\infty\;=\;\frac12\cdot\frac12\;=\;\frac14.$$ Equivalently, $\int_0^\infty r^3/(1+r^2)^3 dr=\tfrac12 B(2,1)=\tfrac12\cdot\Gamma(2)\Gamma(1)/\Gamma(3)=1/4$. Multiplying gives $$\mathrm{Vol}\;=\;b^4\cdot 2\pi^2\cdot\tfrac14\;=\;\frac{\pi^2 b^4}{2}.\qquad\square$$

B.7 — The $\mathbb{CP}^1$ Coefficient Check

To verify the coefficient $b^2/2$ in $\mathcal{K}=(b^2/2)\log(1+|w|^2)$, we use the canonical $\mathbb{CP}^1=\mathbb{CP}^2|_{w^2=0}$ inside $\mathbb{CP}^2$. With $w^1\equiv z\in\mathbb{C}$, the restriction of the Fubini–Study metric is (in the convention $ds^2=2g_{a\bar b}dz^ad\bar z^{\bar b}$, $\omega=ig_{a\bar b}dz^a\wedge d\bar z^{\bar b}$ frozen in §B.5):

$$g_{z\bar z}|_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\;=\;\frac{b^2/2}{(1+|z|^2)^2}\qquad\text{(Hermitian component)},$$
$$ds^2|_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\;=\;2g_{z\bar z}\,dz\,d\bar z\;=\;\frac{b^2}{(1+|z|^2)^2}\,dz\,d\bar z\qquad\text{(real line element)},$$
$$\omega|_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\;=\;i\,g_{z\bar z}\,dz\!\wedge\!d\bar z\;=\;\frac{i\,b^2/2}{(1+|z|^2)^2}\,dz\!\wedge\!d\bar z\qquad\text{(K\"ahler form)}.$$

The three displays are equivalent; the audit point (CORR-005) is that they must be cited with consistent labels to avoid the factor-of-$2$ confusion between Hermitian components and the real Riemannian line element.

B.8 — Cohomology Class of $\omega_{\rm FS}$ and Integrality

The Kähler form $\omega_{\rm FS}$ defines a de Rham cohomology class $[\omega_{\rm FS}]\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{R})$. From B.7 and the fact that the homology class $[\mathbb{CP}^1]$ generates $H_2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$, we conclude

Kähler Class
$$\bigl[\omega_{\rm FS}\bigr]\;=\;\pi b^2\cdot H\;\in\;H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{R}),$$

where $H\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ is the hyperplane class (Poincaré dual to $[\mathbb{CP}^1]$).

Prequantisation / integrality — R2-006 applied (refining v1.1). The standard Chern–Weil normalisation works with $[\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)]\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{R})$; by B.7, $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)=b^2/2$. If one imposes prequantisation of $\omega_{\rm FS}$ (Souriau–Kostant; equivalent to demanding that $\omega_{\rm FS}$ be the curvature of a prequantum line bundle), then

$$\bigl[\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)\bigr]\;\in\;H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\frac{b^2}{2}\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.$$

The minimal nontrivial geometric quantum value is then $b^2=2$, for which $\omega_{\rm FS}/(2\pi)=H$ and the prequantum line bundle is $\mathcal{O}(1)$. Prequantisation is not required by the classical Riemannian geometry: at the level of this notebook, the FS metric, its curvature data, the volume, the Spin$^c$ structure, the Killing-vector and moment-map computations are all well-defined for arbitrary continuous $b>0$. Any dynamical quantisation of $b$ — e.g.~from the Lovelock–flux potential of KKK-LL-7 — is a further model-dependent assumption, not a consequence of this notebook.

§ CCurvature Tensors on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

We now compute the full curvature data of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$: Christoffel symbols, Riemann tensor, Ricci tensor, scalar curvature, and the Weyl/Bochner decomposition. Two principles drastically simplify the computation:

  • The metric is Kähler, so the only nonvanishing Christoffel symbols are $\Gamma^a_{\;bc}$ and $\Gamma^{\bar a}_{\;\bar b\bar c}$; mixed types vanish.
  • The metric is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-invariant and transitive, so it suffices to compute curvature components at a single point (e.g.~the origin $w=0$ of chart $U_0$) and use the homogeneity.

C.1 — Christoffel Symbols on a Kähler Manifold

Levi-Civita Connection on a Kähler Manifold

For a Kähler metric $g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}\mathcal{K}$, the Levi-Civita connection has only “pure-holomorphic” and “pure-antiholomorphic” nonvanishing components:

$$\Gamma^c_{\;ab}\;=\;g^{c\bar d}\,\partial_a g_{b\bar d},\qquad \Gamma^{\bar c}_{\;\bar a\bar b}\;=\;g^{\bar c d}\,\partial_{\bar a} g_{\bar b d}\;=\;\overline{\Gamma^c_{\;ab}},$$

with all mixed Christoffel symbols (e.g.~$\Gamma^a_{\;b\bar c}$, $\Gamma^a_{\;\bar b c}$) vanishing identically [KN69, Ch.~IX, Prop.~7.1]. This is the Kähler condition expressed at the level of the connection.

C.2 — The Riemann Tensor on a Kähler Manifold

Riemann Tensor Components

With the curvature convention of KKK-LL-1 §C.4 (i.e.~$R^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_c\Gamma^a_{\;db}-\partial_d\Gamma^a_{\;cb}+\Gamma^a_{\;ce}\Gamma^e_{\;db}-\Gamma^a_{\;de}\Gamma^e_{\;cb}$ and $R_{abcd}=g_{ae}R^e{}_{bcd}$), the only independent nonvanishing component class on a Kähler manifold is

$$R_{a\bar b c\bar d}\;=\;-\,\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}\,g_{a\bar b}\;+\;g^{e\bar f}\,(\partial_c g_{a\bar f})(\partial_{\bar d}g_{e\bar b}).$$

All components related to this by index permutations on the Kähler symmetries $$R_{a\bar b c\bar d}\;=\;R_{c\bar b a\bar d}\;=\;R_{a\bar d c\bar b}\;=\;\overline{R_{b\bar a d\bar c}}$$ follow by symmetry. Mixed-type components $R_{abcd}$, $R_{ab\bar c\bar d}$, $R_{abc\bar d}$, etc.~all vanish identically on a Kähler manifold [KN69, Ch.~IX, §7]. The sign of the leading term differs in various references; the convention $R_{a\bar b c\bar d}=-\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}g_{a\bar b}+\cdots$ at the origin is the one consistent with $R_{abcd}=g_{ae}R^e{}_{bcd}$ and with $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\partial_\rho\Gamma_{\sigma\mu\nu}-\partial_\sigma\Gamma_{\rho\mu\nu}+\cdots$ in real notation.

C.3 — The Riemann Tensor at the Origin of $U_0$

Computation at $w=0$ — CORR-007 applied

At the origin $w=0$ of the affine chart $U_0$, the Kähler potential $\mathcal{K}=(b^2/2)\log(1+|w|^2)$ has the Taylor expansion

$$\mathcal{K}(w,\bar w)\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\Bigl[\,|w|^2\;-\;\frac{1}{2}|w|^4\;+\;\frac{1}{3}|w|^6\;-\;\cdots\Bigr].$$

Differentiating twice in $w$ and $\bar w$ produces the Hermitian metric components $g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}\mathcal{K}$:

$$g_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\Bigl[\,\delta_{a\bar b}\;-\;\delta_{a\bar b}\,|w|^2\;-\;\bar w^{\bar b}\,w^a\;+\;\mathcal{O}(|w|^4)\,\Bigr],$$

whose first derivatives vanish at the origin, while the relevant mixed second derivative is

$$\boxed{\;\;\partial_c\,\partial_{\bar d}\,g_{a\bar b}\big|_0\;=\;-\,\frac{b^2}{2}\,\bigl(\delta_{a\bar b}\,\delta_{c\bar d}\;+\;\delta_{a\bar d}\,\delta_{c\bar b}\bigr).\;\;}$$

(No additional dimensional factor; this is the clean form requested by the audit.) Hence, using the Kähler Riemann formula of C.2 with $\partial g|_0=0$,

$$R_{a\bar b c\bar d}|_0\;=\;-\,\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}g_{a\bar b}|_0\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\bigl(\delta_{a\bar b}\delta_{c\bar d}\;+\;\delta_{a\bar d}\delta_{c\bar b}\bigr).$$

Re-expressing the deltas as metric components $g_{a\bar b}|_0=(b^2/2)\delta_{a\bar b}$, equivalently $\delta_{a\bar b}=(2/b^2)g_{a\bar b}|_0$, gives

$$R_{a\bar b c\bar d}|_0\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\cdot\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{b^2}\Bigr)^2\bigl(g_{a\bar b}g_{c\bar d}+g_{a\bar d}g_{c\bar b}\bigr)|_0\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\bigl(g_{a\bar b}g_{c\bar d}+g_{a\bar d}g_{c\bar b}\bigr)|_0.$$
Step 1 (explicit) — Derive $\partial_c\,\partial_{\bar d}g_{a\bar b}|_0$

From $g_{a\bar b}=(b^2/2)[(1+|w|^2)\delta_{a\bar b}-\bar w^{\bar b} w^a]/(1+|w|^2)^2$, expanding to lowest non-trivial order in $|w|^2$ gives $$g_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\Bigl[\,\delta_{a\bar b}\,-\,\delta_{a\bar b}|w|^2\,-\,\bar w^{\bar b} w^a\,+\,\mathcal{O}(|w|^4)\,\Bigr],$$ so that $$\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}g_{a\bar b}|_0\;=\;-\frac{b^2}{2}\bigl[\,\delta_{a\bar b}\,\delta_{c\bar d}\;+\;\delta_{a\bar d}\,\delta_{c\bar b}\,\bigr],$$ where the $-\delta_{a\bar b}|w|^2$ term contributes $-\delta_{a\bar b}\delta_{c\bar d}$ via $\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}|w|^2=\delta_{c\bar d}$, and the $-\bar w^{\bar b}w^a$ term contributes $-\delta_{a\bar d}\delta_{c\bar b}$ via $\partial_c w^a=\delta_c^a$, $\partial_{\bar d}\bar w^{\bar b}=\delta_{\bar d}^{\bar b}$.

Step 2 — Insert into the Riemann formula at the origin

At $w=0$ the quadratic Christoffel term in $R_{a\bar bc\bar d}$ vanishes because $\partial g|_0=0$; the only contribution is the linear $-\partial_c\partial_{\bar d}g_{a\bar b}|_0$ term, giving $$R_{a\bar b c\bar d}|_0\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\bigl[\,\delta_{a\bar b}\delta_{c\bar d}\;+\;\delta_{a\bar d}\delta_{c\bar b}\,\bigr]\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\bigl[\,g_{a\bar b}g_{c\bar d}\;+\;g_{a\bar d}g_{c\bar b}\,\bigr]\big|_0\;.$$

C.4 — Riemann Tensor at a Generic Point: Constant Holomorphic Sectional Curvature

By the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-homogeneity of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$, the value at the origin propagates by isometry to every point. Hence the Riemann tensor takes the form

Key Curvature Identity (Constant Holomorphic Sectional Curvature)
$$\boxed{\;\;R_{a\bar b c\bar d}\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\bigl[\,g_{a\bar b}\,g_{c\bar d}\;+\;g_{a\bar d}\,g_{c\bar b}\,\bigr]\quad\text{everywhere on}\;\;\mathbb{CP}^2.\;\;}$$

This is the curvature tensor of a Kähler manifold of constant holomorphic sectional curvature $H=4/b^2$, the standard form on $\mathbb{CP}^n$ [KN69, Ch.~IX, Prop.~7.5 and Th.~7.9; Be87, Ch.~8, §8.95].

The constant $H=4/b^2$ is normalised by the universal definition $H(v)\equiv R(v,\bar v,v,\bar v)/|v|^4$ for a non-null holomorphic tangent vector $v$. Evaluated on $v=\partial_{w^1}|_0$ (CORR-008 applied):

$$R_{1\bar 1 1\bar 1}|_0\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\bigl(g_{1\bar 1}g_{1\bar 1}+g_{1\bar 1}g_{1\bar 1}\bigr)\big|_0\;=\;\frac{4}{b^2}\,(g_{1\bar 1}|_0)^2\;=\;\frac{4}{b^2}\cdot\Bigl(\frac{b^2}{2}\Bigr)^2\;=\;b^2,$$
$$|v|^4\;=\;(g_{1\bar 1}|_0)^2\;=\;\frac{b^4}{4},\qquad H\;=\;\frac{R_{1\bar 1 1\bar 1}|_0}{|v|^4}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{b^4/4}\;=\;\frac{4}{b^2}.$$

C.5 — Ricci Tensor: Kähler–Einstein with $\lambda=6/b^2$

Theorem C.1 — $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is Kähler–Einstein

Contracting the Riemann tensor of C.4 with $g^{c\bar d}$:

$$R_{a\bar b}\;=\;g^{c\bar d}\,R_{a\bar b c\bar d}\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\Bigl[\,g_{a\bar b}\,g^{c\bar d}g_{c\bar d}\;+\;g^{c\bar d}\,g_{a\bar d}\,g_{c\bar b}\,\Bigr]\;=\;\frac{2}{b^2}\bigl[\,n\,g_{a\bar b}\;+\;g_{a\bar b}\,\bigr]\;=\;\frac{2(n+1)}{b^2}\,g_{a\bar b},$$

where $n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{CP}^2=2$ and we used $g^{c\bar d}g_{c\bar d}=\delta^c_c=n$ and $g^{c\bar d}g_{a\bar d}g_{c\bar b}=g_{a\bar b}$. Specialising to $\mathbb{CP}^2$:

$$\boxed{\;\;R_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{6}{b^2}\,g_{a\bar b}\;\;}$$

i.e.~$(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ is a Kähler–Einstein manifold with Einstein constant $\lambda=6/b^2$. For general $n$, the analogous result is $R_{a\bar b}=(2(n+1)/b^2)g_{a\bar b}$.

Reference Remark — Yau theorem is not needed here

For $\mathbb{CP}^n$, the Fubini–Study metric is Kähler-Einstein by direct construction: the Ricci form $\rho=i R_{a\bar b}dw^a\wedge d\bar w^{\bar b}=(6/b^2)\omega_{\rm FS}$ (cf.~C.6.iii below) is manifestly proportional to the Kähler form. No existence theorem is needed; one does not invoke Yau here. Yau's theorem [Yau77], together with the Aubin–Tian work [Ti87] and modern K-stability, applies to the harder question of Kähler-Einstein existence on general Fano manifolds. For $\mathbb{CP}^n$, that question is trivial because the metric is explicit.

C.6 — Scalar Curvature

Scalar Curvature in Complex and Real Conventions

(i) Complex scalar trace. Tracing the Ricci tensor with the inverse Hermitian metric:

$$R_{\mathbb{C}}\;\equiv\;g^{a\bar b}R_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{6}{b^2}\cdot g^{a\bar b}g_{a\bar b}\;=\;\frac{6}{b^2}\cdot n\;=\;\frac{12}{b^2}.$$

(ii) Real scalar curvature. In the underlying real Riemannian geometry, with line element $ds^2=2g_{a\bar b}\,dw^a d\bar w^{\bar b}$, the real scalar curvature is $R_{\rm real}=2R_{\mathbb{C}}$ (see e.g.~[Be87, §2.85; KN69, IX.5]; the factor of $2$ has its origin in the $g_{a\bar b}\leftrightarrow G_{mn}$ relation $G_{mn}=2\,\mathrm{Re}(g_{a\bar b})$ in real coordinates):

$$\boxed{\;\;\hat R\;\equiv\;R_{\rm real}\;=\;2R_{\mathbb{C}}\;=\;\frac{24}{b^2}\quad\text{on}\;\;\mathbb{CP}^2.\;\;}$$

(iii) Ricci form. The closed real $(1,1)$-form $\rho=iR_{a\bar b}dw^a\wedge d\bar w^{\bar b}$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ equals

$$\rho\;=\;\frac{6}{b^2}\,\omega_{\rm FS},\qquad \biggl[\frac{\rho}{2\pi}\biggr]\;=\;3H\;\in\;H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z}),$$

C.7 — The Bochner Tensor and Irreducibility

The Riemann tensor of a Kähler manifold decomposes orthogonally (under the action of the holonomy group $\mathrm{U}(n)$) into three pieces (R4-010 applied: cleaner notation):

$$R\;=\;R_{\rm scal}\;\oplus\;\mathrm{Ric}_0\;\oplus\;B,$$

where $R_{\rm scal}$ is the scalar-trace piece, $\mathrm{Ric}_0$ is the trace-free Ricci piece, and $B$ is the Bochner tensor, the Kähler analogue of the Riemannian Weyl tensor [Bo49, Ya65, Be87, §2.62].

Bochner-flat vs.~constant holomorphic sectional curvature — R4-002 applied

The implication $$\text{constant holomorphic sectional curvature}\;\Longrightarrow\;B=0$$ is correct and is the one we need for $\mathbb{CP}^n$. The converse, however, is false in general: Bochner-flat Kähler manifolds form a strictly larger class than complex space forms (see e.g.~[Br01] for explicit non-space-form Bochner-flat examples). For the present notebook the relevant direction is the forward implication: since $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ has constant holomorphic sectional curvature $H=4/b^2$ (cf.~C.4), the Bochner tensor vanishes. Combined with the Kähler-Einstein property of §C.5 (which kills the trace-free Ricci piece), this leaves only the scalar piece:

$$\boxed{\;\;R_{a\bar b c\bar d}|_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\;=\;R_{\rm scal}\;\;\text{(scalar piece only),}\qquad B|_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=0,\qquad \mathrm{Ric}_0|_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=0.\;\;}$$

This vanishing of the Bochner tensor on $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is the Kähler analogue of the vanishing of the Weyl tensor on round spheres $S^n$ and on real hyperbolic spaces $\mathbb{H}^n$. The Riemann tensor is then determined entirely by the holomorphic sectional curvature $H$, with the relations

$$R_{\rm real}\;=\;n(n+1)\,H,\qquad R_{\mathbb{C}}\;=\;\tfrac12 n(n+1)\,H,$$

verified for $n=2$, $H=4/b^2$: $R_{\rm real}=2\cdot 3\cdot 4/b^2=24/b^2$ and $R_{\mathbb C}=12/b^2$ as in C.6.

Irreducibility as a Riemannian Symmetric Space

$(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ is an irreducible Riemannian symmetric space of compact type [He78, Ch.~X, Table V, Type AIII with $(p,q)=(1,2)$]. Irreducibility (i.e.~non-decomposability as a Riemannian product) is the content of Cartan's classification; equivalently, the isotropy representation of $H=\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(2))$ on the tangent space at the origin is irreducible (cf.~§D.9). This irreducibility is crucial for the Ohashi-type ghost-free analysis of KKK-LL-6: Ohashi's no-go theorem is stated for irreducible compact symmetric spaces, and its extension to the reducible product $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is the technical open question of that notebook.

C.8 — First Chern Class and the Fano Property

The first Chern class of the holomorphic tangent bundle $T\mathbb{CP}^2\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ is computed from the Euler sequence

$$0\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{O}\;\longrightarrow\;\mathcal{O}(1)^{\oplus 3}\;\longrightarrow\;T\mathbb{CP}^2\;\longrightarrow\;0$$

[Ha77, Ch.~II, Th.~8.13(c); GH78, Ch.~3]. Taking total Chern classes and using $c(\mathcal{O}(1))=1+H$:

Total Chern Class of $T\mathbb{CP}^2$
$$c(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;(1+H)^3\;=\;1\;+\;3H\;+\;3H^2.$$

Hence the individual Chern classes are

$$c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;3H,\qquad c_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;3H^2.$$
Fano Property and $c_1$ Positivity

Since $c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=3H>0$ in the sense that $H$ is the Kähler class of a positive line bundle (the hyperplane bundle), $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a Fano variety [Ha77, Ch.~II, §8; GH78, Ch.~3; KN69, Ch.~IX]. The anticanonical line bundle is $-K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=\det T\mathbb{CP}^2=\mathcal{O}(3)$, with $c_1(\mathcal{O}(3))=3H$. The canonical bundle itself is $K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=\Omega^2_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=\mathcal{O}(-3)$. (R3-014 applied: Yau/Tian are not the right citations for the elementary Fano property of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, which is a direct consequence of the adjunction-formula/Euler-sequence computation of $K_{\mathbb{CP}^n}=\mathcal{O}(-n-1)$ found in any standard algebraic-geometry textbook.)

Two consequences central to the KKK programme:

  • For $\mathbb{CP}^2$, the Fubini–Study metric explicitly realises the positive Kähler–Einstein metric in $c_1>0$; no general existence theorem is needed here (R2-007 applied; see also §C.5 note). The general positive-Fano existence question is the Aubin–Yau–Tian programme [Yau77; Ti87] with modern K-stability refinements, but the answer for $\mathbb{CP}^n$ is supplied by the explicit FS construction.
  • The Spin$^c$ obstruction $w_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=c_1\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=3H\,\mathrm{mod}\,2=H\neq 0$ is the precise statement that $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not spin (cf.~§J.1); the canonical Spin$^c$ structure has auxiliary line bundle $L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}=K^{-1}_{\mathbb{CP}^2}=\mathcal{O}(3)$, with $c_1(L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}})=3H$.

§ DIsometry Group and the Coset Construction

This section establishes that the connected isometry group of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ is $\mathrm{Isom}_0(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\mathrm{PSU}(3)=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathbb{Z}_3$, with maximal isotropy $H=\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1))\cong\mathrm{U}(2)$, giving the coset/Hermitian-symmetric-space presentation $\mathbb{CP}^2\cong\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1))$ of Cartan type AIII. These structural facts underpin the gauge-algebra emergence of KKK: the connected isometry algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is the trinification factor associated with this $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (cf.~§H below for the precise index-mapping $c\to 1,\,L\to 2,\,R\to 3$).

D.1 — The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ Action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

Definition D.1 — Action of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The group $\mathrm{SU}(3)=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{C}):g^\dagger g=\mathbb{1},\det g=1\}$ acts on $\mathbb{C}^3$ by matrix multiplication, $z\mapsto gz$. This restricts to an action on $\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}$ (since $g$ is invertible) which commutes with the $\mathbb{C}^*$ scaling defining $\mathbb{CP}^2$, hence descends to a smooth action on the quotient:

$$\mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathbb{CP}^2\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad (g,[z])\;\longmapsto\;[gz].$$

This action is by holomorphic diffeomorphisms (since $z\mapsto gz$ is holomorphic).

Theorem D.1 — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ Preserves the Fubini–Study Metric

The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ defined above preserves the Fubini–Study Kähler structure: both $g_{\rm FS}$ and $\omega_{\rm FS}$ are invariant.

Proof
Step 1 — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ preserves the norm $\rho^2$

For $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $z\in\mathbb{C}^3$, $|gz|^2=(gz)^\dagger(gz)=z^\dagger g^\dagger g z=z^\dagger z=|z|^2=\rho^2$.

Step 2 — $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ preserves $\Phi$ and hence $\omega_{\rm FS}$

The homogeneous Kähler potential $\Phi=(b^2/2)\log\rho^2$ is a function of $\rho^2$ alone, hence is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-invariant. Since $\omega_{\rm FS}=i\partial\bar\partial\Phi$ is constructed from $\Phi$ by a coordinate-invariant differential operation (the $\partial\bar\partial$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$ descends to $\partial\bar\partial$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$, and is preserved by holomorphic transformations), $\omega_{\rm FS}$ is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-invariant.

Step 3 — The metric is determined by $\omega_{\rm FS}$ and $J$

On a Kähler manifold, the metric is determined by the Kähler form $\omega$ and the complex structure $J$ via $g(X,Y)=\omega(X,JY)$. Since $J$ is invariant under any holomorphic transformation (in particular under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$), and $\omega=\omega_{\rm FS}$ is invariant by Step 2, the metric $g_{\rm FS}$ is invariant. $\square$

D.2 — Kernel of the Action: $Z(\mathrm{SU}(3))=\mathbb{Z}_3$

The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not faithful: the centre acts trivially on equivalence classes.

Theorem D.2 — Kernel of the Action

The kernel of the homomorphism $\mathrm{SU}(3)\to\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{CP}^2)$ is the centre

$$Z(\mathrm{SU}(3))\;=\;\bigl\{\omega^k\,\mathbb{1}_3\,:\,k=0,1,2\bigr\}\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}_3,\quad \omega\equiv e^{2\pi i/3}.$$
Proof

An element $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ acts trivially on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ iff for every $[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^2$ one has $[gz]=[z]$, i.e.~$gz=\lambda(z)\,z$ for some $\lambda(z)\in\mathbb{C}^*$. Choosing successively the three coordinate basis vectors $e_0,e_1,e_2$ shows that $g$ is diagonal: $g e_k=\lambda_k e_k$ with $\lambda_k\in\mathbb{C}^*$. Then choosing $z=e_0+e_1$ gives $g(e_0+e_1)=\lambda_0 e_0+\lambda_1 e_1$, which is proportional to $e_0+e_1$ iff $\lambda_0=\lambda_1$. Similarly $\lambda_0=\lambda_1=\lambda_2\equiv\lambda$, so $g=\lambda\mathbb{1}_3$. Imposing $\det g=\lambda^3=1$ gives $\lambda=\omega^k$ for some $k\in\{0,1,2\}$. $\square$

D.3 — $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$ Acts Effectively

Definition & Lemma D.3 — Effective Action of $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$

Setting $\mathrm{PSU}(3)\equiv\mathrm{SU}(3)/Z(\mathrm{SU}(3))=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathbb{Z}_3$, the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors through $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$ and the quotient action is effective (faithful):

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathrm{SU}(3)\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\mathrm{PSU}(3)\;\hookrightarrow\;\mathrm{Diff}(\mathbb{CP}^2),\qquad\dim\mathrm{PSU}(3)=\dim\mathrm{SU}(3)=8.\;\;}$$

The Lie algebras coincide: $\mathfrak{psu}(3)=\mathfrak{su}(3)$.

D.4 — Transitivity

Theorem D.4 — The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ Action is Transitive

For any two points $[z],[z']\in\mathbb{CP}^2$ there exists $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ with $g\cdot[z]=[z']$.

Proof

It suffices to show that any point can be brought to the “origin” $p_0\equiv[1:0:0]\in U_0$ by some $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ (transitivity from $p_0$ to any other point follows by composition with the inverse map). Given $[z]\in\mathbb{CP}^2$, choose a representative $z\in S^5$ (rescale so that $|z|=1$). Extend $\{z\}$ to an orthonormal basis $\{z,v_1,v_2\}$ of $\mathbb{C}^3$ (Gram–Schmidt), and form the matrix $g_0=[z\,|\,v_1\,|\,v_2]\in\mathrm{U}(3)$ whose columns are the three vectors. Then $g_0^{-1}\in\mathrm{U}(3)$ sends $z$ to $e_0=(1,0,0)^\top$. If $\det g_0^{-1}=e^{i\phi}\neq 1$, multiply $g_0^{-1}$ by the diagonal phase matrix $\mathrm{diag}(e^{-i\phi},1,1)$ (which fixes $e_0$ projectively) to obtain $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ with $g\cdot[z]=[e_0]=p_0$. $\square$

D.5 — Stabiliser of the Origin

Theorem D.5 — Stabiliser of $p_0=[1:0:0]$

The stabiliser of the origin $p_0\in\mathbb{CP}^2$ under the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action is

$$H\;\equiv\;\mathrm{Stab}_{\mathrm{SU}(3)}(p_0)\;=\;\mathrm{S}\bigl(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2)\bigr)\;=\;\biggl\{\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\phi} & 0 \\ 0 & V\end{pmatrix}:\,V\in\mathrm{U}(2),\,e^{i\phi}\det V=1\biggr\}\;\cong\;\mathrm{U}(2).$$

(The isomorphism $\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2))\cong\mathrm{U}(2)$ is the standard fact that the constraint $e^{i\phi}\det V=1$ uses up the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factor to fix $e^{i\phi}=(\det V)^{-1}$, leaving $V\in\mathrm{U}(2)$ free.) The dimension is $\dim H=\dim\mathrm{U}(2)=4$.

Proof
Step 1 — Set up the stabiliser condition

$g=\begin{pmatrix}a & B \\ C & D\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ stabilises $p_0=[1:0:0]$ iff $g\cdot e_0\propto e_0$, i.e.~$g\cdot(1,0,0)^\top=(\mu,0,0)^\top$ for some $\mu\in\mathbb{C}^*$. The matrix-vector product $g e_0=(a,C)^\top$ (first column of $g$), so this forces $C=0$.

Step 2 — Use unitarity

The block form $g=\begin{pmatrix}a & B \\ 0 & D\end{pmatrix}$ is unitary iff $g^\dagger g=\mathbb{1}$ block-by-block: $$g^\dagger g=\begin{pmatrix}\bar a & 0 \\ B^\dagger & D^\dagger\end{pmatrix}\!\begin{pmatrix}a & B \\ 0 & D\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}|a|^2 & \bar a B \\ B^\dagger a & B^\dagger B+D^\dagger D\end{pmatrix}.$$ Unitarity yields $|a|^2=1$ (so $a=e^{i\phi}$), $\bar a B=0$ (so $B=0$), and $D^\dagger D=\mathbb{1}_2$ (so $D\in\mathrm{U}(2)$).

Step 3 — The determinant constraint

Then $\det g=e^{i\phi}\det D=1$. So $$H=\biggl\{\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\phi} & 0 \\ 0 & V\end{pmatrix}:V\in\mathrm{U}(2),\;e^{i\phi}\det V=1\biggr\}=:\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2)).$$

Step 4 — Isomorphism $\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2))\cong\mathrm{U}(2)$

The map $V\in\mathrm{U}(2)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}(\det V)^{-1} & 0\\ 0 & V\end{pmatrix}\in H$ is a smooth group isomorphism, with inverse $\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\phi},V)\mapsto V$. Hence $H\cong\mathrm{U}(2)$. $\square$

D.6 — Coset Presentation

Theorem D.6 — Coset Presentation of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

The map $\mathrm{SU}(3)/H\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ defined by $gH\mapsto g\cdot p_0$ is a diffeomorphism that intertwines the left-translation $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action on the coset space with the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$:

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathbb{CP}^2\;\cong\;\mathrm{SU}(3)\big/\mathrm{S}\bigl(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1)\bigr)\;\cong\;\mathrm{SU}(3)\big/\mathrm{U}(2).\;\;}$$

The dimensional accounting is consistent:

$$\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{CP}^2\;=\;\dim\mathrm{SU}(3)-\dim H\;=\;8-4\;=\;4\;\;\checkmark.$$
Justification

The map $\mathrm{SU}(3)/H\to\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the standard orbit map for a transitive action with stabiliser $H$ (cf.~D.4 + D.5). The smoothness and bijectivity follow from the general fact that for a smooth transitive action of a compact Lie group on a manifold, the orbit map is a diffeomorphism onto its image (which is the whole target by transitivity) [He78, Ch.~II, Prop.~4.3]. The equivariance under $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ left-translation is automatic from the construction.

D.7 — Full Isometry Group

The connected component $\mathrm{Isom}_0(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})=\mathrm{PSU}(3)$ (Theorem D.3 + D.1). The full isometry group has additionally a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ factor coming from complex conjugation $c:[Z^0:Z^1:Z^2]\mapsto[\bar Z^0:\bar Z^1:\bar Z^2]$, which is an anti-holomorphic diffeomorphism of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ preserving the FS metric but reversing the complex structure and the Kähler form ($c^*\omega_{\rm FS}=-\omega_{\rm FS}$).

Full Isometry Group
$$\boxed{\;\;\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})\;=\;\mathrm{PSU}(3)\;\rtimes\;\mathbb{Z}_2,\qquad \mathrm{Isom}_0(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})\;=\;\mathrm{PSU}(3).\;\;}$$
Orientation Remark — CORR-014 applied

Complex conjugation on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is orientation-preserving: in complex dimension $2$, $c$ acts as an $\mathbb{R}$-linear involution whose real Jacobian determinant equals $(-1)^n=(-1)^2=+1$ at every point. Equivalently, $c^*(\omega^2/2)=(-\omega)^2/2=\omega^2/2$, and $\omega^2/2$ is the Riemannian volume form. What $c$ reverses is the complex structure and the Kähler form, not the underlying real orientation. The disconnected $\mathbb{Z}_2$ component therefore lies inside $\mathrm{Diff}^+(\mathbb{CP}^2)$. (In odd complex dimensions, e.g.~$\mathbb{CP}^1$, conjugation would reverse orientation.)

For Kaluza–Klein purposes only the connected component matters at the level of the gauge algebra (the disconnected component gives at most a discrete $\mathbb{Z}_2$ outer automorphism); the relevant algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(3)=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{PSU}(3))=\mathrm{Lie}(\mathrm{SU}(3))$ in either case. The global gauge group (e.g.~the distinction between $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ and $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$) is a separate datum determined by the bundle structure on matter representations; this is the open obligation M-17 of KKK-LL-1 (cf.~§F.3 of that notebook) and will be revisited in KKK-LL-9.

D.8 — Hermitian Symmetric Space of Type AIII

Cartan–Helgason Classification

$(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ is a Hermitian symmetric space of compact type, classified by Cartan as Type AIII with $(p,q)=(1,2)$ — that is, $\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2))\cong\mathbb{CP}^2$. The general AIII pattern is $\mathrm{SU}(p+q)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(p)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(q))$, i.e.~the complex Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}_p(\mathbb{C}^{p+q})$; the case $(p,q)=(1,n)$ recovers $\mathbb{CP}^n$. Concretely:

  • Symmetric pair: $(G,H)=(\mathrm{SU}(3),\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1)))$.
  • Symmetric/Cartan involution: $\sigma:\mathrm{SU}(3)\to\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $\sigma(g)=I_{1,2}\,g\,I_{1,2}$ with $I_{1,2}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1)$.
  • Fixed-point group: $G^\sigma_0=\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1))$, agreeing with the stabiliser of D.5 (modulo the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ centre).
  • Rank: $\mathrm{rk}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=1$ (the dimension of a maximal flat totally geodesic submanifold).
  • Compact dual: the noncompact dual of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the complex hyperbolic plane $\mathbb{CH}^2=\mathrm{SU}(1,2)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2))$.

Standard reference: [He78, Ch.~X, §6.2, Type AIII; Ch.~X, Table V].

D.9 — Reductive Splitting and the Isotropy Representation

The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)=\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{SU}(3)$ admits a reductive splitting

Reductive Decomposition
$$\mathfrak{su}(3)\;=\;\mathfrak{h}\;\oplus\;\mathfrak{m},\qquad [\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\subset\mathfrak{h},\;\;[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{m},\;\;[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{h}.$$

Here $\mathfrak{h}=\mathrm{Lie}\,H=\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(1))$ has dimension 4, and $\mathfrak{m}$ is the orthogonal complement (with respect to the Killing form, or equivalently the negative trace form) and has dimension 4. The third bracket condition $[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\subset\mathfrak{h}$ is the additional symmetric-pair condition that makes $\mathbb{CP}^2$ a symmetric (not merely homogeneous) space.

Explicit Splitting in the Gell-Mann Basis at $p_0=[1{:}0{:}0]$ — CORR-016/CORR-026 applied

The stabiliser at $p_0=[1{:}0{:}0]$ consists of those $g\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ such that $g\cdot e_0\in\mathbb{C}^*\cdot e_0$. Infinitesimally, the isotropy algebra $\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{su}(3)$ consists of those anti-Hermitian traceless matrices that fix the line $\mathbb{C}\cdot e_0$, i.e.~matrices of the form $\mathrm{diag}(i\alpha,*,*)$ with the lower-right $2\times 2$ block in $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ and $\mathrm{tr}=0$. Using the Gell-Mann basis $\{T_a=i\lambda_a/2\}_{a=1}^8$ of §E.2:

$$\boxed{\;\;\mathfrak{h}\;=\;\mathrm{span}\bigl\{T_3,\,T_6,\,T_7,\,T_8\bigr\},\qquad\mathfrak{m}\;=\;\mathrm{span}\bigl\{T_1,\,T_2,\,T_4,\,T_5\bigr\}.\;\;}$$

The reasoning: $\lambda_6,\lambda_7$ couple the second and third columns only, leaving $e_0$ fixed; $\lambda_3,\lambda_8$ are diagonal and act by phase on $e_0$ (which is the same as fixing the line $\mathbb{C}\cdot e_0$). By contrast, $\lambda_1,\lambda_2$ couple $e_0\leftrightarrow e_1$ and $\lambda_4,\lambda_5$ couple $e_0\leftrightarrow e_2$, so they move $e_0$ off the line.

Internal structure of $\mathfrak h$ — $\mathfrak{u}(2)=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$

Within $\mathfrak{h}$ the lower-block $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ subalgebra acting on $\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$ is generated by

$$S_1=T_6,\qquad S_2=T_7,\qquad S_3=-\tfrac12\,T_3+\tfrac{\sqrt 3}{2}\,T_8\;=\;\tfrac{i}{2}\,\mathrm{diag}(0,1,-1),$$

satisfying $[S_a,S_b]=-\varepsilon_{abc}S_c$ (the standard anti-Hermitian $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ brackets). The complementary $\mathfrak{u}(1)\subset\mathfrak{h}$ is generated by the orthogonal diagonal combination $\tfrac{\sqrt 3}{2}T_3+\tfrac12 T_8$, which acts as a pure phase on $e_0$ and the opposite phase on the $(e_1,e_2)$ block (preserving $S(\mathrm{U}(1)\times\mathrm{U}(2))$ tracelessness). Hence $\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)\cong\mathfrak{u}(2)$.

Diagnostic: Vanishing of Killing Vectors at $p_0$ — Cross-check with §F.4

An independent diagnostic for the correctness of the isotropy split is the vanishing pattern of the Killing vector fields at $p_0$. A Killing field $K_a$ lies in the infinitesimal stabiliser of $p_0$ iff $K_a|_{p_0}=0$ (a Killing field vanishes at a point iff it generates a one-parameter subgroup fixing that point). From the explicit Killing-vector table of §E.4, at $w=0$ we read off:

$$K_1|_0\neq 0,\;K_2|_0\neq 0,\;K_3|_0=0,\;K_4|_0\neq 0,\;K_5|_0\neq 0,\;K_6|_0=0,\;K_7|_0=0,\;K_8|_0=0,$$

which directly identifies $\mathfrak{m}=\langle T_1,T_2,T_4,T_5\rangle$ (the “moving” directions at $p_0$) and $\mathfrak{h}=\langle T_3,T_6,T_7,T_8\rangle$ (the directions whose fundamental vector field vanishes at $p_0$, i.e.~the infinitesimal stabiliser).

Physical interpretation — CORR-017 applied

The reductive decomposition $\mathfrak{su}(3)=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}$ is a basepoint-dependent piece of representation theory, not a division of the gauge sector into “massless” and “massive” (or “gauge” vs.~“not-gauge”) directions. Its role is to give the linear isotropy representation of $H$ on $T_{p_0}\mathbb{CP}^2$: as an $H$-module, $T_{p_0}\mathbb{CP}^2\cong\mathfrak m$ via the differential of the orbit map.

The actual metric–KK gauge sector is generated by the full isometry algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ — all eight global Killing fields contribute to the 4D Yang–Mills theory after KK reduction. A Killing field may vanish at $p_0$ and still be nonzero (and gauge-relevant) elsewhere on $\mathbb{CP}^2$; transitivity guarantees that the eight Killing fields together span all eight gauge directions globally. The mass spectrum of the KK gauge bosons depends on the moduli stabilisation potential (KKK-LL-7) and on the breaking pattern of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ via discrete Wilson lines and flux, not on the $\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{m}$ split. (Standard reference for these conventions: [CDF91, Vol.~II, Ch.~V].)

§ EKilling Vectors: Explicit Construction

The Killing vector fields of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ generate the Lie algebra of $\mathrm{Isom}_0(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\mathrm{PSU}(3)$, which we have identified in §D.3 with $\mathfrak{su}(3)$. There are therefore exactly eight independent Killing vector fields, one for each generator of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$. We now construct them explicitly.

E.1 — The Killing Equation

Definition E.1 — Killing Vector Field

A smooth vector field $K=K^m(y)\,\partial_m$ on a Riemannian manifold $(M,g)$ is called a Killing vector field if its flow consists of isometries; equivalently, if the Lie derivative of $g$ along $K$ vanishes: $$\mathcal{L}_K g\;=\;0\;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\;\nabla_m K_n\;+\;\nabla_n K_m\;=\;0.$$ The space of Killing vector fields forms a Lie algebra under the commutator, isomorphic up to a sign to the Lie algebra of the connected isometry group [KN63, Ch.~VI, Th.~3.2].

Sign convention (R4-004 applied). The notebook later uses the concrete fundamental-vector-field convention $K_T(p)=(d/dt)|_0\,\exp(tT)\cdot p$ for the left action of $G$ on $M$. For this convention, the assignment $T\mapsto K_T$ is an anti-homomorphism of Lie algebras: $[K_T,K_S]=-K_{[T,S]}$. Equivalently, $T\mapsto -K_T$ is a Lie-algebra homomorphism; or one could use a right action and obtain a homomorphism directly. The anti-homomorphism convention is what feeds §E.5's computation $[K_a,K_b]=+f_{ab}{}^c K_c$ when combined with the anti-Hermitian generator bracket $[T_a,T_b]=-f_{ab}{}^c T_c$ (two sign reversals cancel). See [KN63, Ch.~I, Prop.~4.1; Sh97, Ch.~3] for the general statement.

E.2 — Gell-Mann Basis of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$

Gell-Mann Matrices

The standard basis of the eight-dimensional space of $3\times 3$ traceless Hermitian matrices is the Gell-Mann basis $\{\lambda_a\}_{a=1}^8$:

$$\lambda_1\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;\lambda_2\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&\!-i&0\\i&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;\lambda_3\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\!-1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}\!,$$ $$\lambda_4\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\0&0&0\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;\lambda_5\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&0&\!-i\\0&0&0\\i&0&0\end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;\lambda_6\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix}\!,$$ $$\lambda_7\!=\!\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\0&0&\!-i\\0&i&0\end{pmatrix}\!,\;\;\lambda_8\!=\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\!\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&\!-2\end{pmatrix}\!.$$

The generators of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ are taken anti-Hermitian, $T_a=\tfrac{i}{2}\lambda_a$. The Hermitian-convention Lie bracket on $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is

$$\bigl[\,\lambda_a/2,\,\lambda_b/2\,\bigr]\;=\;i\,f_{ab}{}^c\,(\lambda_c/2),$$

which, multiplying both sides by $i^2=-1$ to convert to anti-Hermitian generators, becomes

$$\boxed{\;\;[\,T_a,\,T_b\,]\;=\;-\,f_{ab}{}^c\,T_c\quad\text{for}\quad T_a=i\lambda_a/2.\;\;}$$

The nonvanishing structure constants $f_{ab}{}^c$ (totally antisymmetric in three indices in the Hermitian convention) are

E.3 — From $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ Generators to Vector Fields on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

For each generator $T_a\in\mathfrak{su}(3)$, the corresponding one-parameter subgroup $\exp(tT_a)\in\mathrm{SU}(3)$ acts on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ by the projection of the linear action on $\mathbb{C}^3$. The associated vector field on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the projection to $\mathbb{CP}^2$ of the radial-quotient lift $\tilde K_a(z)=T_a z$ on $\mathbb{C}^3$.

Killing Vector Construction — General Formula

For $T\in\mathfrak{su}(3)$ and $z\in\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\}$, the lift to $\mathbb{C}^3$ is the linear vector field $\tilde K_T(z)=(Tz)^\mu\,\partial/\partial z^\mu$ (using $\mu=0,1,2$). To descend to $\mathbb{CP}^2$, we project out the radial $\mathbb{C}^*$-direction:

$$K_T([z])\;=\;\pi_*\bigl(\tilde K_T(z)\bigr)\;=\;\bigl(Tz\bigr)^\mu\;-\;\bigl(z^\dagger Tz\bigr)\,z^\mu/|z|^2\;\;\text{(unique horizontal lift in $S^5$)}.$$

In affine chart $U_0$ with $z=(1,w^1,w^2)$ and identifying $w^a\equiv z^a/z^0$ for $a=1,2$, the projected vector field is most economically computed by acting with the differential of the orbit map on $T$:

$$K_T(w,\bar w)\;=\;\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\biggl[\,\bigl(e^{tT}\cdot[1:w^1:w^2]\bigr)\,\biggr]\;\in\;T_{[w]}\mathbb{CP}^2.$$

Working in the chart, we have $e^{tT}\cdot(1,w^1,w^2)^\top=(z^0(t),z^1(t),z^2(t))^\top$ where $\dot z^\mu(0)=T^\mu{}_\nu z^\nu(0)$, with $z(0)=(1,w^1,w^2)^\top$. The affine coordinates evolve as $w^a(t)=z^a(t)/z^0(t)$, and so

$$\dot w^a(0)\;=\;\frac{\dot z^a(0)}{z^0(0)}\;-\;\frac{z^a(0)\,\dot z^0(0)}{(z^0(0))^2}\;=\;\bigl(T^a{}_0\,+\,T^a{}_b\,w^b\bigr)\;-\;w^a\bigl(T^0{}_0\,+\,T^0{}_b\,w^b\bigr).$$
Affine-Chart Formula
$$\boxed{\;\;K_T^a(w,\bar w)\;=\;\bigl(T\,(1,w)\bigr)^a\;-\;w^a\,\bigl(T\,(1,w)\bigr)^0,\qquad a=1,2,\;\;}$$

where on the right we have indicated by $T\,(1,w)$ the matrix-vector product $T\cdot(1,w^1,w^2)^\top$, and superscript $a$ or $0$ selects the corresponding component. (CORR-020 applied.) The real vector field on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (viewed as a $4$-real-dimensional manifold) is

$$K_T\;=\;K_T^a\,\partial_{w^a}\;+\;\overline{K_T^a}\,\partial_{\bar w^a},$$

since reality on a complex manifold requires both holomorphic and antiholomorphic components, with the $\partial_{\bar w}$-components being the complex conjugates of the $\partial_w$-components. The Lie-algebra labels $\lambda_a$ in the Hermitian basis correspond to anti-Hermitian generators $T_a=i\lambda_a/2$ as fixed in §E.2 above.

E.5 — Closure of the Algebra: Direct Verification of the Bracket Sign — R2-002 applied

Sign correction relative to v1.1

Version 1.1 stated $[K_a,K_b]=-f_{ab}{}^c K_c$. This was wrong: with the corrected $K_2$ (and $K_5$) signs from the table above, a direct calculation shows $[K_1,K_2]=+K_3$, and the general bracket law is

$$\boxed{\;\;\bigl[K_a,\,K_b\bigr]\;=\;+\,f_{ab}{}^c\,K_c\;\;}$$

The conceptual explanation is the cancellation of two minus signs:

  1. For the left action $g\cdot[Z]=[gZ]$, the fundamental-vector-field map $T\mapsto K_T$ is an anti-homomorphism of Lie algebras: $[K_{T_a},K_{T_b}]=-K_{[T_a,T_b]}$ [KN63, Ch.~I, Prop.~4.1; Sh97, Ch.~3].
  2. The anti-Hermitian generators $T_a=i\lambda_a/2$ satisfy $[T_a,T_b]=-f_{ab}{}^c T_c$ (cf.~§E.2).

Combining: $[K_a,K_b]=-K_{[T_a,T_b]}=-K_{-f_{ab}{}^cT_c}=+f_{ab}{}^c K_c$. Both minus signs cancel.

Direct verification — $[K_1,K_2]=+K_3$ — with the corrected $K_2$

From the (corrected) table of §E.4:

$$K_1\;=\;\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(1-(w^1)^2\bigr)\partial_{w^1}\;-\;\tfrac{i}{2}\,w^1w^2\,\partial_{w^2}\;+\;\text{c.c.},$$
$$K_2\;=\;-\,\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1+(w^1)^2\bigr)\partial_{w^1}\;-\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,w^1w^2\,\partial_{w^2}\;+\;\text{c.c.}.$$

For the holomorphic parts, the Lie bracket is $[K_1,K_2]=K_1(K_2)-K_2(K_1)$ component by component. The $\partial_{w^1}$-component is

$$K_1(K_2^{w^1})-K_2(K_1^{w^1})\;=\;\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(1-(w^1)^2\bigr)\!\cdot\!\bigl(-w^1\bigr)\;-\;\Bigl[-\tfrac12\bigl(1+(w^1)^2\bigr)\Bigr]\!\cdot\!\tfrac{i}{2}\!\cdot\!(-2w^1)$$
$$=\;-\,\tfrac{i}{2}w^1\bigl(1-(w^1)^2\bigr)\;-\;\tfrac{i}{2}w^1\bigl(1+(w^1)^2\bigr)\;=\;-\,i\,w^1.$$

The $\partial_{w^2}$-component is

$$K_1(K_2^{w^2})-K_2(K_1^{w^2})\;=\;\tfrac{i}{2}\bigl(1-(w^1)^2\bigr)\!\cdot\!\bigl(-\tfrac12 w^2\bigr)\;-\;\bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2} w^1 w^2\bigr)\!\cdot\!\bigl(-\tfrac12 w^1\bigr)$$
$$-\;\Bigl[-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1+(w^1)^2\bigr)\Bigr]\!\cdot\!\bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}\bigr)\!\cdot\!w^2\;-\;\bigl(-\tfrac12 w^1 w^2\bigr)\!\cdot\!\bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}\bigr)w^1\;=\;-\,\tfrac{i}{2}\,w^2.$$

(After cancellation of the $(w^1)^2 w^2$ terms.) Hence

$$[K_1,K_2]\;=\;-\,i\,w^1\,\partial_{w^1}\;-\;\tfrac{i}{2}\,w^2\,\partial_{w^2}\;+\;\text{c.c.}\;=\;+\,K_3,$$

using $K_3=-iw^1\partial_{w^1}-\tfrac{i}{2}w^2\partial_{w^2}+\text{c.c.}$ from the table. This matches $f_{12}{}^3=1$, giving $[K_1,K_2]=+f_{12}{}^3 K_3=+K_3$. $\square$

Convention remark — cancellation of two signs

The vector-field bracket $[K_a,K_b]=+f_{ab}{}^c K_c$ arises from the cancellation of two sign reversals: (i) the left-action anti-homomorphism $[K_{T_a},K_{T_b}]=-K_{[T_a,T_b]}$ [KN63, Ch.~I, Prop.~4.1; Sh97, Ch.~3], (ii) the anti-Hermitian generator bracket $[T_a,T_b]=-f_{ab}{}^c T_c$ (cf.~§E.2). Inserting (ii) into (i): $[K_a,K_b]=-K_{-f_{ab}{}^c T_c}=+f_{ab}{}^c K_c$. The direct calculation above is the unambiguous evidence; this two-sign-cancellation argument is the conceptual explanation. Either an anti-Hermitian generator convention with left action or a Hermitian generator convention with right action gives $+f$; mixing produces $-f$.

E.6 — Verification of the Killing Equation at the Origin

We close this section by verifying the Killing equation $\nabla_{(m} K_{n)}=0$ for $K=K_3$ at the origin $w=0$ of chart $U_0$, as a sanity check on the construction. (CORR-022 applied.)

Setup at $w=0$

At the origin, the metric is $g_{a\bar b}|_0=(b^2/2)\delta_{a\bar b}$, the inverse is $g^{a\bar b}|_0=(2/b^2)\delta^{a\bar b}$, and the Christoffel symbols vanish, $\Gamma|_0=0$, so $\nabla_m K_n|_0=\partial_m K_n|_0$. For $K_3$ we have (cf.~§E.4):

$$K_3^{w^1}\;=\;-\,i\,w^1,\qquad K_3^{w^2}\;=\;-\,\tfrac{i}{2}\,w^2.$$

The lowered-index components, with $K_{3,\bar a}=g_{a\bar a}K_3^a$ (no sum), are:

$$K_{3,\bar 1}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,(-i\,w^1)\;=\;-\,\frac{i\,b^2}{2}\,w^1,\qquad K_{3,\bar 2}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\!\cdot\!\bigl(-\tfrac{i}{2}w^2\bigr)\;=\;-\,\frac{i\,b^2}{4}\,w^2,$$
$$K_{3,1}\;=\;\overline{K_{3,\bar 1}}\;=\;+\,\frac{i\,b^2}{2}\,\bar w^1,\qquad K_{3,2}\;=\;\overline{K_{3,\bar 2}}\;=\;+\,\frac{i\,b^2}{4}\,\bar w^2.$$
Killing equation by pairwise cancellation

The Killing equation on a Kähler manifold splits into three independent pieces:

$$\nabla_a K_b\;+\;\nabla_b K_a\;=\;0,\quad \nabla_{\bar a}K_{\bar b}\;+\;\nabla_{\bar b}K_{\bar a}\;=\;0,\quad \nabla_a K_{\bar b}\;+\;\nabla_{\bar b}K_a\;=\;0.$$

The first two are automatic for holomorphic Killing vectors. The third, at the origin where $\nabla=\partial$, requires

$$\partial_a K_{\bar b}+\partial_{\bar b}K_a\;\big|_{0}\;\stackrel{?}{=}\;0.$$

For $K=K_3$ and $a=b=1$: using the expressions above,

$$\partial_{w^1}K_{3,\bar 1}\;=\;\partial_{w^1}\Bigl(-\tfrac{i\,b^2}{2}w^1\Bigr)\;=\;-\,\frac{i\,b^2}{2},$$
$$\partial_{\bar w^1}K_{3,1}\;=\;\partial_{\bar w^1}\Bigl(+\tfrac{i\,b^2}{2}\bar w^1\Bigr)\;=\;+\,\frac{i\,b^2}{2}.$$

Sum: $\partial_{w^1}K_{3,\bar 1}+\partial_{\bar w^1}K_{3,1}=0$ by pairwise cancellation, not because individual derivatives vanish. Identical reasoning for $a=b=2$:

$$\partial_{w^2}K_{3,\bar 2}+\partial_{\bar w^2}K_{3,2}\;=\;-\tfrac{i\,b^2}{4}+\tfrac{i\,b^2}{4}\;=\;0.$$

The off-diagonal mixed cases $(a\neq b)$ vanish individually since $K_{3,\bar 1}$ depends only on $w^1$ (not $w^2$ or $\bar w^a$) and $K_{3,1}$ depends only on $\bar w^1$ (not $w^a$ or $\bar w^2$). $\square$

General Check

The Killing equation can be checked at any point by the same method, with appropriate Christoffel contributions when away from the origin. The transitivity of the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ action (D.4) and the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-invariance of $g_{\rm FS}$ (D.1) imply that if the Killing equation holds at one point, it holds everywhere — so the origin check is in fact sufficient. The analogous check for the other seven Killing vectors proceeds identically and is omitted.

F.1 — Symplectic Background: Moment Maps on Kähler Manifolds

Definition F.1 — Hamiltonian Action and Moment Map

Let $(M,\omega)$ be a symplectic manifold and let $G$ act on $M$ by symplectomorphisms (i.e.~$g^*\omega=\omega$ for all $g\in G$). Let $K_a$ be the fundamental vector field associated with $T_a\in\mathfrak{g}$ (i.e.~$K_a(p)=\tfrac{d}{dt}\big|_0\exp(tT_a)\cdot p$). The action is called Hamiltonian if there exists an equivariant map $\mu:M\to\mathfrak{g}^*$, written $\mu_a:=\langle\mu,T_a\rangle$, satisfying

$$\iota_{K_a}\omega\;=\;-\,d\mu_a\qquad\forall\,T_a\in\mathfrak{g}.\tag{F.1}$$

The minus sign on the right of (F.1) is the one consistent with $K_a=J\nabla\mu_a$ in the convention $\omega(X,Y)=g(JX,Y)$ (cf.~the box above). Some textbooks adopt the opposite convention $\omega(X,Y)=g(X,JY)$, in which case (F.1) has a plus sign and $K_a=-J\nabla\mu_a$. The two conventions are physically equivalent; we use the first throughout the KKK-LL series. References: [MS98, Ch.~5; Au04, Ch.~III].

Claim F.1 — The SU(3) Action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is Hamiltonian

The Kähler form $\omega_{\rm FS}=i\,g_{a\bar b}\,dw^a\wedge d\bar w^{\bar b}$ is $\mathrm{SU}(3)$-invariant (since both $g_{\rm FS}$ and the complex structure are; cf.~§D.1). Since $H^1(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{R})=0$ (cf.~§A.4), every closed $1$-form on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is exact, so the $1$-forms $\iota_{K_a}\omega$ (which are closed by Cartan's formula and $\mathcal{L}_{K_a}\omega=0$) are globally exact. Hence moment maps $\mu_a$ exist [Au04, Prop.~III.1.5]. Uniqueness up to additive constants (R3-003 applied): if $\mu_a$ and $\mu'_a$ both satisfy $\iota_{K_a}\omega=-d\mu_a$, then $d(\mu_a-\mu'_a)=0$, so $\mu_a-\mu'_a$ is locally constant. Since $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is connected, this constant is global. The zero-mean condition imposed in §F.6 then fixes the constant uniquely.

G.1 — Two Distinct Routes to a 4D Gauge Coupling — R2-008/R2-009 applied

Status — two routes, not two equivalent routes

In KK-style higher-dimensional models, 4D Yang-Mills kinetic terms can originate from two logically distinct sectors: (a) the metric-KK route, in which off-diagonal components of the higher-dimensional metric become 4D gauge fields via the Killing vectors of the internal isometry group; and (b) the internal Yang-Mills route, in which an independent 16D gauge field $\mathcal A_M$ reduces to a 4D gauge field directly. These routes are not equivalent without an additional identification (e.g.~a diagonal embedding, a truncation, or a Wilson-line ansatz tying the metric-KK and bulk-YM sectors together). The present notebook treats only the metric-KK route in detail; the bulk-YM route is mentioned for completeness as Route 2 below, with the equality of couplings explicitly flagged as conditional on such an identification.

Route 1 — Pure-Gravity KK Reduction (metric–KK gauge bosons)

From the 16D Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{\rm EH}=R^{(16)}/(2\kappa_{16}^2)$ with metric KK ansatz $G_{\mu m}=\alpha\,A_\mu^a(x)\,K_a^m(y)$ (where $\alpha$ is an ansatz normalisation constant), the reduction generates a 4D kinetic term

$$\mathcal{L}^{(4)}_{\rm YM}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{4g_4^2}\,\delta_{ab}\,F_{\mu\nu}^a F^{b,\mu\nu},\qquad \frac{1}{g_{4,a}^2}\;=\;C_{\rm ansatz}\,\frac{1}{\kappa_{16}^2}\,\int_{Y^{12}}d^{12}y\sqrt{g_{12}}\,g_{mn}K_a^m K_a^n,\tag{G.1}$$

where $C_{\rm ansatz}$ is a dimensionless constant determined by the KK ansatz normalisation $\alpha$, the Einstein-frame Weyl rescaling, and the chosen gauge-field/generator-trace conventions. The exact value of $C_{\rm ansatz}$ is fixed once and for all in the explicit 16D EH reduction of KKK-LL-3 §C. At this stage the safest statement is

$$\boxed{\;\;\frac{1}{g_{4,a}^2}\;\propto\;\frac{1}{\kappa_{16}^2}\int_{Y^{12}}g_{mn}K_a^m K_a^n\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_{12}}\;\;}\tag{G.1$'$}$$

i.e.~the gauge-coupling dependence on the internal geometry is via the Killing-norm integral, with the absolute multiplicative coefficient deferred. For the product internal manifold $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ with our scaled FS metric, the integral factorises: for each $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ factor, $\int_{Y^{12}}g_{mn}K_a^m K_a^n\,\mathrm{vol}_{g_{12}}=c_K(b)\cdot[\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)]^2$ where $c_K(b)$ is the one-factor Killing-norm integral computed in §G.5.

Route 2 — Independent Internal Yang-Mills (when applicable)

G.3 — Integration by Parts and the Laplace-Beltrami Eigenvalue — CORR-031

Sign convention — pinned once and for all

Throughout this section we use the analyst's Laplace-Beltrami operator $\Delta=\nabla^m\nabla_m=g^{mn}\nabla_m\nabla_n$, which is non-positive on compact closed Riemannian manifolds; its spectrum on scalars is $\{-\Lambda_\ell\}$ with $\Lambda_\ell\geq 0$. Eigenfunctions thus satisfy $-\Delta\phi=\Lambda\phi$ with $\Lambda\geq 0$, and integration by parts on a closed manifold gives

$$\int_M|\nabla\phi|^2\,\mathrm{vol}_g\;=\;-\int_M\phi\,\Delta\phi\,\mathrm{vol}_g\;=\;\Lambda\int_M\phi^2\,\mathrm{vol}_g.\tag{G.3}$$
Theorem G.1 — Moment Maps are First-Mode Eigenfunctions

Each KKK moment map $\mu_a=(b^2/4)\,Z^\dagger\lambda_a Z/|Z|^2$ is an eigenfunction of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{\rm FS})$ with eigenvalue

$$-\Delta\mu_a\;=\;\Lambda_1\,\mu_a,\qquad \Lambda_1\;=\;\frac{12}{b^2}.\tag{G.4}$$

This is the eigenvalue of the adjoint representation $\mathbf{8}=V(1,1)$ of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$.

Proof of (G.4) — Casimir computation, CORR-040 applied

On a homogeneous space $G/H$ with $G$-invariant metric inherited from an $\mathrm{Ad}_G$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$, the Laplacian on a function in the $V(\lambda)$ isotypic component acts as multiplication by the quadratic Casimir eigenvalue $C_2(\lambda)$, up to the overall metric scale [He78, Ch.~II; Wa73]. For $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ with the standard convention $\mathrm{Tr}(\lambda_a\lambda_b)=2\delta_{ab}$,

$$C_2(\ell,\ell)\;=\;\ell(\ell+2)\qquad\text{for the irrep}\;V(\ell,\ell).$$

The overall scale $4/b^2$ is fixed by the FS normalisation: comparing with the classical result [BGM71; IT78] that the first non-zero eigenvalue of $-\Delta$ on $\mathbb{CP}^n$ in the convention with real scalar curvature $\hat R=4n(n+1)/b^2$ (cf.~§C.6: $\hat R=24/b^2$ for $n=2$) is

$$\Lambda_\ell\;=\;\frac{4}{b^2}\,C_2(\ell,\ell)\;=\;\frac{4\ell(\ell+2)}{b^2}.$$

For the adjoint ($\ell=1$): $\Lambda_1=4\cdot 3/b^2=12/b^2$. Since $\mu_a$ transforms in $\mathbf{8}=V(1,1)$, equation (G.4) follows. $\square$

I.1 — Peter-Weyl on the Homogeneous Space $\mathrm{SU}(3)/S(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))$

Theorem I.1 — Peter-Weyl Decomposition (Frobenius Reciprocity)

Let $G=\mathrm{SU}(3)$, $H=S(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))$, $\mathbb{CP}^2=G/H$. Then [Wa73; Ca29]

$$L^2(\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;\bigoplus_{(p,q)\in\hat G_H}\,V(p,q)\otimes V(p,q)^{H,*},\qquad \hat G_H\;:=\;\{(p,q)\in\hat G\,:\,V(p,q)^H\neq 0\},$$

where $V(p,q)$ is the irrep of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ with highest weight $(p,q)$ (Dynkin labels), and $V(p,q)^H$ denotes the $H$-invariant subspace.

Class-One Property — CORR-039 applied

The pair $(G,H)=(\mathrm{SU}(3),\,\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(1)\!\times\!\mathrm{U}(2)))$ is a compact Gelfand pair for scalar functions on $\mathbb{CP}^2$: as $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a compact rank-one symmetric space (cf.~§D.8), each spherical (i.e., $H$-fixed) irreducible representation appears in $L^2(\mathbb{CP}^2)$ with multiplicity exactly one. The spherical irreducibles are precisely $V(\ell,\ell)$ for $\ell\geq 0$, each carrying a one-dimensional $H$-fixed subspace [He00, Ch.~V, §4; Wa73, Ch.~II]. Hence the Peter-Weyl/Frobenius decomposition collapses to

$$L^2(\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;\widehat\bigoplus_{\ell\geq 0}V(\ell,\ell),\qquad \dim V(\ell,\ell)\;=\;(\ell+1)^3.\tag{I.1}$$

This is the $\mathbb{CP}^2$-analogue of the Legendre-polynomial expansion on $S^2=\mathrm{SO}(3)/\mathrm{SO}(2)$ where $L^2(S^2)=\widehat\bigoplus_\ell V_\ell$, $\dim V_\ell=2\ell+1$. See [IT78; Be87, Appendix].

I.2 — Laplace Eigenvalues

Theorem I.2 — Spectrum of $-\Delta_g$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2_b$

The eigenvalues of the (positive) Laplace-Beltrami operator on $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g^{\rm FS}_b)$ are

$$E_\ell\;=\;\frac{4\ell(\ell+2)}{b^2}\;=\;\frac{4(\ell^2+2\ell)}{b^2},\qquad \ell=0,1,2,\ldots,\tag{I.2}$$

each with multiplicity $\dim V(\ell,\ell)=(\ell+1)^3$.

Proof Sketch — Casimir Eigenvalue — CORR-040 applied

On a normal homogeneous space $G/H$ with $G$-invariant metric induced from an $\mathrm{Ad}_G$-invariant inner product on $\mathfrak{g}$, the Laplacian on a function in the $V(\lambda)$ isotypic component acts as multiplication by (a fixed constant times) the quadratic Casimir eigenvalue $C_2(\lambda)$ [He78, Ch.~II; Wa73]. For $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ with the standard normalisation $\mathrm{Tr}(\lambda_a\lambda_b)=2\delta_{ab}$, the Casimir takes the closed form

$$C_2(\ell,\ell)\;=\;\ell(\ell+2)\qquad\text{on }\,V(\ell,\ell).$$

The overall scale of $-\Delta_g$ is then $4/b^2$, fixed unambiguously by the FS normalisation through $\hat R=24/b^2$ (real scalar curvature; equivalently Kähler class $[\omega]=\pi b^2 H$). Hence

$$E_\ell\;=\;\frac{4}{b^2}\,C_2(\ell,\ell)\;=\;\frac{4\,\ell(\ell+2)}{b^2}.$$

Cross-check: for $\ell=1$, $C_2(1,1)=3$, giving $E_1=12/b^2$, in agreement with the moment-map calculation of §G.4. $\square$

J.1 — The Obstruction: $w_2(\mathbb{CP}^2)\neq 0$

Theorem J.1 — $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not Spin

The second Stiefel-Whitney class of $T\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the non-zero element

$$w_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\;=\;c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\;\bmod\;2\;=\;3H\;\bmod\;2\;=\;H\;\in\;H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z}/2),\tag{J.1}$$

where $H$ is the hyperplane class. Since $H\neq 0$ in $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$, the manifold $\mathbb{CP}^2$ does not admit a Spin structure [MS74, Cor.~11.12; LM89, Ch.~II].

Proof of (J.1)

For a complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n$, the total Stiefel-Whitney class of the underlying real tangent bundle equals the reduction modulo 2 of the total Chern class: $w(TX)=c(TX)\bmod 2$. Hence $w_2(TX)=c_1(TX)\bmod 2$. For $X=\mathbb{CP}^2$, $c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=3H$ (cf.~§C.8), so $w_2=3H\bmod 2=H$. Since $H$ generates $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z}/2)$ non-trivially, $w_2\neq 0$. $\square$

J.2 — Canonical Spin$^c$ Structure

Theorem J.2 — Every Almost Complex Manifold is Spin$^c$

An oriented 4-manifold $X$ admits a Spin$^c$ structure iff there exists $c\in H^2(X;\mathbb{Z})$ with $c\equiv w_2(TX)\bmod 2$ [Hi78; LM89, Ch.~II.10]. For an almost complex manifold, such a $c$ is canonically supplied by $c_1(TX)$ itself, since $c_1\equiv w_2\bmod 2$.

▪ Canonical Spin$^c$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ — R4-001 applied

For an almost complex manifold $X$ of complex dimension $n$, the canonical Spin$^c$ structure has spinor bundle and chirality decomposition

$$S^c\;=\;\Lambda^{0,*}\,T^{*(0,1)}X,\qquad S^{c,+}\;=\;\Lambda^{0,\rm even}\,T^{*(0,1)}X,\qquad S^{c,-}\;=\;\Lambda^{0,\rm odd}\,T^{*(0,1)}X,$$

and the Spin$^c$ auxiliary line bundle (i.e., the determinant of the Spin$^c$ structure, denoted $L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}$) is

$$\boxed{\;\;L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}\;=\;K_X^{-1}\;=\;\det T^{(1,0)}X.\;\;}\tag{J.2}$$

For $X=\mathbb{CP}^2$, this gives $L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}=K_{\mathbb{CP}^2}^{-1}\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(3)$ and $c_1(L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}})=3H$. The associated Dirac operator is $\sqrt{2}\,(\bar\partial+\bar\partial^*)$ acting on $(0,*)$-forms.

R4-001 correction note — what was wrong in v1.3. The v1.3 displayed formula $L_{\rm Spin^c}=\det(S^{c,+})\otimes\det(S^{c,-})^{-1}$ is incorrect for the canonical Spin$^c$ structure on a complex manifold of complex dimension $n=2$. In that dimension:
  • $S^{c,+}=\Lambda^{0,0}\oplus\Lambda^{0,2}T^{*(0,1)}X$ (rank 2), so $\det_\mathbb{C} S^{c,+}=\Lambda^{0,2}T^{*(0,1)}X\cong K_X^{-1}$;
  • $S^{c,-}=\Lambda^{0,1}T^{*(0,1)}X$ (rank 2), and $\det_\mathbb{C} S^{c,-}=\Lambda^{0,2}T^{*(0,1)}X\cong K_X^{-1}$ as well (the top exterior power of a rank-2 bundle is the full determinant).
The quotient $\det S^{c,+}\otimes\det S^{c,-,-1}$ is therefore the trivial line bundle on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (real dimension four), not $K_X^{-1}$. The Spin$^c$ auxiliary line is correctly defined as $L_{\mathfrak{s}_{\rm can}}:=\det_\mathbb{C} S^{c,+}$ (or equivalently $\det_\mathbb{C} S^{c,-}$ in this dimension), which is $K_X^{-1}$. (For the standard treatment see [Fr00, Ch.~3.4; LM89, Appendix~D].) The downstream index polynomial $\mathrm{Ind}(D^{c,\rm can}_{\mathcal{O}(k)})=(k+1)(k+2)/2$ is unaffected by this correction.
No formal square root needed — clarification

The formal $K^{-1/2}$ appears only if one wishes to split the canonical Spin$^c$ bundle as a (non-existent on a non-spin manifold) Spin bundle tensored with a square root of $K$:

$$\Lambda^{0,*}T^{*(0,1)}X\;=\;\underbrace{\bigl(\Lambda^{0,*}T^{*(0,1)}X\otimes K_X^{1/2}\bigr)}_{\text{formal Spin bundle (if it existed)}}\otimes K_X^{-1/2},$$
