§ APhysical Motivation: Why a 16-Dimensional Lovelock Kaluza–Klein Programme?

A.1 — The Standard Model and Gravity: The Unification Problem

The Standard Model of particle physics (SM) and Einstein's General Relativity (GR) are the two pillars of contemporary fundamental physics. Each is the most successful predictive theory in its respective regime: the SM agrees with measurement at the per-mille level for selected electroweak precision observables and at the percent level (or worse) for Higgs couplings, rare flavour processes, and many QCD observables [PDG24]; GR has passed every observational test to date, including PPN parameters at parts in $10^{4}$–$10^{5}$, perihelion advance, frame-dragging (Gravity Probe B at the percent level [W14]), and the inspiral phase of binary black-hole mergers [LIGO16] — with the highest-precision laboratory tests (lunar laser ranging, Shapiro delay via Cassini) reaching parts in $10^{5}$–$10^{6}$, not a single universal precision number [W14]. Yet the two theories are conceptually incompatible: the SM is a relativistic quantum gauge theory with a gauge group $\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y$ taken from experiment and on the order of nineteen continuous parameters in the minimal version (gauge couplings, Higgs sector, three generations of charged-fermion Yukawas, CKM, and $\bar\theta$; neutrino masses add further parameters) [W96]; GR is a classical geometric theory of dynamical spacetime curvature whose perturbative quantisation is non-renormalisable [tHV74, GS86].

The KKK programme takes seriously the original Kaluza–Klein insight [Ka21, Kl26] that gauge interactions are gravitational interactions on extra dimensions, but targets three classical weaknesses of the traditional KK mechanism. Specifically, in KKK:

  • The gauge Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_L\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_R$ — the trinification algebra of de Rújula–Georgi–Glashow [dRGG84] — arises from the connected isometry group $\mathrm{PSU}(3)^3\cong[\mathrm{SU}(3)]^3/(\mathbb{Z}_3)^3$ of the internal manifold $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$; the gauge couplings are computable internal volume integrals over Killing-vector norms. The global gauge group acting on matter triplets (full $\mathrm{SU}(3)^3$, with centre charges) is an additional discrete datum requiring a lift from PSU(3)$^3$ to an $\mathrm{SU}(3)^3$-compatible gauge-bundle structure on the gauge bundle over $Y$ (cf.~M-17 of Pass II; Notebook KKK-LL-9).
  • The fibration $Y^{12}\hookrightarrow E^{16}\to M^{1,3}$ is used as a vacuum ansatz in the present notebook (Axiom F.1); the long-term programme aims to derive the fibration from a structureless 16-manifold via Hélein's variational mechanism [H23] (cf.~§ G.2) rather than to assume it, but that dynamical derivation is not carried out here. The specific selection of $Y=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ as the resulting compactification is an independent vacuum ansatz, not a derived output of Hélein's theorem itself.
  • The net chiral index of the twisted $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Dirac operator on $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ realises the value three for a specified family-bundle twist [AS63, AS68], an outcome robust against continuous metric deformations once the twist is fixed; deriving that twist dynamically from anomaly-cancellation / tadpole conditions is a deferred step (Notebook KKK-LL-9, KKK-LL-8).
  • The gravitational sector is the unique metric action yielding second-order field equations under Lovelock's assumptions [La38, Lo71, Lo72] — the Lovelock–Lanczos action — including all $\lfloor D/2\rfloor=8$ dimensionally-extended Euler densities. Ghost-freedom of perturbations around a particular background still requires a linearised analysis. Recent work by Ohashi [Oh26] for compact irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces motivates the necessity of cubic Lovelock terms for ghost evasion and provides a roadmap for moduli stabilisation; whether the analogous result extends to the reducible product $Y=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ employed here is an open calculation, the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-6.

A.2 — Classical Kaluza–Klein: Triumphs and Obstacles

The Kaluza–Klein programme begins with the observation [Ka21] that pure five-dimensional gravity, when reduced on a circle of radius $R$, naturally produces four-dimensional gravity together with an Abelian gauge theory and a scalar (radion). Klein [Kl26] noted that the gauge charge spectrum is quantised in units of $1/R$, anticipating modern flux quantisation. The mature KK programme of the late 1970s and 1980s [W81, DNP86, CDF91, BL87] extended the construction to non-Abelian gauge theories via internal manifolds $Y$ with non-trivial isometry group:

$$\boxed{\;\mathrm{Isom}_0(Y)\;\to\;\mathfrak{g}^{(4\mathrm{D})}_{\rm iso}\;\quad\text{(KK gauge algebra from internal isometries; global structure separate)}\;}$$

The arrow $\mathrm{Isom}_0(Y)\to\mathfrak{g}^{(4\mathrm{D})}_{\rm iso}$ indicates that the connected internal isometry group fixes the gauge Lie algebra of the KK gauge fields (not the global gauge group, which depends on additional discrete data — see § F.3 and M-17 below).

Two Distinct Routes to 4D Gauge Couplings (not to be identified)

There are two separate mechanisms by which 4D gauge fields and their couplings arise in the present setup. The corresponding coupling-volume relations are different and must not be conflated:

  • Route (i): Bulk 16D Yang–Mills (with gauge algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\rm bulk}\equiv\mathfrak{g}_{16}$). A bulk YM field with constant internal zero modes reduces with the elementary identity $-\tfrac{1}{4g_{16}^2}\int_{E^{16}}\sqrt{-G}\,F^2\;\to\;-\tfrac{\mathrm{Vol}(Y)}{4g_{16}^2}\int_{M^{1,3}}\sqrt{-g_4}\,F_{(4)}^2$, hence $$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{g_{4,\rm YM}^2}\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(Y)}{g_{16}^2}\quad\text{(bulk-YM route, constant zero modes, fixed generator normalisation).}\;}$$
  • Route (ii): Metric-isometry KK gauge fields (with gauge algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{\rm iso}\equiv\mathrm{Lie}\,\mathrm{Isom}_0(Y)$). Off-diagonal metric components $\delta g_{\mu m}=A_\mu^a(x)K^a_m(y)$ inherit their kinetic term from the 16D Einstein–Hilbert action via $\sqrt{-G}R^{(16)}$, with coupling $$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{g_{4,\rm KK}^2}\;\sim\;\frac{1}{2\kappa_{16}^2}\int_Y\!d^{12}y\,\sqrt{g_Y}\,g_{mn}\,K^a_{(i)\,m}\,K^b_{(j)\,n}\,\delta_{ab}\delta_{ij}\quad\text{(metric-KK route; subject to 4D Einstein-frame Weyl rescaling).}\;}$$

The two routes give kinetically distinct gauge fields. Any identification — for instance taking $\mathfrak{g}_{\rm bulk}\supset\mathfrak{g}_{\rm iso}$ as a sub-algebra realised by both gauge fields aligned along the isometry directions — requires an explicit matching condition (a diagonal Higgsing of redundant gauge bosons or a constraint imposed at the level of the master action). Such a matching is not automatic and is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-7. Throughout the rest of the present setup, we keep the two routes distinct in notation: $\mathfrak{g}_{\rm iso}$ for the isometry algebra and $\mathfrak{g}_{16}\equiv\mathfrak{g}_{\rm bulk}$ for the bulk YM algebra of § D.

This mechanism is the conceptual ancestor of all modern higher-dimensional unification — including supergravity, string compactifications, and braneworld models. However, classical KK theories face three structural obstacles, identified in numerous independent analyses [W81, W85, DNP86, SS82]:

The Three Obstacles of Classical Kaluza–Klein

Obstacle 1 — The fibration assumption. The base–fibre decomposition $E\simeq M\times Y$ (or more generally, the principal-bundle structure $G\hookrightarrow E\to M$) is postulated by hand rather than derived from dynamics. There is no first-principles reason within classical KK theory why spacetime should split into "four large directions and several small ones". This is what Klein himself acknowledged in his 1926 paper as a "purely formal" assumption, and what Witten [W81] explicitly identified as one of the deepest conceptual deficiencies of the programme.

Obstacle 2 — The Einstein–Hilbert restriction in $D>4$. In $D>4$ dimensions, the Einstein–Hilbert action $S_\mathrm{EH}=\int d^Dx\sqrt{-g}\,R$ is no longer the most general diffeomorphism-invariant action yielding second-order equations of motion. Higher-curvature terms generically suffer the Ostrogradsky instability [Os1850, Wo15] and produce ghost degrees of freedom. The unique class of higher-curvature theories that retains second-order metric field equations and so avoids the generic Ostrogradsky instability in $D>4$ — the Lovelock–Lanczos gravities [La38, Lo71, Lo72] — was almost entirely ignored in the classical KK literature, which contented itself with Einstein–Hilbert gravity in higher dimensions. (Background-dependent tensor-ghost analysis around a particular vacuum requires a separate linearised computation; cf.~Pillar I and Notebook KKK-LL-6.)

Obstacle 3 — The generation puzzle. Why are there three (and only three) generations of fundamental fermions in nature? Classical KK theories had no geometric mechanism to predict this number. Indeed, Witten's celebrated 1985 chiral-fermion theorem [W85] showed that no smooth, purely gravitational compactification on a manifold with isometry group containing the Standard-Model group can produce chiral fermions at all. This is widely regarded as the single most serious blow to the classical KK programme.

A.3 — The Three Obstacles and the KKK Response

Physical Intuition — How KKK Addresses Each Obstacle

The KKK programme addresses the three obstacles via three independent — but mutually reinforcing — technical innovations, each of which is fully established in the modern mathematical or physics literature; their combination is the novel contribution of the present programme. (The word “addresses” rather than “defuses” or “resolves” reflects the epistemic discipline of the present notebook: an obstacle is “addressed” by the existence of a route, but its full resolution requires the deferred derivations of the subsequent notebooks of the series.)

  • Obstacle 1 (fibration) is addressed by Hélein's variational mechanism [H23], which provides a route for a principal-bundle structure $G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ to emerge as a critical point of an action functional $\mathcal{A}[\theta^\mathfrak{g},\pi_\mathfrak{g}]$ on a structureless total space, under stated technical hypotheses (including compactness of the structure group). The mechanism motivates the bundle structure but does not, in the present notebook, derive the specific $Y=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ vacuum: that choice is an independent ansatz (Axiom F.1).
  • Obstacle 2 (gravity in $D>4$) is addressed by adopting the maximal Lovelock–Lanczos action with all $\lfloor D/2\rfloor=8$ Euler densities switched on, providing eight independent EFT parameters $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_7$ (the highest, $\alpha_8$, is topological in $D=16$). Ohashi's recent analysis [Oh26] for compact irreducible Riemannian symmetric spaces (CIRS) establishes that cubic Lovelock terms can be necessary for ghost evasion in such compactifications. Since $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is reducible, the Ohashi result is used here as motivation and constraint, not as a theorem directly applicable to KKK; an explicit Hessian and tensor-ghost calculation on the reducible product is required (Notebook KKK-LL-6).
  • Obstacle 3 (generations) is addressed by the Atiyah–Singer index theorem [AS63, AS68] applied to the twisted $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Dirac operator on $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$. Since each $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factor is not spin but admits a canonical $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure with determinant line $K^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(3)$ [HP78], the index is computable in closed form and realises a net chiral count of three for an appropriate choice of family line bundle. Witten's theorem [W85] is bypassed precisely because the compactification is not a pure-gravity, spin compactification. (Cancellation of the degree-18 chiral anomaly polynomial of the 16D fermion is a separate consistency requirement, deferred to Notebook KKK-LL-8.)

C.2 — Differential-Form Normalisations

Convention C.2 — Forms, Hodge Star, Volume Form

For a $p$-form $\omega_p$ in $D$ dimensions:

$$\omega_p=\frac{1}{p!}\,\omega_{M_1\cdots M_p}\,dx^{M_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{M_p}\,,\qquad |\omega_p|^2\equiv\frac{1}{p!}\,\omega_{M_1\cdots M_p}\,\omega^{M_1\cdots M_p}\,.$$

Levi-Civita conventions: $\varepsilon_{M_1\cdots M_D}$ denotes the totally antisymmetric symbol with $\varepsilon_{01\cdots(D-1)}=+1$ in any coordinate chart; the totally antisymmetric Levi-Civita tensor in mostly-plus Lorentzian signature is

$$E_{M_1\cdots M_D}\;\equiv\;\sqrt{-g}\;\varepsilon_{M_1\cdots M_D}\,,\qquad E^{M_1\cdots M_D}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\varepsilon^{M_1\cdots M_D}\,,$$

so that $E_{0\cdots(D-1)}=\sqrt{-g}$ and $E^{0\cdots(D-1)}=-1/\sqrt{-g}$. The volume form on the oriented Lorentzian manifold is $\mathrm{vol}_D=\sqrt{-g}\,d^Dx$. The Hodge star is defined via the Levi-Civita tensor:

$$(\star\omega)_{M_{p+1}\cdots M_D}=\frac{1}{p!}\,E_{M_1\cdots M_D}\,\omega^{M_1\cdots M_p}\,,\qquad \omega_p\wedge\star\omega_p=|\omega_p|^2\,\mathrm{vol}_D\,.$$

With mostly-plus Lorentzian signature and the orientation fixed above, the Hodge-star square identity on $p$-forms is

$$\star\star\omega_p\;=\;(-1)^{p(D-p)+1}\,\omega_p\qquad\text{(Lorentzian)}\,,\qquad\star\star\omega_p\;=\;(-1)^{p(D-p)}\,\omega_p\qquad\text{(Euclidean)}\,.$$

The Lorentzian extra sign $(-1)^1$ relative to the Euclidean case reflects the time-direction signature $\eta_{00}=-1$. With this identity the $p$-form kinetic term $-\tfrac{1}{2}d C_p\wedge\star dC_p$ has the canonical positive-energy normalisation, and (anti-)self-duality conditions $\star\omega_p=\pm\omega_p$ are consistent only when $\star^2=+1$, i.e.~when $p(D-p)+1$ is even in Lorentzian signature.

C.3 — Gamma Matrices and Clifford Algebra

Convention C.3 — 16D Clifford Algebra and Chirality

The 16-dimensional Dirac matrices $\Gamma^A$ in mostly-plus signature $(-,+,\ldots,+)$ obey

$$\{\Gamma^A,\Gamma^B\}=2\eta^{AB}\,\mathbf{1}_{2^8}\,,\qquad A,B\in\{0,\ldots,15\}\,.$$

In our representation $(\Gamma^0)^2=-\mathbf{1}$ and $(\Gamma^i)^2=+\mathbf{1}$ for $i=1,\ldots,15$. We define the chirality matrix as

$$\Gamma_{17}\;\equiv\;i\,\Gamma^0\Gamma^1\cdots\Gamma^{15}\,,$$

which satisfies $\Gamma_{17}^2=+\mathbf{1}$ (the factor of $i$ compensates for $(\Gamma^0)^2=-1$ in Lorentzian signature) and is Hermitian in any unitary gamma representation [LM89, Ch. I]. Dirac spinors in $D=16$ have $2^{D/2}=2^8=256$ complex components.

Reality structure adopted: complex $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Weyl. We do not impose any Majorana or Majorana–Weyl reality condition on the 16D fermion: a charged $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ spinor coupled to a non-trivial determinant-line connection $A^L$ is intrinsically complex (the would-be charge conjugation acts non-trivially on the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factor). The classification of real Clifford modules in mixed signature $\mathrm{Cl}(s,t)$ is convention-dependent and a full $(1,15)$ table would distract from the central construction; should a real structure be needed for a particular sub-sector (e.g.~a putative neutrino seesaw realisation), it will be reintroduced in a dedicated appendix to Notebook KKK-LL-8 with explicit Clifford-module analysis [LM89, Ch.~I, §§1–5]. Weyl (complex chiral) spinors exist whenever $D$ is even — in particular for $D=16$ — with $\Gamma_{17}\psi=\pm\psi$ projecting onto $2^7=128$-dimensional complex chiral components.

Therefore, in this series the universal fermion of § D and § E.4 is a complex $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Weyl spinor of $\mathrm{Spin}^c(1,15)\equiv(\mathrm{Spin}(1,15)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$: 256 complex Dirac components, 128 complex Weyl components per chirality, charged under the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ factor (via the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure) as well as under the gauge group. The fibre-product / $\mathbb{Z}_2$-quotient structure of the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ group is what makes the construction work on a non-spin manifold; the notation $\mathrm{Spin}(1,15)\otimes\mathrm{U}(1)$ as a tensor product of groups is incorrect [LM89, App.~D; Fr00].

The split under $\mathrm{Spin}(1,15)\to\mathrm{Spin}(1,3)\times\mathrm{Spin}(12)$ uses 4D and 12D representations. With $\gamma^\alpha$ the 4D Dirac matrices satisfying $\{\gamma^\alpha,\gamma^\beta\}=2\eta^{\alpha\beta}\mathbf{1}_4$ and $\gamma_5\equiv i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ obeying $\gamma_5^2=+\mathbf{1}_4$, and $\tilde\gamma^a$ ($a=4,\ldots,15$) the 12D Euclidean Dirac matrices with $\{\tilde\gamma^a,\tilde\gamma^b\}=2\delta^{ab}\mathbf{1}_{64}$ chosen Hermitian, we define the 12D chirality matrix without any phase factor,

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\gamma_{13}\;\equiv\;\tilde\gamma^4\,\tilde\gamma^5\,\cdots\,\tilde\gamma^{15}\,.\;}$$

This is Hermitian and squares to $+\mathbf{1}_{64}$: in $D=12$ Euclidean, reversing the order of the $12$ generators in the product (or computing the square by anticommuting one factor through the rest) requires $\binom{12}{2}=66$ swaps, giving sign $(-1)^{66}=+1$, and each $(\tilde\gamma^a)^2=+\mathbf{1}$. (More generally, in $D$ Euclidean dimensions the product $\tilde\gamma^1\cdots\tilde\gamma^D$ is Hermitian and squares to $(-1)^{D(D-1)/2}$; for $D=12$ this is $+1$, hence no phase factor is needed.) The 16D gammas are the tensor product

$$\Gamma^\alpha=\gamma^\alpha\otimes\mathbf{1}_{64}\,,\qquad \Gamma^a=\gamma_5\otimes\tilde\gamma^a\,,$$

and a direct computation gives $\Gamma_{17}=\gamma_5\otimes\tilde\gamma_{13}$ up to the overall sign fixed by the ordering of internal indices; this sign convention will be tracked consistently throughout the KKK-LL series.

C.4 — Curvature Conventions and the Generalised Kronecker Delta

Convention C.4 — Riemann Tensor

The Riemann curvature tensor is defined by

$$R^M{}_{NPQ}=\partial_P\Gamma^M_{QN}-\partial_Q\Gamma^M_{PN}+\Gamma^M_{PR}\Gamma^R_{QN}-\Gamma^M_{QR}\Gamma^R_{PN}\,,$$

with the sign convention $[\nabla_P,\nabla_Q]V^M=R^M{}_{NPQ}V^N$, following Wald [Wd84] and Misner–Thorne–Wheeler [MTW73]. The Ricci tensor is $R_{MN}=R^P{}_{MPN}$ and the Ricci scalar is $R=g^{MN}R_{MN}$. With these conventions, the Einstein tensor $G_{MN}=R_{MN}-\tfrac{1}{2}Rg_{MN}$ is conserved: $\nabla^M G_{MN}=0$.

Convention C.5 — The Generalised Kronecker Delta

Antisymmetrisation bracket weight (unit-weight convention). Throughout the KKK-LL series we use the unit-weight antisymmetrisation bracket:

$$T_{[a_1\cdots a_q]}\;\equiv\;\frac{1}{q!}\,\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,T_{a_{\sigma(1)}\cdots a_{\sigma(q)}}\,,$$

and similarly for upper indices [Wd84, p.~26; L19, §~2]. With this convention the $q$-fold antisymmetrisation of an already-antisymmetric tensor is the identity ($T_{[a_1\cdots a_q]}=T_{a_1\cdots a_q}$ if $T$ is totally antisymmetric), and the generalised Kronecker delta below has the clean prefactor $(2p)!$ rather than $1$.

The fully antisymmetric generalised Kronecker delta of rank $2p$ is the totally antisymmetric tensor

$$\delta^{A_1B_1\cdots A_pB_p}_{C_1D_1\cdots C_pD_p}\;\equiv\;(2p)!\,\delta^{[A_1}_{C_1}\delta^{B_1}_{D_1}\cdots\delta^{A_p}_{C_p}\delta^{B_p]}_{D_p}\;=\;(2p)!\,\delta^{A_1}_{[C_1}\delta^{B_1}_{D_1}\cdots\delta^{A_p}_{C_p}\delta^{B_p}_{D_p]}\,,$$

antisymmetrised separately and equivalently on either the upper index set $\{A_1,B_1,\ldots,A_p,B_p\}$ or the lower index set $\{C_1,D_1,\ldots,C_p,D_p\}$ (full antisymmetry in both gives the same object). It is equal to the $2p\times 2p$ determinant

$$\delta^{A_1B_1\cdots A_pB_p}_{C_1D_1\cdots C_pD_p}\;=\;\det\!\begin{pmatrix}\delta^{A_1}_{C_1}&\delta^{B_1}_{C_1}&\cdots&\delta^{A_p}_{C_1}&\delta^{B_p}_{C_1}\\[2pt] \delta^{A_1}_{D_1}&\delta^{B_1}_{D_1}&\cdots&\delta^{A_p}_{D_1}&\delta^{B_p}_{D_1}\\[2pt] \vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\[2pt] \delta^{A_1}_{D_p}&\delta^{B_1}_{D_p}&\cdots&\delta^{A_p}_{D_p}&\delta^{B_p}_{D_p}\end{pmatrix}.$$

This object is the kinematical heart of the Lovelock construction: its full antisymmetry is precisely what cancels all third- and higher-derivative terms in the field equations [Lo71, PK13, L19].

Convention C.6 — The Lovelock-Product Canonical Form (Anchored to Lachaume 2019)

The canonical form of the $p$-th Lovelock product adopted throughout the entire KKK-LL series is the one fixed by Lachaume [L19, § 2] following the Fulling–King–Wybourne–Cummins normal-form analysis [FKWC92]. We write spacetime as a Lorentzian manifold of total dimension $D=n+1$ (where $n$ is the spatial dimension) and define

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{R}_p\;\equiv\;\mathcal{L}_p\;=\;\frac{1}{2^p}\,\delta^{\gamma_1\delta_1\gamma_2\delta_2\cdots\gamma_p\delta_p}_{\alpha_1\beta_1\alpha_2\beta_2\cdots\alpha_p\beta_p}\;R^{\alpha_1\beta_1}{}_{\gamma_1\delta_1}R^{\alpha_2\beta_2}{}_{\gamma_2\delta_2}\cdots R^{\alpha_p\beta_p}{}_{\gamma_p\delta_p}\,,\qquad p\in\{0,1,\ldots,p_n\}\,,}$$

with $\mathcal{R}_0\equiv 1$ (the cosmological term) and

$$p_n\;=\;\left\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\right\rfloor\;=\;\lfloor D/2\rfloor\,.$$

For $D=16$ this gives $p_n=8$, and the antisymmetries of the generalised Kronecker delta enforce

$$\mathcal{R}_p\equiv 0\quad\text{for}\quad p>p_n\,,$$

so the Lovelock series automatically truncates [L19, § 2; Lo71]. In $D=2p$ the term $\mathcal{R}_p$ is the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern density and is topological (variational equation vanishes identically); in $D=2p+1$ or higher it is dynamical. In our case ($D=16$) the maximal term $\mathcal{R}_8$ is topological (the 16D Euler density), while $\mathcal{R}_0,\ldots,\mathcal{R}_7$ are dynamical, supplying the eight independent couplings $\alpha_k$ of the master action of § E.

Permutation form. Expanding the generalised Kronecker delta as a sum over $\mathfrak{S}_{2p}$, Lachaume [L19, § 3] writes

$$\mathcal{R}_p\;=\;\frac{1}{2^p}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{2p}}\!\varepsilon(\sigma)\,R^{\mu_{\sigma(1)}\mu_{\sigma(2)}}{}_{\mu_1\mu_2}\cdots R^{\mu_{\sigma(2p-1)}\mu_{\sigma(2p)}}{}_{\mu_{2p-1}\mu_{2p}}\,,$$

a sum of $(2p)!$ terms which are not linearly independent. The reduction to a linearly-independent basis — the central problem solved in [FKWC92] — is summarised in Convention C.7 below.

Convention C.7 — Basis-Counting via Young Plethysms (Lachaume 2019 + FKWC 1992)

The Riemann tensor in its $(2,2)$-covariant form $R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta}$ carries the symmetries: antisymmetric in each pair $[\alpha\beta]$ and $[\gamma\delta]$; symmetric under exchange of the two pairs $R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=R_{\gamma\delta\alpha\beta}$; and obeying the first (algebraic) Bianchi identity $R_{[\alpha\beta\gamma]\delta}=0$. As an irreducible $\mathrm{GL}$-tensor, it transforms in the Young symmetriser corresponding to the partition $(2,2)$ (a $2\times 2$ Young diagram with two rows of two boxes each) [FKWC92, § 3]. Fulling, King, Wybourne and Cummins proved that the number of independent scalar contractions of $p$ Riemann tensors equals the number of Young diagrams with all row lengths even appearing in the decomposition of the $p$-th symmetric plethysm of the $(2,2)$ representation,

$$\mathrm{Sym}^{p}\bigl([2,2]\bigr)\;\equiv\;[2,2]\,\circledS\,[2,2]\,\circledS\,\cdots\,\circledS\,[2,2]\quad(p\ \text{copies})\,,$$

regarded as a representation of the orthogonal group acting on the Riemann-tensor index space, where $\circledS$ denotes the plethysm (symmetric tensor product) operation in the representation ring of $\mathrm{GL}$ or, equivalently, the symmetric power in the lambda-ring of representations. The all-even-row diagrams are the ones invariant under the orthogonal group and hence dual to scalar contractions [FKWC92, Thm.~4.1; L19, § 3]. Convention C.8 — Chern–Gauss–Bonnet Normalisation of the Top Lovelock Density

With the canonical Lachaume normalisation $\mathcal{L}_k=2^{-k}\delta^{\cdots}_{\cdots}\prod R$ of Convention C.6, the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem on a closed oriented Riemannian manifold of even dimension $D=2m$ reads

$$\boxed{\;\chi(M^{2m})\;=\;\frac{1}{(4\pi)^m\,m!}\int_{M^{2m}}\mathcal{L}_m\,\mathrm{vol}_{2m}\,,\qquad \text{equivalently}\qquad \int_{M^{2m}}\mathcal{L}_m\,\mathrm{vol}_{2m}=(4\pi)^m\,m!\;\chi(M^{2m})\,.\;}$$

This is the form of the theorem appropriate to the Lachaume normalisation [L19, § 2]; cf. Chern's original calculation [Ch44], the classical exposition in Kobayashi–Nomizu [KN69, Ch.~XII], and the modern treatments in Eguchi–Gilkey–Hanson [EGH80] and Nash [Na91]. The theorem as stated holds for closed orientable Riemannian manifolds; for the Lorentzian/non-compact total space $E^{16}$ (which asymptotes to $M^{1,3}\times Y$, neither closed nor Riemannian) the integral is to be understood in its Wick-rotated / Euclidean-signature form, or supplemented by appropriate Lovelock boundary terms à la Myers [My87]. The internal factors $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ and the closed product $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ are closed orientable Riemannian and lie squarely in the theorem's domain. Several elementary checks below confirm the normalisation. The unnormalised statement $\int\mathcal{L}_m=\chi(M^{2m})$ is incorrect.

  • $D=2$ (Gauss–Bonnet): $\chi(M^2)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int R\,\mathrm{vol}_2$. For the round $S^2$ of unit radius, $R=2$ and $\mathrm{Vol}=4\pi$, giving $\chi=2$ ✓.
  • $D=4$: $\chi(M^4)=\frac{1}{32\pi^2}\int\mathcal{L}_2\,\mathrm{vol}_4$. For $\mathbb{CP}^2$ Fubini–Study at radius $b$: $\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2)=3$ (from Betti numbers $b_0=b_2=b_4=1$, $b_1=b_3=0$, hence $\chi=\sum(-1)^k b_k=3$) implies $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\mathcal{L}_2=32\pi^2\cdot 3=96\pi^2$ ✓ (independent of $b$, since $\mathcal{L}_2$ is conformally Weyl-weighted on a 4-manifold).
  • $D=16$: $\chi(M^{16})=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^8\cdot 8!}\int\mathcal{L}_8\,\mathrm{vol}_{16}$ — this is the universal factor invoked anywhere in the KKK-LL series when reducing the topological $\mathcal{L}_8$ term.

§ EThe KKK Master Action $S_\mathrm{KKK}$

E.1 — Statement of the Master Action

Master Action of the KKK Programme
$$ \boxed{\; S_\mathrm{KKK}\;=\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{2\kappa_{16}^2}\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;\sum_{k=0}^{8}\alpha_k\,\mathcal{L}_k}_{\text{Lovelock--Lanczos gravity}} \;+\;\underbrace{S_\mathrm{YM}^{(16)}+S_\mathrm{ferm}^{(16)}}_{\text{matter}} \;+\;\underbrace{S_{G_6}\;+\;\sum_{i=1}^{3}S_{F^{(i)}}}_{\text{flux stabilisation}}\;} $$

where $\{\alpha_k\}_{k=0}^{8}$ are nine bare a-priori-independent coefficients, of which eight ($k=0,\ldots,7$) affect the local field equations on a closed 16-manifold and one ($k=8$) is topological (the 16D Euler density, contributing only via the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet term, Convention C.8). With the gravitational prefactor $\frac{1}{2\kappa_{16}^2}$ (mass dimension $[2\kappa_{16}^2]_{\rm mass}=-14$) factored out, dimensional analysis requires

$$\boxed{\;[\alpha_k]_{\rm mass}\;=\;2-2k\,,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,8\,,\;}$$

since $\mathcal{L}_k$ has mass dimension $2k$ (it is a polynomial of degree $k$ in the curvature). Thus $\alpha_0$ has mass dimension $+2$ (the bare cosmological constant), $\alpha_1$ is dimensionless (the Einstein–Hilbert coefficient, conventionally fixed to $\alpha_1=1$ so that $\mathcal{L}_1=R$ enters with the canonical normalisation of the $\frac{1}{2\kappa_{16}^2}\int R$ Einstein–Hilbert term), $\alpha_2$ has $-2$ (Gauss–Bonnet), $\alpha_3$ has $-4$ (cubic Lovelock), and so on down to $\alpha_8$ with mass dimension $-14$. The notation $\mathcal{L}_k\equiv\mathcal{R}_k$ denotes the $k$-th dimensionally-extended Euler density in the canonical Lachaume normalisation $\mathcal{L}_k=2^{-k}\,\delta\cdots R\cdots R$ fixed in Convention C.6; we use $\mathcal{L}_k$ and $\mathcal{R}_k$ interchangeably. The $\alpha_k$ are EFT parameters, not propagating degrees of freedom; the propagating spectrum is the 16D graviton plus the matter fields of § D. The explicit definition of $\mathcal{L}_k$ is given in § E.2 below.

E.2 — The Lovelock–Lanczos Gravitational Sector

Theorem E.1 — Lovelock's Uniqueness Theorem (precise form)

Let $H_{MN}[g]$ be a symmetric rank-2 covariant tensor concomitant of the metric $g_{MN}$ and its first and second partial derivatives, depending no more than linearly on the second derivatives of $g$, and identically conserved $\nabla^M H_{MN}=0$ as a consequence of its dependence on $g$ alone. Lovelock [Lo71] proved that the most general such $H_{MN}$ in $D$ spacetime dimensions is the variational derivative of a linear combination of the dimensionally-continued Euler densities:

$$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{LL}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}\alpha_k\,\mathcal{L}_k\,,\qquad \mathcal{L}_k=\frac{1}{2^k}\,\delta^{A_1B_1\cdots A_kB_k}_{C_1D_1\cdots C_kD_k}\,\prod_{i=1}^{k}R^{C_iD_i}{}_{A_iB_i}\,.$$

Equivalently — the standard Lovelock–Lanczos action — the Lagrangian density yielding such an $H_{MN}$ via the Euler–Lagrange equations is the linear combination above. The corresponding field equations are quasi-linear in the second derivatives of the metric (no third or higher derivatives) and reduce to the Einstein equations in $D=4$ (where only $\mathcal{L}_0$ and $\mathcal{L}_1$ are non-topological), establishing the uniqueness of General Relativity in four dimensions. In the curvature-form language [Za05],

$$\mathcal{L}_k\,\mathrm{vol}=\frac{1}{(D-2k)!}\,\varepsilon_{A_1B_1\cdots A_kB_k\,C_1\cdots C_{D-2k}}\,R^{A_1B_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge R^{A_kB_k}\wedge e^{C_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{C_{D-2k}}\,,$$

where $R^{AB}=\tfrac{1}{2}R^{AB}{}_{MN}\,dx^M\wedge dx^N$ is the curvature 2-form and $e^A=e^A{}_M dx^M$ the vielbein 1-form.

Normalisation cross-check (component vs.~form language). The component definition $\mathcal{L}_k=2^{-k}\delta^{\cdots}_{\cdots}\prod R^{\cdots}{}_{\cdots}$ and the form-language definition above are consistent precisely when the curvature 2-form is written with the $\tfrac{1}{2}$ prefactor $R^{AB}=\tfrac{1}{2}R^{AB}{}_{MN}dx^M\wedge dx^N$ and the volume form is $\mathrm{vol}=\tfrac{1}{D!}\varepsilon_{A_1\cdots A_D}\,e^{A_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{A_D}$. The factor matching can be checked explicitly: at $k=1$, the form expression $\tfrac{1}{(D-2)!}\varepsilon_{AB C_1\cdots C_{D-2}}R^{AB}\wedge e^{C_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{C_{D-2}}=R\cdot\mathrm{vol}$ reproduces $\mathcal{L}_1=R$ (the $\tfrac{1}{2}$ in $R^{AB}$ cancels the $\tfrac{1}{2^1}$ in $\mathcal{L}_1=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{AB}_{CD}R^{CD}{}_{AB}$). At $k=2$, both expressions give $\mathcal{L}_2=R^2-4R_{MN}R^{MN}+R_{MNPQ}R^{MNPQ}$ ✓. The first few densities are:

  • $\mathcal{R}_0=1$ (cosmological constant);
  • $\mathcal{R}_1=R$ (Einstein–Hilbert);
  • $\mathcal{R}_2=R^2-4R^\alpha{}_\gamma R^\gamma{}_\alpha+R^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta}$ (Gauss–Bonnet [La38, Zw85, L19, § 2]).
Explicit Cubic Lovelock Density $\mathcal{R}_3$ (Lachaume 2019, § 2)

The cubic Lovelock density, central to KKK moduli stabilisation, has the following explicit canonical form (Lachaume [L19, § 2], in agreement with Müller-Hoissen [MH85] and the basis-counting of [FKWC92] giving 8 independent scalars at order 3):

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{R}_3 \;=\;\; & R^{3} \;+\; 2\,R^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\alpha\beta}\,R^{\varepsilon\eta}{}_{\gamma\delta}\,R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\varepsilon\eta} \;+\; 3\,R\,R^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\alpha\beta}\,R^{\alpha\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta} \\[6pt] & +\; 8\,R^{\gamma\eta}{}_{\alpha\beta}\,R^{\varepsilon\beta}{}_{\gamma\delta}\,R^{\alpha\delta}{}_{\varepsilon\eta} \;-\; 12\,R\,R^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}\,R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta} \;+\; 16\,R^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}\,R^{\gamma}{}_{\beta}\,R^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma} \\[6pt] & -\; 24\,R^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}\,R^{\alpha\varepsilon}{}_{\gamma\delta}\,R^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\beta\varepsilon} \;+\; 24\,R^{\beta}{}_{\alpha}\,R^{\delta\gamma}\,R^{\alpha\gamma}{}_{\beta\delta} \end{aligned} $$

This is the eight-term expansion of $\mathcal{R}_3$ in the FKWC normal-form basis: the eight independent scalar invariants correspond to the $p=3$ row of the table in Convention C.7 (one 2-row diagram + two 3-row diagrams + five $\geq 4$-row diagrams $=8$ total). The collected, signed coefficients $\{+1,+2,+3,+8,-12,+16,-24,+24\}$ are not themselves all divisible by $2^3=8$; what Lachaume [L19, § 4] establishes is the structural statement that the underlying uncollected permutation multiplicities (before grouping equivalent terms via the Riemann tensor symmetries and the antisymmetry of the generalised Kronecker delta) are divisible by $2^p$ for the order-$p$ density — a divisibility that follows from the $1/2^p$ prefactor of $\mathcal{R}_p$ and the explicit factor of $2^p$ generated by the antisymmetrisations in $\delta^{\cdots}_{\cdots}$. The collected canonical coefficients above are the FKWC-basis coefficients.

Physical relevance for KKK. The presence of multiple independent invariants in $\mathcal{R}_3$ — rather than $R^3$ alone — is essential for the moduli-stabilisation programme: it is precisely the Ricci- and Riemann-dependent terms (i.e. the 3-row and $\geq 4$-row sector of the FKWC basis) that supply the cubic curvature interactions Ohashi [Oh26] requires for ghost evasion on CIRS compactifications. Their explicit evaluation on the Fubini–Study background of each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ — and the precise FKWC-row identification of the eight terms above — is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-3. (The intuition that the last five terms of the expansion correspond to the $\geq 4$-row diagrams is a heuristic motivated by the order in which Lachaume lists them; the formal identification requires the explicit FKWC tableau-to-monomial dictionary.)

Higher Lovelock densities $\mathcal{R}_4,\ldots,\mathcal{R}_8$ in $D=16$

The dimensionally-extended Euler densities $\mathcal{R}_4,\mathcal{R}_5,\mathcal{R}_6$ contribute non-trivially to the field equations in $D=16$ (they are dynamical) and decompose in the FKWC basis into $26,90,409$ independent scalar invariants respectively (Convention C.7). $\mathcal{R}_7$ also contributes to local equations but its FKWC basis dimension is not tabulated in [L19]. The maximal density $\mathcal{R}_8$ is topological in $D=16$ on closed manifolds without boundary: its variation contributes no local bulk equations of motion, and via the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem (Convention C.8) its integral over a closed orientable Riemannian 16-manifold computes the Euler characteristic up to a fixed universal factor,

$$\int_{M^{16}_{\rm closed}}\mathcal{R}_8\,\mathrm{vol}_{16}\;=\;(4\pi)^8\,8!\;\chi(M^{16})\quad\text{(closed Riemannian)}.$$

On manifolds with boundary, or on the non-compact Lorentzian $E^{16}$, $\mathcal{R}_8$ can contribute via Lovelock boundary terms à la Myers [My87], which affect quasi-local charges and the variational principle; the precise treatment of these boundary contributions when the KKK setup is studied on a manifold with boundary or with asymptotic conditions is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-6. Component-form expansions of $\mathcal{R}_4,\ldots,\mathcal{R}_7$ are not written here because they would occupy several pages each. Subsequent notebooks (KKK-LL-4 and beyond) list them in components only when needed for the explicit evaluation on the closed internal $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$, where simplifications from the Kähler–Einstein condition $R_{m\bar n}=(6/b^2)g_{m\bar n}$ reduce the number of independent invariants drastically.

Physical Intuition — Why Lovelock Gravity is the Right Choice

The deep reason that Lovelock Lagrangians yield second-order equations despite involving higher powers of curvature is topological: $\mathcal{L}_k$ is the dimensional continuation of the Euler density of a $2k$-dimensional manifold. In $D=2k$ dimensions, $\int\mathcal{L}_k\,\mathrm{vol}=(4\pi)^k k!\,\chi(M^{2k})$ — a topological invariant in the canonical Lachaume normalisation (Convention C.8) — and the variational equation vanishes identically; in $D>2k$, the same algebraic combination ceases to be topological but retains enough antisymmetry (via the generalised Kronecker delta) to cancel all third- and higher-derivative terms that would otherwise produce Ostrogradsky ghosts. The connection to string theory [Zw85] provides additional physical motivation: the unique ghost-free $\alpha'$-correction to the graviton effective action in the open bosonic string is precisely the Gauss–Bonnet term $\mathcal{L}_2$.

In $D=16$ we have $\lfloor D/2\rfloor=8$, hence the Lovelock series runs from $\mathcal{L}_0$ to $\mathcal{L}_8$, providing nine bare coefficients $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_8$. Of these, $\mathcal{L}_8$ is the 16D Euler density — topological — and does not contribute to the field equations on a closed 16-manifold; the remaining eight, $\alpha_0,\ldots,\alpha_7$, are independent EFT parameters entering the local equations of motion — precisely the parametric resource needed to tune moduli stabilisation and to evade the ghost no-go theorems of [CCdF06, CP08, Oh26].

E.3 — The 16D Yang–Mills Sector

16D Yang–Mills Action

For a 16D gauge connection $\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^I T_I$ valued in the gauge Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{16}$, with field strength $\mathcal{F}_{MN}=\partial_M\mathcal{A}_N-\partial_N\mathcal{A}_M+[\mathcal{A}_M,\mathcal{A}_N]$ and Hermitian generators $T_I$ normalised by $\mathrm{Tr}(T_I T_J)=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{IJ}$ (the standard normalisation for $\mathfrak{su}(N)$, valid block-by-block on $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_L\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_R\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)$):

$$S_\mathrm{YM}^{(16)}=-\frac{1}{2g_{16}^2}\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{F}_{MN}\mathcal{F}^{MN})\;=\;-\frac{1}{4g_{16}^2}\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;\mathcal{F}^I_{MN}\mathcal{F}^{I\,MN}\,.$$

For Yang–Mills in $D$ dimensions the gauge coupling has mass dimension $[g_D^2]_{\rm mass}=4-D$, so in $D=16$ the coupling carries $[g_{16}^2]_{\rm mass}=-12$ and $[g_{16}]_{\rm mass}=-6$. On compactification, the 4D gauge couplings of the bulk-YM route are determined directly by the internal volume,

$$\frac{1}{g_{4,\rm YM}^2}\;=\;\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(Y)}{g_{16}^2}\,,$$

for constant internal zero modes and a fixed generator normalisation. The metric-isometry KK route (gauge fields from off-diagonal metric components) gives a kinematically distinct gauge sector with coupling determined by the Killing-vector norm integrals as in § A.2, and is not identified with the bulk-YM coupling at this level. See § A.2 boxed equations and Notebook KKK-LL-7 for the precise treatment.

E.4 — The 16D Fermionic Sector

16D Fermion Action with $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ and Gauge Connections

The 16D fermion is a complex chiral $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Weyl spinor $\Psi$ (cf.~Convention C.3). A $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure on $E^{16}$ (or, in the present compactification, on each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ factor of $Y^{12}$) is specified by an integer-cohomology determinant line bundle $L$ satisfying the integrality condition

$$c_1(L)\;\equiv\;w_2(TY)\pmod 2\,,$$

i.e.~the first Chern class of $L$ lifts the second Stiefel–Whitney class to integer cohomology. The associated $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ group is the fibre product $\mathrm{Spin}^c(1,15)\equiv(\mathrm{Spin}(1,15)\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$ — not a direct product — with the diagonal $\mathbb{Z}_2$ identified between the kernel of $\mathrm{Spin}\to\mathrm{SO}$ and the square in $\mathrm{U}(1)$ [LM89, App.~D; Fr00]. The $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ spinor bundle is constructed intrinsically from this group structure on the orthonormal frame bundle; no auxiliary square root $L^{1/2}$ of the determinant line as an ordinary line bundle is required, nor would one exist on $\mathbb{CP}^2$, since $c_1(\mathcal{O}(1))=H$ is not divisible by $2$ in $H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathbb{Z})$.

Concrete determinant line for KKK. On each $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factor, the canonical complex $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure (associated with the Kähler/complex structure) has determinant line $K^{-1}=\mathcal{O}(3)$ where $K$ is the canonical bundle [HP78, LM89, App.~D]; the integrality is verified since $c_1(\mathcal{O}(3))=3H\equiv H\equiv w_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)\pmod 2$. On the product $Y=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ the determinant line is the external tensor product

$$L_Y\;=\;\boxtimes_{i=1}^{3}K_i^{-1}\;=\;\pi_c^*\mathcal{O}(3)\otimes\pi_L^*\mathcal{O}(3)\otimes\pi_R^*\mathcal{O}(3)\;\equiv\;\mathcal{O}(3,3,3)\,,$$

with $\pi_i:Y\to\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ the projections; equivalently $c_1(L_Y)=3(H_c+H_L+H_R)$ in $H^2(Y,\mathbb{Z})$. Twisting by an auxiliary honest complex vector bundle $E_\rho$ (e.g.~external tensor products of $\mathcal{O}(k_i)$ for trinification embedding) gives the family Dirac operator $\slashed D^c_{E_\rho}$ on which the index theorem of § G.3 acts.

The Dirac covariant derivative on a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ spinor section $\Psi\in\Gamma(S^c\otimes E_\rho)$ in the chiral $\mathrm{S}^{c}_+$ component is

$$\nabla^{c,\rho}_{M}\,\Psi \;\equiv\;\Bigl(\partial_M\;+\;\tfrac{1}{4}\,\omega_M{}^{AB}\,\Gamma_{AB}\;+\;\tfrac{i}{2}\,A^{L}_M\;+\;i\,\rho(\mathcal{A}_M^I T_I)\Bigr)\Psi\,, $$

where $\omega_M{}^{AB}$ is the Levi-Civita spin connection, $A^L=A^L_M\,dx^M$ is the $\mathrm{U}(1)$ connection on $L$, and $\rho(T_I)$ are the representation matrices of the 16D gauge generators on $V_\rho$. The factor of $\tfrac{1}{2}$ in front of $A^L$ is the intrinsic Spin$^c$ half-charge encoded in the $(\mathrm{Spin}\times\mathrm{U}(1))/\mathbb{Z}_2$ quotient — it appears once, in the connection on the Spin$^c$ spinor bundle, and is not the result of taking a square root of an ordinary line bundle. The action is

$$\boxed{\;S_\mathrm{ferm}^{(16)}\;=\;\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;\overline\Psi\,i\,\Gamma^M\,\nabla^{c,\rho}_M\,\Psi\;,}$$

understood as a shorthand for the Lorentzian chiral fermion action; the precise treatment of chiral projectors, the Hermitian symmetrisation $\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(\overline\Psi i\Gamma^M\nabla_M\Psi - (\nabla_M\overline\Psi) i\Gamma^M\Psi\bigr)$, and the path-integral measure for Weyl spinors in Lorentzian signature will be made fully rigorous in Notebook KKK-LL-8 (Fermionic Reduction and Anomalies).

Anomaly Consistency Requirement (open obligation)

A chiral fermion in $D=16=4\ell$ dimensions can carry local gravitational, gauge, and mixed anomalies organised by a degree-$18$ anomaly polynomial,

$$I_{18}\;=\;\Bigl[\hat A(TE^{16})\;e^{c_1(L)/2}\;\mathrm{ch}(E_\rho)\Bigr]_{\text{degree }18}\;+\;\text{gauge-sector contributions}\,,$$

obtained by descent from the index density [AGW84, GSW87, Bi08]. Consistency of the 16D EFT requires the cancellation of $I_{18}$ — either by an appropriate choice of representation content, by a Green–Schwarz-type counterterm, or by completion to a vectorlike fermion spectrum. All chiral 16D matter statements of the present notebook are conditional on this cancellation, which is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-8. This is an open obligation, not a derived consistency of the present setup.

E.5 — The Flux Sectors $S_{G_6}$ and $S_{F^{(i)}}$

Six-Form Flux Action and Quantisation

Following the construction of Freund–Rubin [FR80] and Sethi–Vafa–Witten-type tadpole arguments [SVW96], we introduce a 6-form field strength $G_6=dC_5+\cdots$ with action

$$S_{G_6}=-\frac{1}{2\cdot 6!\,g_6^2}\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;G_{M_1\cdots M_6}\,G^{M_1\cdots M_6}\,,$$

where $g_6$ is the 6-form gauge coupling, with mass dimension $[g_6^2]_{\rm mass}=4-D+2(p+1)=4-16+12=0$ for a 5-form potential in 16D (i.e.~$g_6^2$ is dimensionless when $G_6$ carries no length scale). To make the quantisation manifestly dimensionless and unambiguous, we fix a single normalisation by introducing a fundamental length $\ell$ (the “flux quantum length”, of order $M_{\rm Pl}^{-1}$ or the string scale) and impose

$$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{(2\pi\ell)^5}\,[G_6]\;\in\;H^6\!\bigl(Y^{12},\mathbb{Z}\bigr)\,,\qquad \frac{1}{(2\pi\ell)^5}\oint_{\Sigma_A}G_6\;=\;N_A\in\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad A=1,\ldots,7\,;\;}$$

for the basis of non-trivial 6-cycles of the closed internal manifold $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$. By the Künneth formula and $H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}[H]/(H^3)$ with $\deg H=2$, the degree-six integer cohomology of the product has rank seven, with basis

$$\bigl\{H_c^2 H_L,\;\;H_c^2 H_R,\;\;H_L^2 H_c,\;\;H_L^2 H_R,\;\;H_R^2 H_c,\;\;H_R^2 H_L,\;\;H_c H_L H_R\bigr\}\,.$$

The full flux ansatz therefore involves seven integer flux quanta $\{N_A\}_{A=1}^{7}$; a single integer $N$ corresponds either to a diagonal one-parameter sublattice or to the universal coefficient $H_c H_L H_R$, an ansatz to be specified per application. In the present notebook we adopt the diagonal-ansatz reading whenever the flux quantum is denoted by a single $N$ without further indices; the full seven-quantum analysis is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-10 (Step 2 of moduli stabilisation).

The 6-form gauge coupling $g_6$ in $S_{G_6}$ above and the flux-quantum length $\ell$ are related to a fundamental magnetic charge $\mu_5$ for the dual 4-form by the Dirac–Zwanziger product condition; we present this as a single consistent normalisation choice (introducing $\ell$ as a fundamental length scale and absorbing dimensions into $C_5$), without further model-dependent input. Alternative conventions in which the periods $\oint G_6$ are directly in $2\pi\mathbb{Z}$ are obtained from the present one by setting $\ell=1$ and rescaling.

For the internal abelian 2-form fluxes on each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$, we likewise fix integer cohomology by writing

$$F^{(i)}\;=\;n_i\;F_0\,,\qquad \bigl[\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\,F_0\bigr]\;=\;\text{generator of }H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2_i,\mathbb{Z})\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}\,,\quad n_i\in\mathbb{Z}\,,$$

so that $\oint_{\mathbb{CP}^1\subset\mathbb{CP}^2}F^{(i)}/(2\pi)=n_i$. In the Fubini–Study metric normalisation of § F.2 (with $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega/(\pi b^2)=1$), this gives the explicit form $F^{(i)}=(2 n_i/b^2)\,\omega_i$ for the integer-cohomology magnetic flux on $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$. The flux action is

$$S_{F^{(i)}}=-\frac{1}{4 g_{(i)}^2}\int_{E^{16}}\!d^{16}x\,\sqrt{-G}\;F^{(i)}_{MN}F^{(i)MN}\,,$$

with the abelian flux generated by a $\mathrm{U}(1)\subset\mathfrak{g}_{16}$ subfactor and $g_{(i)}$ the corresponding 16D gauge coupling. The integer flux quantum $n_i$ controls both the magnitude of $F^{(i)}$ and, via the Atiyah–Singer index theorem of § G.3, the number of chiral zero modes on $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ [Di31, HP78].

§ FGeometric Hypothesis: $E^{16}\simeq M^{1,3}\!\times\!(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$

F.1 — The Internal Manifold $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$

Axiom F.1 — Internal Manifold

We posit that the total 16-dimensional manifold $E^{16}$ admits a (locally) product structure

$$E^{16}\;\simeq\;M^{1,3}\;\times\;Y^{12}\,,\qquad Y^{12}\;\equiv\;\mathbb{CP}^2_c\;\times\;\mathbb{CP}^2_L\;\times\;\mathbb{CP}^2_R\,,$$

where each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ ($i\in\{c,L,R\}$) is endowed with the (scaled) Fubini–Study Kähler–Einstein metric of radius parameter $b_i>0$. The product structure is a vacuum ansatz: it is not derived off-shell from any preceding theorem within the present notebook. Whether the Hélein variational principle (§ G.2) admits a critical-point/saddle whose effective geometry coincides with $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is an open question; the construction therefore proceeds by assuming this vacuum and verifying the consequences, with the dynamical-selection of the vacuum (Hélein → CP² product) marked as a programme target.

Physical Intuition — Why $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$?

The choice of internal manifold is motivated by three independent phenomenological and mathematical considerations, each of which makes $\mathbb{CP}^2$ a natural building block (without uniqueness in the strict sense, as we now make precise):

  1. Gauge algebra. $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is the smallest compact, irreducible, simply connected Riemannian symmetric space of real dimension $\leq 4$ whose isometry Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ — the connected isometry group is $\mathrm{PSU}(3)\equiv\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathbb{Z}_3$ (the centerless form of $\mathrm{SU}(3)$). $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ itself is not a subgroup of $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$: it is a finite cover. Both groups share the same Lie algebra, so the Kaluza–Klein gauge fields fill the algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)$; the global structure (i.e.~which representations are realised on matter fields) is fixed by additional discrete data including the choice of $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ twist (see § F.4). Three copies provide the algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)^{\oplus 3}$ needed for trinification [He78, Be87].
  2. Generation count. The Atiyah–Singer index of the twisted $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Dirac operator on a product of three $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors factorises multiplicatively, and admits the value $3$ for an appropriate choice of family bundle — an index realisation of three generations (not a derivation, since the family bundle is part of the construction; see § G.3 and the corresponding deferred derivation in Notebook KKK-LL-9).
  3. Lovelock dimensions. A single $\mathbb{CP}^2$ has dimension 4; Lovelock terms $\mathcal{R}_k$ with $k\geq 3$ vanish identically on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ alone (since $2k>\dim$). Three copies give $\dim Y^{12}=12$, which together with $\dim M^{1,3}=4$ produces $D=16$ and a Lovelock series running up to $\mathcal{R}_8$ (Convention C.6) — enough higher-curvature freedom to potentially evade the Ohashi class of ghost-no-go theorems [Oh26] and to support moduli stabilisation. Whether this evasion actually succeeds for the reducible product $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is a separate calculation, since Ohashi's theorem is stated for compact irreducible symmetric spaces and $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is reducible.

These three constraints converge on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ as a natural building block; the choice is well-motivated but not unique — e.g.~$S^4$ also has isometry algebra $\mathfrak{so}(5)\supset\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$ and could yield other gauge groups, and other CIRS choices remain to be explored.

F.2 — Fubini–Study Metric and Three Independent Breathing Moduli

Fubini–Study Metric on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

In affine inhomogeneous coordinates $(z^1,z^2)$ on the chart $\{Z^0\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{CP}^2$, the Fubini–Study Kähler–Einstein metric of Kähler scale $b>0$ is [Be87, Ch. 8]:

$$\boxed{\;g^\mathrm{FS}_{a\bar{b}}\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2}\,\partial_a\partial_{\bar{b}}\log\rho^2\;=\;\frac{b^2}{2\rho^2}\left(\delta_{a\bar{b}}\;-\;\frac{\bar{z}^a z^b}{\rho^2}\right),\qquad \rho^2\equiv 1+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2\,.\;}$$

We adopt the real metric convention $ds^2=2\,g_{a\bar{b}}\,dz^a\,d\bar{z}^b$ (sum convention), so that the Kähler 2-form $\omega^\mathrm{FS}=i\,g^\mathrm{FS}_{a\bar{b}}\,dz^a\wedge d\bar{z}^b$ is closed and harmonic with $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1\subset\mathbb{CP}^2}\omega^{\rm FS}=\pi b^2$. The metric is Kähler–Einstein with

$$R^\mathrm{FS}_{mn}=\frac{6}{b^2}\,g^\mathrm{FS}_{mn}\,,\qquad R^\mathrm{FS}=\frac{24}{b^2}\,,\qquad \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2,b)=\frac{\pi^2 b^4}{2}\,.$$

The Kähler potential is $\mathcal{K}=\frac{b^2}{2}\log\rho^2$; the holomorphic sectional curvature is the constant $4/b^2$. Coefficient cross-check. The factor $b^2/2$ in $g^{\rm FS}_{a\bar b}$ and $\mathcal{K}$ is fixed unambiguously by the conventions $ds^2=2g_{a\bar b}dz^a d\bar z^b$ and $\omega=ig_{a\bar b}dz^a\wedge d\bar z^b$ together with the normalisation $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega^{\rm FS}=\pi b^2$: an elementary calculation in polar coordinates on $\mathbb{CP}^1$ gives $\int\omega=2\pi\alpha$ for $\mathcal{K}=\alpha\log\rho^2$, hence $\alpha=b^2/2$. The values $R=24/b^2$, $\mathrm{Vol}=\pi^2 b^4/2$, $I_0=\pi^2 b^4/2$, $I_1=12\pi^2 b^2$, $I_2=96\pi^2$ used in §H are coherent with this choice; some earlier drafts displayed $g_{a\bar b}=b^2\partial\bar\partial\log\rho^2$ (without the factor $1/2$), giving an inconsistent doubling of $\mathrm{Vol}$ and $\int\omega$ relative to the rest of the notebook; this v5.1 statement is the freeze-grade form (cf.~REV.7.7 below).

Classification statement. As a simply connected, complete Kähler manifold of complex dimension 2 with positive constant holomorphic sectional curvature, $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with Fubini–Study metric is unique up to overall scale (this is the complex space-form classification; see [KN69, Ch.~IX, Thm.~7.9]). Note that the statement requires the Kähler structure; absent it, ``holomorphic sectional curvature'' is not defined.

Three Independent Breathing Moduli

On the product $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ we admit three independent Kähler scales $b_1,b_2,b_3>0$, corresponding to three independent breathing modes ("radions") of the three $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors:

$$g^\mathrm{tot}_{mn}(y)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{3}b_i^2\,\hat{g}^\mathrm{FS}_{m_in_i}(y_i)\,,$$

where $\hat{g}^\mathrm{FS}$ is the unit-scale Fubini–Study metric. The fully isotropic case $b_1=b_2=b_3\equiv b$ is the high-symmetry locus; the generic anisotropic vacuum has $(b_1,b_2,b_3)$ all distinct. Whether anisotropic vacua reproduce the observed quark-mass hierarchy across the three generations is a programme target to be tested in Notebook KKK-LL-11, not a result of the present setup.

F.3 — Isometry Group and Trinification Gauge Algebra

Theorem F.1 — Isometry Group of $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ [KN69, He78]

The connected isometry group of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ with the Fubini–Study metric is $\mathrm{PSU}(3)=\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathbb{Z}_3$ of dimension 8, arising from the coset presentation $\mathbb{CP}^2\cong\mathrm{SU}(3)/\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))$. Hence the connected isometry group of $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ is

$$\mathrm{Isom}_0\!\left((\mathbb{CP}^2)^3\right)\;\cong\;\mathrm{PSU}(3)^3\;\cong\;\frac{\mathrm{SU}(3)^3}{(\mathbb{Z}_3)^3}\,,\qquad \dim=24\,.$$

The Lie algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(3)^{\oplus 3}$, generated by eight Killing vector fields $K^a_{(i)}=K^{a\,m}_{(i)}\partial_m$ ($a=1,\ldots,8$) on each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ factor, satisfying

$$\nabla_m K^a_{(i)n}+\nabla_n K^a_{(i)m}=0\,,\qquad [K^a_{(i)},K^b_{(j)}]=\delta_{ij}\,f^{ab}{}_c\,K^c_{(i)}\,,$$

where $f^{ab}{}_c$ are the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ structure constants in the Gell-Mann basis. The Killing potentials (moment maps) $\mu^a$ for the Kähler form $\omega$ on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ satisfy

$$\boxed{\;K^{a\,m}\;=\;J^m{}_n\,\nabla^n\mu^a\,,\qquad i_{K^a}\omega\;=\;d\mu^a\,,\;}$$

where $J^m{}_n$ is the (almost) complex structure on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (equivalent forms differ by sign conventions of $\omega$). Note: the gradient $g_{mn}\nabla^n\mu^a$ alone is not a Killing vector; on a Kähler manifold the Hamiltonian/Killing vector field generated by a moment map $\mu^a$ is the gradient composed with the complex structure, equivalent to using the symplectic (Kähler) form to lower the index, as displayed above [Be87, Ch.~8; Sa89]. Sign-convention verification on $\mathbb{CP}^1$: in the affine chart $z\in\mathbb{C}$, the Fubini–Study Kähler form is $\omega=(ib^2/2)(1+|z|^2)^{-2}\,dz\wedge d\bar z$ (cf.~§ F.2 with B-01). The $\mathfrak{u}(1)$ rotation generator $K=i(z\partial_z-\bar z\partial_{\bar z})$ is Killing (it preserves the FS metric). Its contraction with $\omega$ gives $i_K\omega=(b^2/2)(1+|z|^2)^{-2}(\bar z\,dz+z\,d\bar z)=d\mu$ with $\mu=-(b^2/2)(1+|z|^2)^{-1}+\mathrm{const}$. With $J^m{}_n$ acting as $J(\partial_z)=i\partial_z$, $J(\partial_{\bar z})=-i\partial_{\bar z}$, the relation $K^m=J^m{}_n\nabla^n\mu$ is verified directly with the displayed signs. The convention is thereby locked as adopted; the analogous check on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ for the eight $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ generators proceeds identically and is recorded in Notebook KKK-LL-2 (Pass III obligation MJ-29: closed).

Distinction: Lie algebra vs.~global gauge group. The 24-dimensional Killing algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)^{\oplus 3}$ is what the Kaluza–Klein reduction extracts geometrically from the 16D metric. The global 4D gauge group $G_{\rm gauge}^{(4{\rm D})}$ is not automatically $\mathrm{SU}(3)^3$ (nor $\mathrm{PSU}(3)^3$): it is determined by the global structure under which the matter fields transform — e.g.~the centre $(\mathbb{Z}_3)^3$ acts non-trivially on $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-charged spinors, and the actual global gauge group must be compatible with all representations realised on the zero-mode spectrum. The standard trinification convention takes $G_{\rm gauge}^{(4{\rm D})}=\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_R/\mathbb{Z}_3$ (with the diagonal $\mathbb{Z}_3$ acting trivially on quarks and leptons); the precise global structure consistent with the chiral spectrum will be fixed in Notebook KKK-LL-9.

Physical Intuition — The Trinification Identification

The Kaluza–Klein metric ansatz

$$ds^2_{16}=g_{\mu\nu}^{(4)}(x)\,dx^\mu dx^\nu+g_{mn}^{(12)}(y)\,\bigl(dy^m+A^a_\mu(x)K^a_{(i)m}(y)dx^\mu\bigr)\bigl(dy^n+A^b_\nu(x)K^b_{(j)n}(y)dx^\nu\bigr)$$

identifies, upon dimensional reduction, the off-diagonal metric components $\delta g_{\mu m}=A^a_\mu(x)K^a_m(y)$ as 4D gauge fields $A^a_\mu(x)$ taking values in the 24-dimensional algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)_c\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_L\oplus\mathfrak{su}(3)_R$. The three $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ factors are identified phenomenologically with colour, left, and right respectively, in the trinification embedding of de Rújula–Georgi–Glashow [dRGG84] and Babu–He–Pakvasa [BHP86]:

$$\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_L\times\mathrm{SU}(3)_R\;\xrightarrow{\substack{\text{abelian flux} \\ \text{ or equivariant-bundle data} \\ \text{ or gauge-Higgs VEV}\\ \text{(see footnote on Wilson lines)}}}\;\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_L\times\mathrm{U}(1)_Y\;\xrightarrow{\langle H\rangle}\;\mathrm{SU}(3)_c\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{em}\,.$$

Note on the unavailability of ordinary Wilson lines. Since each $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is simply connected, $\pi_1\bigl((\mathbb{CP}^2)^3\bigr)=0$ and $H^1(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathbb{R})=0$. Ordinary flat Wilson-line breaking à la Hosotani [Ho83], which requires non-contractible 1-cycles supporting holonomy VEVs, is therefore not available on the smooth product $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$. The symmetry-breaking mechanism in KKK is instead realised by one (or a combination) of: (i) abelian internal magnetic flux backgrounds proportional to the Kähler form (cf.~§ E.5); (ii) equivariant-bundle data on the gauge bundle over $Y$; (iii) gauge-Higgs unification VEVs $\langle\mathcal{A}_m\rangle$ along non-Abelian internal directions. If a Hosotani-type construction is required, the geometric setup must be enlarged to a freely-acting quotient or orbifold of $Y$ with non-trivial $\pi_1$, and the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$, index, isometry, and anomaly analysis must be redone on that space. The choice of breaking route is part of model-building, deferred to Notebook KKK-LL-14.

PSU(3) representations vs.~SU(3) fundamental triplets. The connected isometry group $\mathrm{PSU}(3)^3$ acts on the spinor zero modes via its own representations, which are the SU(3) representations with trivial centre action: the adjoint ($\mathbf{8}$), the singlet, and tensor products thereof, but not the fundamental triplet $\mathbf{3}$ (whose centre acts by a non-trivial cube root of unity). Trinification matter, however, sits in fundamental triplets of each $\mathrm{SU}(3)_i$. The required global structure is consistent provided the triplet representations are realised on $\mathrm{Spin}^c$-twisted bundles whose Chern data carry the correct centre charges; this is part of the model-building data fixed in Notebook KKK-LL-9.

F.4 — Why $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not Spin: The $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Structure

$\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not Spin but is canonically $\mathrm{Spin}^c$

For any almost-complex (in particular Kähler) manifold $Z$, the second Stiefel–Whitney class equals the mod-2 reduction of the first Chern class:

$$w_2(TZ)=c_1(TZ)\bmod 2\,.$$

For $\mathbb{CP}^2$, $c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=3H$ where $H\in H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ is the hyperplane class, so $w_2(T\mathbb{CP}^2)=H\neq 0$; thus $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is not spin. However, every almost-complex manifold admits a canonical $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure with determinant line bundle

$$L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c}=K^{-1}=\det(T^{(1,0)}\mathbb{CP}^2)=\mathcal{O}(3)\,,\qquad c_1(L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c})=3H\,,$$

where $K$ is the canonical line bundle and the relation $w_2+c_1(L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c})\equiv 0\pmod 2$ is automatically satisfied. This is the Hawking–Pope construction [HP78], later put on a fully rigorous footing by Lawson–Michelsohn [LM89, Ch. I, §3] and Friedrich [Fr00]. On the closed product $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ the determinant line of the canonical product $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure is the external tensor product

$$L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c}^{(Y)}\;=\;\boxtimes_{i=1}^{3}K_i^{-1}\;\equiv\;\pi_c^*\mathcal{O}(3)\otimes\pi_L^*\mathcal{O}(3)\otimes\pi_R^*\mathcal{O}(3)\;\equiv\;\mathcal{O}(3,3,3)\,,$$

with $\pi_i:Y\to\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ the projection to the $i$-th factor. In Chern-class language this reads $c_1(L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c}^{(Y)})=3(H_c+H_L+H_R)\in H^2(Y,\mathbb{Z})$ as a shorthand for the external sum. (Note: this is not a single CP² line bundle pulled back diagonally; the three factors are independent.)

Importance of the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Structure

The $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure is not a technicality but the very heart of the KKK fermion sector: it is precisely the violation of the spin condition $w_2\neq 0$, compensated by a $\mathrm{U}(1)$ gauge field on the determinant line bundle, that allows the construction to evade Witten's chiral fermion theorem [W85], which forbids chiral fermions from pure-gravity compactifications on spin manifolds with isometry containing the SM group. In KKK the chirality comes not from pure gravity but from the gauge connection on $L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c}$ — an internal flux indistinguishable, at the level of zero-mode counting, from a magnetic flux in a Wu–Yang monopole background. This is what is gained by passing to $\mathrm{Spin}^c$.

G.2 — Pillar II — Hélein's Dynamical Principal-Bundle Mechanism

Pillar II — Geometric Sector
Hélein's Dynamical Principal-Bundle Mechanism

Statement. Hélein [H23] formulates a variational principle $\mathcal{A}[\theta^\mathfrak{g},\pi_\mathfrak{g}]$ on a $(4+r)$-dimensional total space $\mathcal{Y}$ (an a-priori structureless manifold) involving a $\mathfrak{g}$-valued 1-form $\theta$ and an auxiliary Lagrange-multiplier field $\pi$. Under hypotheses including compactness of the fibre, of the resulting base, and additional regularity conditions, critical points of $\mathcal{A}$ induce a principal $G$-bundle structure $G\hookrightarrow\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}$ over a 4D base $\mathcal{X}$, with induced metric and connection solving the coupled Einstein–Yang–Mills system. The mechanism therefore derives the principal-bundle structure (rather than postulating it), but it does not by itself derive the specific internal manifold, its metric, its fluxes, or the choice of vacuum.

Established by. Hélein 2023 [H23], building on Ehresmann 1951 [Eh51] (Ehresmann fibration theorem) and the Frobenius integrability theorem [Fr1877]. The mechanism uses the Lagrange-multiplier sector to enforce a Maurer–Cartan integrability condition along the fibre direction.

Rôle in KKK. Hélein's mechanism is used as a motivational argument for why a fibration/foliation structure can be expected to emerge dynamically rather than being imposed by hand. It is not used to derive $E^{16}\simeq M^{1,3}\times(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ — that specific choice of internal manifold remains an independent vacuum hypothesis (cf.~Axiom F.1). The relation between Hélein's structure-group bundle and the KK isometry algebra is the following: the local fibre of the bundle is a homogeneous space $G/H$, and identifying $G/H=\mathbb{CP}^2$ corresponds to $G=\mathrm{SU}(3)$ (or $\mathrm{PSU}(3)$) and $H=\mathrm{S}(\mathrm{U}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1))$ (the isotropy group at a point); the gauge algebra of the 4D theory is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ (the isometry algebra of the fibre), not $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ (the isotropy algebra). Clarifying this structure-group / isometry / isotropy distinction across the Hélein and KK formalisms is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-2.

G.3 — Pillar III — Index-Theoretic Generation Counting on $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$

Pillar III — Topological Sector
Index-Theoretic Generation Counting on $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$

Statement. The number of 4D chiral fermion families arising from the dimensional reduction of the 16D $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Weyl fermion equals the topological index of the twisted $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Dirac operator on $Y^{12}=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$, computed by the Atiyah–Singer theorem:

$$\mathrm{Ind}_Y(\slashed{D}^c_E)=\int_Y e^{\tfrac{1}{2}c_1(L_{\mathrm{Spin}^c})}\,\hat{A}(TY)\,\mathrm{ch}(E)\;\in\;\mathbb{Z}\,,$$

where the right-hand side is understood as the top-degree component of the integrand integrated over $Y$. For a specific choice of family bundle $E$ (specified in § G.3), the index evaluates to $3$. This is an index realisation of the three-generation count, not a prediction in the strict sense: the family bundle $E$ is part of the construction. Deriving $E$ from dynamics (e.g.~from a tadpole-cancellation condition or from minimisation of the flux-induced potential) is a required deferred step.

Established by. Atiyah–Singer 1963–1971 [AS63, AS68, AS71], modernly Lawson–Michelsohn [LM89]; the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ refinement and its identification with Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch is due to Hirzebruch 1966 [Hi66], and the first physics application to non-spin internal spaces is Hawking–Pope 1978 [HP78].

Rôle in KKK. Provides a topological mechanism for family number that is robust against continuous metric deformations. Relation to Witten's chiral-fermion theorem. Witten [W85] showed that compactification of pure gravity (no additional gauge fields, no $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure) on a smooth compact spin manifold with SM-isometry gauge algebra cannot yield chiral 4D fermions, under the precise hypotheses: (i)~smooth compact internal manifold $Y$; (ii)~pure-gravity setup, i.e.~zero modes drawn only from the 11D (or higher-D) gravitino; (iii)~standard spin structure on $Y$; (iv)~SM gauge algebra realised via Killing vectors. The KKK setup violates hypotheses (ii) and (iii): it includes an independent gauge sector and an internal flux, and the internal manifold is non-spin (carrying a $\mathrm{Spin}^c$, not spin, structure). The $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ flux provides the "magnetic" coupling that breaks the obstruction to chirality.

Explicit Index Calculation — Three Generations as an Index Realisation

For the canonical complex Spin$^c$ Dirac operator on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ — identified up to the canonical $K^{-1}$ twist with the Dolbeault operator $\bar\partial+\bar\partial^\dagger\colon\Omega^{0,\rm even}\to\Omega^{0,\rm odd}$ — twisted by the holomorphic line bundle $\mathcal{O}(k)$, the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem [Hi66] gives the holomorphic Euler characteristic

$$\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{CP}^2}(\slashed{D}^c_{\mathcal{O}(k)})\;=\;\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathcal{O}(k))\;=\;\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\,,\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,.$$

For $k\geq 0$, all higher cohomology vanishes ($H^j(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathcal{O}(k))=0$ for $j>0$ by Kodaira vanishing) and $\chi=\dim H^0(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathcal{O}(k))$ is the dimension of the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree $k$ in three projective variables. For $k<0$, higher cohomology contributes via Serre duality $H^j(\mathcal{O}(k))\cong H^{2-j}(\mathcal{O}(-k-3))^\vee$, and the polynomial formula above still gives the Euler characteristic (which can be negative). For $k=1$, $\chi=\mathrm{Ind}=3$ (corresponding to the three projective coordinates $Z^0,Z^1,Z^2$). On the product $(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3$ with external-product family bundle $E_F=E_c\boxtimes E_L\boxtimes E_R$ — using the canonical product $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ structure with determinant line $\mathcal{O}(3,3,3)$ — the multiplicativity of the Chern character on external products gives

$$\mathrm{Ind}_{Y^{12}}(\slashed{D}^c_{E_F})=\prod_{i=c,L,R}\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathbb{CP}^2_i}(\slashed{D}^c_{E_i})\,.$$

Choosing one factor to carry $E=\mathcal{O}(1)$ (giving $3$) and the other two trivial (giving $1$ each) realises a net chiral index of exactly 3. This is the index realisation underlying the KKK three-generation count; the choice of bundle is part of the construction and is justified separately in Notebook KKK-LL-9 via flux quantisation and tadpole considerations.

Limits of what the index controls. The index $\mathrm{Ind}(\slashed D^c_E)=\dim\ker\slashed D^c_E|_{S^+}-\dim\ker\slashed D^c_E|_{S^-}$ is by definition a net chiral index (left minus right zero modes in the chosen bundle representation). It does not, by itself, establish (i) that the zero modes form three complete Standard-Model or trinification families without vectorlike exotics; (ii) the correct hypercharge assignments on the 4D matter; (iii) the cancellation of 4D anomalies. Each of these requires an independent computation of the cohomology of $\slashed D^c_E$, the representation content under the residual gauge symmetry, and the 4D anomaly polynomial — the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-9.

$$m_H^2\;\sim\;\frac{c_H}{b^2}\,,$$

with a model-dependent group-theoretic and geometric coefficient $c_H$ that — for the isotropic radius $b$ — depends on the choice of internal profile $\eta_m$ and the gauge embedding. (For three equal $\mathbb{CP}^2$ factors the total internal Ricci scalar is $R(Y^{12})=3\cdot 24/b^2=72/b^2$; this is itself of mass dimension $[b^{-2}]$, so any formula $m_H^2\propto R(Y)/b^2$ is dimensionally inconsistent and is to be reread as $m_H^2\propto c_H/b^2$ with $c_H$ a dimensionless number determined by the explicit reduction.) Vanishing of $\lambda_{5,6,7}$, if realised, would distinguish the construction from a generic 2HDM and would be testable [Br12].

I.2 — Yukawa Hierarchies (Programme Target)

Yukawa couplings arise as internal overlap integrals between fermion zero modes of the twisted $\mathrm{Spin}^c$ Dirac operator and the internal Higgs profile:

$$Y_{\alpha\beta}=\int_{Y^{12}}\!d^{12}y\,\sqrt{g_{12}}\,\chi^\dagger_{1,\alpha}(y)\,\tilde\gamma^m\eta_m(y)\,\chi_{2,\beta}(y)\,.$$

By analogy with the magnetised toroidal/intersecting-brane constructions of Cremades–Ib\'a\~nez–Marchesano [CIM04, MS05], one anticipates that magnetic flux on each $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ factor localises zero modes as coherent states peaked at distinct points $p_i\in\mathbb{CP}^2_i$, producing exponentially-suppressed overlaps

$$Y_{ij}\sim\exp\!\left[-\tfrac{q}{2}\bigl(\tilde d^{2}_{iH}+\tilde d^{2}_{jH}\bigr)\right]e^{i\Theta_{ij}}\,,\qquad \tilde d_{iH}\equiv d^{\mathrm{FS}}_{iH}/b\,,\;\; q\;\;\text{dimensionless},$$

with $d^{\mathrm{FS}}_{iH}$ the Fubini–Study geodesic distance between the $i$-th localisation point $p_i\in\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ and the Higgs-profile centre $p_H$, made dimensionless by dividing by the internal radius $b$; equivalently, $q$ may be absorbed into a redefinition of the magnetic-length scale on $\mathbb{CP}^2_i$ and the exponent rewritten as $\exp[-(d^{\mathrm{FS}})^2/(2\ell_B^2)]$ with $\ell_B^2=b^2/q$. The dimensional consistency of the exponent is enforced at this level; the precise asymptotic form on the FS background — not on a flat torus — requires the Berezin–Toeplitz / Bergman-kernel analysis below.

This is an analogy/conjecture, not a derivation. The Cremades et al. construction is intrinsically toroidal/flat; the corresponding asymptotic behaviour on the Fubini–Study background of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ requires a separate Berezin–Toeplitz/Bergman-kernel analysis with large line bundle, which is the subject of Notebook KKK-LL-11. The matching of (i)~the asymptotic Gaussian profile on coherent states, (ii)~the resulting overlap-integral hierarchy, and (iii)~the observed CKM phase structure must be established explicitly there.

I.3 — The QCD $\theta$-Term (Programme Target)

A candidate 16D topological coupling between the colour gauge field $F_c$ and the quantised internal $G_6$ flux, anticipated to be the geometric origin of the 4D QCD angle, has the schematic form (not a frozen normalisation; the precise $2\pi$, trace, and intersection-pairing structure is deferred to Notebook KKK-LL-16). To keep the integrand dimensionless and consistent with the $G_6$ quantisation $[G_6]/(2\pi\ell)^5\in H^6(Y,\mathbb{Z})$ of § E.5, we introduce the dimensionless integer flux class

$$\mathcal{G}_6\;\equiv\;\frac{G_6}{(2\pi\ell)^5}\,,\qquad [\mathcal{G}_6]\in H^6(Y,\mathbb{Z})\,,$$

and write the topological coupling as

$$S^{(16)}_\theta\;=\;\kappa_\theta\int_{E^{16}}\mathcal{P}_4(F_c)\wedge\mathcal{G}_6\wedge\mathcal{G}_6\qquad\text{(schematic)}\,,$$

equivalently $\kappa_\theta(2\pi\ell)^{-10}\int\mathcal{P}_4(F_c)\wedge G_6\wedge G_6$. (The earlier draft form used the prefactor $(2\pi\ell)^{-6}$, which is inconsistent with the $(2\pi\ell)^{-5}$ quantisation of $G_6$ and is hereby retired; cf.~REV.7.8 B-01.)

where $\mathcal{P}_4(F_c)\equiv\mathrm{tr}_R(F_c\wedge F_c)$ is the degree-four characteristic form in the colour representation $R$, proportional to the second Chern character density $\mathrm{ch}_2(F_c)$ (or equivalently to $-c_2+\tfrac{1}{2}c_1^2$ for a colour bundle, with trace normalisation $\mathrm{tr}_F(T_IT_J)=\tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{IJ}$ in the fundamental; cf.~Convention C.7). The notation $\mathcal{P}_4$ avoids confusion with the Pontryagin classes $p_k(TY)$: it denotes a gauge characteristic form, not a tangent-bundle one. Note: $\mathrm{tr}(F\wedge F)$ is not the fourth Chern character $\mathrm{ch}_4$ (an 8-form built from $F^4$). The degree count is $4+6+6=16$ ✓.

Required reduction. The coefficient $\kappa_\theta$ must carry all relevant dimensions, trace-normalisation factors, $2\pi$ factors, and the flux-lattice pairing; specifically, expressing $G_6$ in the integer-cohomology basis as $\tfrac{1}{(2\pi\ell)^5}[G_6]=\sum_A N_A[\omega^{(6)}_A]\in H^6(Y,\mathbb{Z})$ with the seven-element basis of M-12, dimensional reduction of $S^{(16)}_\theta$ produces a 4D theta angle of the form

$$\theta_{\rm QCD}\;=\;\kappa_\theta\,Q_{AB}\,N_A\,N_B\quad\pmod{2\pi}\,,$$

where $Q_{AB}$ is the intersection matrix encoding the pairing $\int_Y\omega^{(6)}_A\wedge\omega^{(6)}_B$ on the de Rham middle cohomology of $Y$, and where $\kappa_\theta$ must be fixed by anomaly matching. Whether this reduction yields a Peccei–Quinn-relaxable $\theta_{\rm QCD}$, and whether the model contains a natural PQ axion with appropriate decay constant and quality protection, is a programme target requiring an explicit construction (Notebook KKK-LL-16).

If such an axion exists, its mass at leading two-flavour chiral order is the standard QCD axion expression [W78, Wi78, DC16]:

$$\boxed{\;m_a\;=\;\frac{m_\pi f_\pi}{f_a}\,\frac{\sqrt{z}}{1+z}\,,\qquad z\equiv\frac{m_u}{m_d}\approx 0.485\,,\;}$$

up to NLO chiral corrections. (The earlier draft formula $m_a\propto\sqrt{z/(1+z)}$ contained an incorrect $z$-dependence and is superseded.) The modern precision form, including NLO and electromagnetic corrections, is given in Di Cortona–Hardy–Vega–Villadoro [DC16]. Whether the model satisfies the neutron-EDM bound $|\bar\theta|\lesssim 10^{-10}$ [Ab20] requires both an explicit PQ-axion construction and a verification that the axion-quality problem is solved by the geometric origin of the coupling.

I.4 — Holographic Analogy for Hadrons (Phenomenological Analogy)

The KKK setup can be compared phenomenologically to holographic QCD constructions [Ma98, PS02, SS05, EKSS05, KKSS06], in which a higher-dimensional gravity dual reproduces the hadronic resonance spectrum. The present construction is not a holographic dual in the AdS/CFT sense: $E^{16}$ has no AdS-like warped radial direction, no boundary dictionary, no large-$N$ limit, and no defined holographic action. We use the holographic constructions only as a quantitative benchmark for the KK mass scale and resonance spectrum that the KKK programme aims to reproduce.

As a phenomenological calibration of the hard-wall model [EKSS05], with $z_{\rm IR}^{-1}\sim 323$ MeV chosen to fit, $m_\rho=\gamma_{0,1}/z_{\rm IR}=2.405\cdot 323=777$ MeV vs.~$m_\rho^{\rm exp}=775$ MeV [PDG24]. This is a hard-wall fit, not a KKK output: 323 MeV is the fitted inverse wall position, not a KKK prediction. The KKK programme target is to derive $z_{\rm IR}$ (or its analogue) from the internal moduli stabilisation of § H and to check consistency with the observed $\rho$-meson and other low-lying hadrons (Notebook KKK-LL-13). The qualitative facts — that the KK mass $m_{\rm KK}\sim\sqrt{12}/b\sim 700$ MeV at $b\sim 1$ fm is in the hadronic range — provide an order-of-magnitude consistency check, not a derivation.

On UV Completion (Speculation)

The KKK programme is presented at the level of an effective field theory in 16 dimensions; a microscopic UV completion (string theory, M-theory, or otherwise) is not specified. Whether KKK can be embedded into a string-theoretic UV completion is open and faces hard obstacles, including: the critical-dimension constraint of perturbative bosonic/superstring theory (26 / 10), the requirement of anomaly cancellation in 16D, the existence of a ghost-free order-by-order expansion of all Lovelock terms, and the consistency of the $\mathrm{Spin}^c$/charge structure with bulk string degrees of freedom. We do not claim that KKK is automatically embeddable in F-theory or other generalised string compactifications: such an embedding is a research question, not a part of the present construction. The principal practical virtue of the EFT approach is that physics can be computed at the 4D effective level (within the EFT regime of validity) without committing to a specific microscopic origin.
