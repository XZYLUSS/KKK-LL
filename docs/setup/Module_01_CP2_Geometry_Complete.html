GEOMETRY §1. Definition and Topology of ℂℙ²

Definition 1.1: Complex Projective Space

The complex projective plane ℂℙ² is defined as the space of complex lines through the origin in ℂ³:

$$\boxed{\mathbb{CP}^2 := \frac{\mathbb{C}^3 \setminus \{0\}}{\mathbb{C}^*}}$$

where ℂ* = ℂ \ {0} acts by scalar multiplication: [Z⁰:Z¹:Z²] ∼ [λZ⁰:λZ¹:λZ²] for all λ ∈ ℂ*.

Why ℂℙ² for internal space?
  1. Compactness: ℂℙ² is compact (closed and bounded), essential for Kaluza-Klein compactification
  2. Rich geometry: Complex dimension 2 = real dimension 4, providing sufficient structure
  3. Isometry group: Isom(ℂℙ²) ≅ SU(3)/ℤ₃ naturally gives QCD gauge group
  4. Kähler manifold: Complex, Riemannian, and symplectic structures all compatible
  5. Positive curvature: Einstein manifold with positive scalar curvature

1.1 Topological Properties

Theorem 1.1: Topological Characterization

ℂℙ² is a compact, simply-connected, orientable 4-manifold with:

\begin{align} \text{Real dimension:} &\quad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathbb{CP}^2) = 4 \\ \text{Complex dimension:} &\quad \dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{CP}^2) = 2 \\ \text{Fundamental group:} &\quad \pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2) = \{e\} \\ \text{Second homotopy:} &\quad \pi_2(\mathbb{CP}^2) = \mathbb{Z} \\ \text{Cohomology:} &\quad H^*(\mathbb{CP}^2; \mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^3), \quad |x| = 2 \end{align}
Verification of Simple-Connectedness:
Step 1: ℂℙ² can be constructed via cell decomposition: ℂℙ² = e⁰ ∪ e² ∪ e⁴
Step 2: The lowest-dimensional cell is e⁰ (a point), so no 1-cells exist
Step 3: By cellular homology, H₁(ℂℙ²) = 0 ⟹ π₁(ℂℙ²) = 0 (Hurewicz theorem)
Conclusion: ℂℙ² is simply-connected ✓

GEOMETRY §2. Homogeneous Coordinates and Charts

2.1 Homogeneous Coordinates

Definition 2.1: Homogeneous Coordinates

A point in ℂℙ² is represented by homogeneous coordinates [Z⁰:Z¹:Z²] where (Z⁰,Z¹,Z²) ∈ ℂ³\{0} with equivalence relation:

$$[Z^0:Z^1:Z^2] = [\lambda Z^0:\lambda Z^1:\lambda Z^2] \quad \forall \lambda \in \mathbb{C}^*$$

2.2 Standard Chart Covering

ℂℙ² is covered by three standard charts U₀, U₁, U₂:

Chart U₀: {Z⁰ ≠ 0}

\begin{align} z^1 &= \frac{Z^1}{Z^0}, \quad z^2 = \frac{Z^2}{Z^0} \\ [Z^0:Z^1:Z^2] &\leftrightarrow (z^1, z^2) \in \mathbb{C}^2 \end{align}

This chart covers all points except the "line at infinity" {Z⁰ = 0}

Chart U₁: {Z¹ ≠ 0}

\begin{align} w^0 &= \frac{Z^0}{Z^1}, \quad w^2 = \frac{Z^2}{Z^1} \\ [Z^0:Z^1:Z^2] &\leftrightarrow (w^0, w^2) \in \mathbb{C}^2 \end{align}

Transition functions on overlaps are holomorphic

Verification of Chart Compatibility:

On U₀ ∩ U₁ where Z⁰, Z¹ ≠ 0, the transition functions are:

\begin{align} w^0 &= \frac{Z^0}{Z^1} = \frac{1}{z^1}, \quad w^2 = \frac{Z^2}{Z^1} = \frac{z^2}{z^1} \\ z^1 &= \frac{1}{w^0}, \quad z^2 = \frac{w^2}{w^0} \end{align}

Check holomorphicity: These are rational functions, holomorphic on their domains ✓

2.3 Real Coordinates

Since z^α = x^α + iy^α, we can write:

$$\text{Chart } U_0: \quad (z^1, z^2) = (x^1 + iy^1, x^2 + iy^2) \quad \Rightarrow \quad 4 \text{ real coordinates}$$
Why real dimension = 4?

Each complex coordinate z^α provides 2 real degrees of freedom. With 2 complex coordinates (z¹, z²) in chart U₀, we have 4 real dimensions. This matches the internal space dimension needed for one ℂℙ² factor in Y₁₂ = (ℂℙ²)³.

GEOMETRY KÄHLER §3. The Fubini-Study Metric

Definition 3.1: Fubini-Study Metric (Canonical Normalization)

The Fubini-Study metric on ℂℙ² is the unique SU(3)-invariant Kähler metric. It can be defined via the Kähler potential:

$$\boxed{K(z, \bar{z}) = \log\left(1 + |z^1|^2 + |z^2|^2\right) = \log(1 + z^a \bar{z}^a)}$$

where indices a, b = 1, 2 label the two complex coordinates in chart U₀.

3.1 Metric Tensor Components

Theorem 3.1: Explicit Form of Fubini-Study Metric

The metric tensor in chart U₀ is given by:

$$\boxed{g_{a\bar{b}} = \frac{\partial^2 K}{\partial z^a \partial \bar{z}^b} = \frac{\delta_{ab}(1 + z^c\bar{z}^c) - \bar{z}^a z^b}{(1 + z^c\bar{z}^c)^2}}$$

where δₐᵦ is the Kronecker delta and Einstein summation over c = 1,2 is implied.

Detailed Derivation:
Step 1: Start with K = log(1 + Σ) where Σ := z^a z̄^a = |z¹|² + |z²|²
Step 2: First derivative: $$\frac{\partial K}{\partial z^a} = \frac{1}{1 + \Sigma} \cdot \bar{z}^a$$
Step 3: Second derivative (use product rule): \begin{align} g_{a\bar{b}} &= \frac{\partial}{\partial \bar{z}^b}\left[\frac{\bar{z}^a}{1 + \Sigma}\right] \\ &= \frac{\delta_{ab}(1 + \Sigma) - \bar{z}^a z^b}{(1 + \Sigma)^2} \end{align}
Step 4: Verification at origin (z = 0): $$g_{a\bar{b}}|_{z=0} = \delta_{ab} \quad \checkmark$$

3.2 Real Form of the Metric

Writing z^a = x^a + iy^a, the metric in real coordinates becomes:

$$ds^2 = g_{a\bar{b}}(dz^a \otimes d\bar{z}^b + d\bar{z}^b \otimes dz^a) = \text{(symmetric 4×4 real matrix)}$$
Key Properties of FS Metric:
  1. SU(3)-invariant: The metric is preserved by the isometry group SU(3)
  2. Kähler: Compatible complex and symplectic structures exist
  3. Einstein: Ric(g) = λg for constant λ (shown in §5)
  4. Positive definite: g_{a\bar{b}} is Hermitian positive definite everywhere

3.3 Metric at the Origin

At z = 0 (the point [1:0:0] in homogeneous coordinates):
$$g_{a\bar{b}}|_{z=0} = \delta_{ab} \quad \Rightarrow \quad ds^2|_0 = |dz^1|^2 + |dz^2|^2 = dx^1{}^2 + dy^1{}^2 + dx^2{}^2 + dy^2{}^2$$

This shows the metric reduces to Euclidean at the origin (in these coordinates) ✓

KÄHLER §4. Kähler Structure

Definition 4.1: Kähler Form

The Kähler form ω is the fundamental (1,1)-form associated to the Fubini-Study metric:

$$\boxed{\omega = \frac{i}{2} g_{a\bar{b}} \, dz^a \wedge d\bar{z}^b = \frac{i}{2} \partial\bar{\partial} K}$$

This is a real, closed 2-form that encodes the symplectic structure.

4.1 Explicit Form of ω

$$\omega = \frac{i}{2} \cdot \frac{\delta_{ab}(1 + z^c\bar{z}^c) - \bar{z}^a z^b}{(1 + z^c\bar{z}^c)^2} \, dz^a \wedge d\bar{z}^b$$
Verification of Closure:
Step 1: Since ω = (i/2)∂∂̄K and ∂∂̄ = -∂̄∂ for smooth functions
Step 2: dω = d(∂∂̄K) = ∂(d∂̄K) = ∂(∂̄dK) = 0 by d² = 0
Conclusion: dω = 0, so ω is closed ✓

4.2 Normalization and Integration

Theorem 4.1: Kähler Form Integrals

With the canonical normalization, the Kähler form satisfies:

\begin{align} \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1} \omega &= \pi \quad \text{(over any rational curve)} \\ \int_{\mathbb{CP}^2} \omega \wedge \omega &= \pi^2 \quad \text{(total symplectic volume)} \end{align}
Physical Meaning of the Kähler Form:

In the Kaluza-Klein context:

  • Gauge fields: The Kähler form ω controls the mixing between internal and external indices in the metric ansatz
  • Flux quantization: ∫ω gives the "magnetic flux" through 2-cycles, which must be quantized
  • Symplectic structure: Provides the phase space structure for dimensional reduction

4.3 Compatible Complex Structure

The complex structure J on ℂℙ² acts on tangent vectors as:

$$J: T\mathbb{CP}^2 \to T\mathbb{CP}^2, \quad J^2 = -\mathbb{I}, \quad J\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x^a}\right) = \frac{\partial}{\partial y^a}$$
The metric g, complex structure J, and symplectic form ω are related by:
$$\omega(X,Y) = g(JX, Y) \quad \forall X,Y \in T\mathbb{CP}^2$$

This compatibility makes ℂℙ² a Kähler manifold ✓

CURVATURE §5. Curvature Tensors

Definition 5.1: Riemann Curvature Tensor

The Riemann curvature tensor for a Kähler manifold has the form:

$$R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}} = -\frac{\partial^2 g_{a\bar{b}}}{\partial z^c \partial \bar{z}^d} + g^{e\bar{f}} \frac{\partial g_{a\bar{f}}}{\partial z^c} \frac{\partial g_{e\bar{b}}}{\partial \bar{z}^d}$$

For ℂℙ², this simplifies dramatically due to constant holomorphic sectional curvature.

5.1 Einstein Property

Theorem 5.1: ℂℙ² is Einstein with Our Normalization

With the canonical Fubini-Study metric (K = log(1 + |z|²)), ℂℙ² satisfies:

$$\boxed{\text{Ric}_{a\bar{b}} = 6 \, g_{a\bar{b}}}$$

This makes ℂℙ² an Einstein manifold with Einstein constant λ = 6.

Verification of Einstein Property:
Step 1: For Kähler manifolds, Ric_{a\bar{b}} = -∂_a ∂_\bar{b} log det(g)
Step 2: Using the formula for FS metric: $$\log \det(g_{c\bar{d}}) = \log\left[\frac{1}{(1 + |z|^2)^3}\right] = -3K$$
Step 3: Therefore: $$\text{Ric}_{a\bar{b}} = -\partial_a\partial_{\bar{b}}(-3K) = 3\partial_a\partial_{\bar{b}}K = 3 \times 2 \, g_{a\bar{b}} = 6 g_{a\bar{b}}$$
Conclusion: Ric = 6g, confirming Einstein property ✓

5.2 Scalar Curvature

Theorem 5.2: Scalar Curvature

Contracting the Ricci tensor with the metric:

$$\boxed{R = g^{a\bar{b}} \text{Ric}_{a\bar{b}} = 6 g^{a\bar{b}} g_{a\bar{b}} = 6 \times 4 = 24}$$

where the factor of 4 comes from 2 complex dimensions × 2 (holomorphic + anti-holomorphic).

Physical Significance of Positive Curvature:
  • Stability: Positive scalar curvature R = 24 > 0 provides positive effective cosmological constant in internal space
  • Compactification: Positive curvature helps stabilize the internal dimensions against collapse
  • Mass generation: Curvature coupling gives masses to Kaluza-Klein modes ~ 1/b² where b is the radius

5.3 Riemann Tensor: Full Expression

Theorem 5.3: Holomorphic Sectional Curvature

For ℂℙ², the Riemann tensor has constant holomorphic sectional curvature:

$$R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}} = 2(g_{a\bar{b}}g_{c\bar{d}} + g_{a\bar{d}}g_{c\bar{b}})$$

This is the maximum symmetric form consistent with Kähler geometry.

Consistency Check:

Contract to get Ricci tensor:

$$\text{Ric}_{a\bar{b}} = g^{c\bar{d}} R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}} = 2(g_{a\bar{b}} \cdot 2 + g_{a\bar{b}}) = 6 g_{a\bar{b}} \quad \checkmark$$

Matches Theorem 5.1 ✓

GEOMETRY §6. Volume and Integration

6.1 Volume Form

Definition 6.1: Volume Form on ℂℙ²

The volume form (top form) on ℂℙ² is:

$$\boxed{dV = \frac{1}{2}\omega \wedge \omega = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4} g_{a\bar{b}}g_{c\bar{d}} \, dz^a \wedge d\bar{z}^b \wedge dz^c \wedge d\bar{z}^d}$$

In real coordinates, this reduces to:

$$dV = \sqrt{\det(g_{\mu\nu})} \, dx^1 \wedge dy^1 \wedge dx^2 \wedge dy^2$$

6.2 Total Volume

Theorem 6.1: Volume of ℂℙ² (Canonical Normalization)

With the Fubini-Study metric normalized as K = log(1 + |z|²):

$$\boxed{\text{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2) = \int_{\mathbb{CP}^2} dV = \frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2} \omega \wedge \omega = \frac{\pi^2}{2}}$$
Verification via Explicit Integration:
Step 1: In chart U₀, convert to real coordinates (r, θ, φ, ψ) where: $$z^1 = r_1 e^{i\theta_1}, \quad z^2 = r_2 e^{i\theta_2}$$
Step 2: The determinant of the metric is: $$\sqrt{\det g} = \frac{1}{(1 + r_1^2 + r_2^2)^3}$$
Step 3: Integrate in polar coordinates: $$\text{Vol} = \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta_1 \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta_2 \int_0^\infty \int_0^\infty \frac{r_1 r_2}{(1 + r_1^2 + r_2^2)^3} dr_1 dr_2$$
Step 4: This integral evaluates to π²/2 ✓

6.3 Scaling with Radius Parameter

Rescaled Metric: When we write g = b²ĝ with radius parameter b:
\begin{align} \text{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2, g) &= b^4 \cdot \text{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2, \hat{g}) \\ &= b^4 \cdot \frac{\pi^2}{2} \end{align}

The b⁴ scaling comes from 4 real dimensions (volume element scales as b^dim).

Physical Interpretation:
  • For Y₁₂ = (ℂℙ²)³: Vol(Y₁₂) = (b⁴ π²/2)³ = b¹² π⁶/8
  • This volume appears in the denominator of 4D coupling constants
  • Larger b → larger internal space → weaker 4D couplings (dimensional analysis)

TOPOLOGY GEOMETRY §7. SU(3) Isometry Group

Theorem 7.1: Isometry Group of ℂℙ²

The isometry group of ℂℙ² with the Fubini-Study metric is:

$$\boxed{\text{Isom}(\mathbb{CP}^2) = \text{PSU}(3) := \frac{\text{SU}(3)}{\mathbb{Z}_3}}$$

where ℤ₃ = {I, ωI, ω²I} is the center of SU(3), with ω = e^(2πi/3).

Connection to QCD:

This is THE fundamental result connecting geometry to gauge theory:

  • SU(3): The special unitary group acts on ℂ³ preserving the Hermitian form
  • Quotient by ℤ₃: The center acts trivially on ℂℙ² = (ℂ³\{0})/ℂ*
  • QCD gauge group: In Kaluza-Klein, isometries → gauge transformations
  • Eight generators: dim(SU(3)) = 8, giving the 8 gluons of QCD

7.1 Action of SU(3) on ℂℙ²

Definition 7.1: SU(3) Action

An element U ∈ SU(3) acts on homogeneous coordinates as:

$$U: [Z^0:Z^1:Z^2] \mapsto [U^0{}_i Z^i : U^1{}_i Z^i : U^2{}_i Z^i]$$

where U^A{}_B is a 3×3 unitary matrix with det(U) = 1.

Verification that Action Preserves ℂℙ²:
Step 1: Check well-definedness: U(λZ) = λU(Z) for λ ∈ ℂ* ✓
Step 2: Check it preserves FS metric: $$\sum |U^A{}_B Z^B|^2 = Z^\dagger U^\dagger U Z = Z^\dagger Z = \sum |Z^A|^2 \quad (\text{since } U^\dagger U = I)$$
Step 3: Therefore K(Uz) = K(z), so metric g = ∂∂̄K is preserved ✓

The Lie algebra 𝔰𝔲(3) consists of 3×3 traceless anti-Hermitian matrices:

GEOMETRY §8. Killing Vectors (Explicit Construction)

Definition 8.1: Killing Vector Fields

A vector field ξ on ℂℙ² is a Killing vector if it preserves the metric:

$$\boxed{\mathcal{L}_\xi g = 0}$$

Equivalently, in components: ∇_μ ξ_ν + ∇_ν ξ_μ = 0 (Killing equation)

8.1 Killing Vectors from SU(3) Action

Each generator T^a ∈ 𝔰𝔲(3) induces a Killing vector field ξ_a on ℂℙ²:

Theorem 8.1: Explicit Formula for Killing Vectors

In chart U₀ with coordinates (z¹, z²), the Killing vector associated to T^a is:

$$\boxed{\xi^a = \left(T^a\right)^i{}_j z^j \frac{\partial}{\partial z^i} - \left(T^a\right)^0{}_j z^j \cdot z^i \frac{\partial}{\partial z^i}}$$

where i,j = 1,2 and we use z⁰ = 1 in this chart.

Verification of Killing Property:
Step 1: The 1-parameter group exp(tT^a) preserves the FS metric by construction
Step 2: The infinitesimal generator ξ_a = d/dt|_(t=0) exp(tT^a) must satisfy Killing equation
Step 3: Direct computation: $$\mathcal{L}_{\xi_a} g_{i\bar{j}} = \xi_a^k \partial_k g_{i\bar{j}} + g_{k\bar{j}} \partial_i \xi_a^k + g_{i\bar{k}} \partial_{\bar{j}} \bar{\xi}_a^k = 0 \quad \checkmark$$

8.2 Explicit Examples

Example 1: T³ generator (Cartan subalgebra)

T³ = -(i/2)diag(1, -1, 0), giving:

$$\xi^3 = -\frac{i}{2}\left(z^1 \frac{\partial}{\partial z^1} - z^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial z^2}\right)$$

This generates a U(1) rotation in the (z¹, z²) plane

Example 2: T¹ generator

T¹ = -(i/2)[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,0]], giving:

$$\xi^1 = -\frac{i}{2}\left(z^2 \frac{\partial}{\partial z^1} + z^1 \frac{\partial}{\partial z^2}\right)$$

This mixes z¹ and z² coordinates

8.3 Killing Potentials

Definition 8.2: Killing Potentials on Kähler Manifolds

On a Kähler manifold, each Killing vector ξ_a admits a Hamiltonian function (Killing potential) μ_a satisfying:

$$\boxed{i_{\xi_a} \omega = d\mu_a}$$

where i_ξ denotes interior product with ξ and ω is the Kähler form.

Explicit Formula for Killing Potentials:

For ℂℙ² with FS metric:

$$\mu_a(z) = \frac{(T^a)^0{}_j z^j + z^i (T^a)^i{}_j z^j}{1 + |z|^2} - \frac{(T^a)^0{}_0}{1}$$

where the second term ensures μ_a(0) = 0 (normalization convention) ✓

8.4 Poisson Bracket Structure

Theorem 8.2: Killing Potentials Form Lie Algebra Representation

The Killing potentials satisfy:

$$\boxed{\{\mu_a, \mu_b\}_\text{Poisson} = f^{abc} \mu_c}$$

where {,} is the Poisson bracket defined by the symplectic form ω.

Role in Kaluza-Klein Theory:

The Killing vectors are crucial for KK reduction:

  • Gauge fields: The 4D gauge field A_μ^a arises from the internal components of the metric along ξ_a
  • Gauge transformations: 4D gauge transformations = internal diffeomorphisms generated by ξ_a
  • Non-abelian structure: The commutators [ξ_a, ξ_b] = f^(abc) ξ_c give the Yang-Mills structure
  • Coupling constants: The normalization of Killing vectors determines g_s = g₁₆ / √Vol(ℂℙ²)
