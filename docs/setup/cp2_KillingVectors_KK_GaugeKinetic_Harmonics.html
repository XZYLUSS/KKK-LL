Explicit SU(3) Killing vectors on $(\\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})$, KK gauge kinetic matrix, and $(p,q)$ harmonics

Conventions used here match the “unit” Fubini–Study normalization with holomorphic sectional curvature $4$: $$\\mathrm{Ric}(\\hat g)=6\\,\\hat g,\\qquad \\hat R=24.$$ In the affine patch $Z^0\\neq 0$ we set $z^1=Z^1/Z^0$, $z^2=Z^2/Z^0$ and $$\\rho = 1+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2,\\qquad K=\\log\\rho.$$ The FS metric is $g_{a\\bar b}=\\partial_a\\partial_{\\bar b}K$.

0. Geometry setup on the affine patch

On $U_0=\\{Z^0\\neq 0\\}$, define

$$z^1=\\frac{Z^1}{Z^0},\\qquad z^2=\\frac{Z^2}{Z^0},\\qquad \\rho = 1+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2.$$

The FS Kähler potential is $K=\\log\\rho$, hence

$$g_{a\\bar b}=\\partial_a\\partial_{\\bar b}\\log\\rho =\\frac{\\rho\\,\\delta_{a\\bar b}-\\bar z^{\\bar a}z^b}{\\rho^2}, \\qquad a,b\\in\\{1,2\\}.$$

Equivalently, in matrix form

$$g_{a\\bar b}=\\frac1{\\rho^2} \\begin{pmatrix} 1+|z^2|^2 & -\\bar z^{\\bar 1}z^2\\\\ -\\bar z^{\\bar 2}z^1 & 1+|z^1|^2 \\end{pmatrix}, \\qquad \\det(g_{a\\bar b})=\\frac1{\\rho^3}.$$

1. The 8 Killing vectors $K_A$ generating $\\mathfrak{su}(3)$

1.1 Induced vector fields from the projective SU(3) action

Let $T_A=\\lambda_A/2$ be the fundamental SU(3) generators (Gell–Mann matrices $\\lambda_A$), normalized by

$$\\mathrm{Tr}(T_A T_B)=\\frac12\\delta_{AB}.$$

SU(3) acts linearly on homogeneous coordinates $Z=(Z^0,Z^1,Z^2)^T$ via

$$\\delta Z = i\\,\\varepsilon^A T_A Z.$$

On the affine patch $Z^0=1$ (so $Z=(1,z^1,z^2)^T$), the induced infinitesimal transformation of $(z^1,z^2)$ is obtained from $$z^\\alpha=\\frac{Z^\\alpha}{Z^0},\\qquad \\alpha=1,2,$$ so

$$\\delta z^\\alpha = \\frac{\\delta Z^\\alpha}{Z^0}-z^\\alpha\\frac{\\delta Z^0}{Z^0} = i\\varepsilon^A\\Big((T_A Z)^\\alpha - z^\\alpha (T_A Z)^0\\Big).$$

This defines holomorphic vector fields

$$k_A = k_A^1(z)\\,\\partial_{z^1}+k_A^2(z)\\,\\partial_{z^2},\\qquad \\delta z^\\alpha=\\varepsilon^A k_A^\\alpha(z).$$

The associated real Killing vectors on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ are

$$K_A = k_A^\\alpha\\partial_{z^\\alpha}+\\bar k_A^{\\bar\\alpha}\\partial_{\\bar z^{\\bar\\alpha}}.$$

2. Reduction of the Einstein term and the exact gauge kinetic matrix

2.1 KK metric ansatz on $M_4\\times \\mathbb{CP}^2$

Let $\\hat g_{mn}(y)$ be the unit FS metric on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ and introduce a breathing radius $b$:

$$g_{mn}(y)=b^2\\hat g_{mn}(y).$$

Use the standard “isometry gauging” ansatz

$$ds^2 = g_{\\mu\\nu}(x)dx^\\mu dx^\\nu + b^2\\hat g_{mn}(y)\\big(dy^m + K_A^m(y)A^A_\\mu(x)dx^\\mu\\big) \\big(dy^n + K_B^n(y)A^B_\\nu(x)dx^\\nu\\big).$$

The higher-dimensional Einstein–Hilbert action is

$$S_{EH}=\\frac{1}{2\\kappa_D^2}\\int d^4x\\,d^4y\\,\\sqrt{-G}\\,R[G],\\qquad D=8.$$

2.2 Extracting the $F^2$ term

Expanding $R[G]$ to quadratic order in $A$ (equivalently in $F=dA+AA$) gives a universal Yang–Mills term

$$S_{EH}\\supset -\\frac14\\int d^4x\\,\\sqrt{-g_4}\\; \\Big(\\frac{1}{g_4^2}\\,\\delta_{AB}\\Big) F^A_{\\mu\\nu}F^{B\\mu\\nu},$$

with gauge kinetic matrix

$$\\boxed{ \\frac{1}{g_4^2}\\,\\delta_{AB} =\\frac{b^{6}}{2\\kappa_8^2} \\int_{\\mathbb{CP}^2} d^4y\\,\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\hat g_{mn}\\,K_A^mK_B^n }\\qquad (\\text{for }M_4\\times \\mathbb{CP}^2).$$
Here $\\sqrt{-G}=\\sqrt{-g_4}\\;b^4\\sqrt{\\hat g}$ and $\\hat g_{mn}$ is dimensionless. The factor $b^6=b^{4+2}$ is the standard $b^n$ from the measure times $b^2$ from $g_{mn}$ in $K_A\\cdot K_B$.

2.3 Evaluation of the internal integral: $\\int \\sqrt{\\hat g}\\,\\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n$

By SU(3)-invariance, the integral must be proportional to $\\delta_{AB}$:

$$\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\,\\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n=C\\,\\delta_{AB}.$$

We compute $C$ using Killing potentials (“moment maps”) $\\mu_A$ defined by

$$\\iota_{K_A}\\omega = d\\mu_A,$$

where $\\omega$ is the FS Kähler form. On a Kähler manifold this implies

$$K_A^m = J^{mn}\\partial_n\\mu_A, \\qquad \\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n = \\hat g^{mn}\\partial_m\\mu_A\\partial_n\\mu_B.$$

Integrating by parts gives

$$\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n =\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\partial_m\\mu_A\\partial^m\\mu_B = -\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\mu_A\\,\\hat\\Delta\\mu_B.$$

For $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ with our FS normalization, the moment maps $\\mu_A$ are scalar harmonics in the adjoint representation, i.e. the $k=1$ family, and satisfy the Laplacian eigenvalue equation

$$\\hat\\Delta\\mu_A = 12\\,\\mu_A.$$

Hence

$$\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n =12\\int\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\mu_A\\mu_B.$$

Now compute $\\int \\mu_A\\mu_B$ purely by SU(3)-invariance. Represent points of $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ by rank-1 projectors

$$P=\\frac{ZZ^{\\dagger}}{Z^{\\dagger}Z},\\qquad P^2=P,\\qquad \\mathrm{Tr}(P)=1.$$

Define

$$\\mu_A = \\mathrm{Tr}(T_A P).$$

Using the Haar-induced (FS) invariant measure on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$, one has the universal second-moment identity for $d=3$:

$$\\int_{\\mathbb{CP}^2}\\! d\\mathrm{vol}\\; P_{ij}P_{kl} = \\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}(\\mathbb{CP}^2)}{3\\cdot 4}\\Big(\\delta_{ij}\\delta_{kl}+\\delta_{il}\\delta_{kj}\\Big) =\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}}{12}(\\delta_{ij}\\delta_{kl}+\\delta_{il}\\delta_{kj}).$$

Contracting with $(T_A)_{ji}(T_B)_{lk}$ gives

$$\\int \\mu_A\\mu_B =\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}}{12}\\,\\mathrm{Tr}(T_AT_B) =\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}}{24}\\,\\delta_{AB}.$$

Therefore

$$\\boxed{ \\int_{\\mathbb{CP}^2}\\!\\sqrt{\\hat g}\\;\\hat g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n =12\\cdot\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}}{24}\\,\\delta_{AB} =\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}(\\mathbb{CP}^2)}{2}\\,\\delta_{AB}. }$$
Final gauge kinetic matrix (exact): $$\\boxed{ \\frac{1}{g_4^2}\\,\\delta_{AB} =\\frac{b^6}{2\\kappa_8^2}\\cdot\\frac{\\mathrm{Vol}(\\mathbb{CP}^2)}{2}\\,\\delta_{AB} =\\frac{b^6\\,\\mathrm{Vol}(\\mathbb{CP}^2)}{4\\kappa_8^2}\\,\\delta_{AB}.}$$

3. KK harmonics as $(p,q)$ SU(3) representations

3.1 The coset viewpoint and representation theory

As a homogeneous space,

$$\\mathbb{CP}^2 \\cong \\frac{\\mathrm{SU}(3)}{\\mathrm{U}(2)}.$$

The scalar KK harmonics are functions on the coset: $\\mathcal{H}=L^2(\\mathrm{SU}(3)/\\mathrm{U}(2))$. By Peter–Weyl / harmonic analysis on cosets, $\\mathcal{H}$ decomposes under the left action of SU(3) into irreducibles $V_{(p,q)}$ with multiplicity equal to the number of $\\mathrm{U}(2)$-singlets inside $V_{(p,q)}$.

Key fact: an SU(3) irrep $V_{(p,q)}$ contains a U(2) singlet iff $p=q=k$. Therefore the scalar harmonics furnish precisely the family

$$\\boxed{\\text{scalar harmonics on }\\mathbb{CP}^2:\\quad (p,q)=(k,k),\\qquad k=0,1,2,\\dots}$$

The dimension of an SU(3) irrep is

$$\\dim(p,q)=\\frac12(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2),$$

hence for $(k,k)$:

$$\\boxed{\\dim(k,k)=(k+1)^3.}$$

3.2 Explicit construction by homogeneous polynomials (fully concrete)

Let $Z^i$ ($i=0,1,2$) be homogeneous coordinates on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$. A scalar function on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ must be invariant under $Z\\sim\\lambda Z$, $\\lambda\\in\\mathbb{C}^*$. Hence a natural basis of SU(3)-covariant functions is built from equal-degree polynomials in $Z$ and $\\bar Z$:

$$\\widetilde Y^{(k)}{}_{i_1\\cdots i_k}{}^{\\bar j_1\\cdots \\bar j_k}(Z,\\bar Z) \\;\\propto\\; \\frac{Z^{i_1}\\cdots Z^{i_k}\\,\\bar Z^{\\bar j_1}\\cdots\\bar Z^{\\bar j_k}}{(Z^{\\dagger}Z)^k}.$$

Project onto the irreducible, symmetric-traceless part in the indices $(i_1\\cdots i_k)$ and $(\\bar j_1\\cdots\\bar j_k)$. This gives an SU(3) irrep with Dynkin labels $(k,k)$.

Check at $k=1$: $(1,1)$ has dimension $8$ and is the adjoint of SU(3). The corresponding $k=1$ scalars are precisely the moment maps $\\mu_A$ (Killing potentials).

3.3 Laplacian eigenvalues via SU(3) Casimir (explicit)

On a compact symmetric space $G/H$, the scalar Laplacian eigenvalues on harmonics in irrep $V_{(p,q)}$ are proportional to the quadratic Casimir $C_2(p,q)$. For SU(3),

$$C_2(p,q)=\\frac13\\Big(p^2+q^2+pq+3p+3q\\Big).$$

For scalars on $\\mathbb{CP}^2$ we have $(p,q)=(k,k)$, hence

$$C_2(k,k)=\\frac13(3k^2+6k)=k(k+2).$$

With FS normalization (holomorphic sectional curvature $4$), the Laplacian eigenvalue is

$$\\boxed{\\lambda_k = 4\\,C_2(k,k)=4k(k+2).}$$

So the KK masses for a breathing radius $b$ are

$$m_k^2 \\sim \\frac{\\lambda_k}{b^2} = \\frac{4k(k+2)}{b^2},\\qquad k=0,1,2,\\dots$$
