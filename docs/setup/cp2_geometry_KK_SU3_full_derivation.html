0. $\mathbb{CP}^2$: definition, compactness, dimensions

Definition

Complex projective space is the space of complex lines through the origin in $\mathbb{C}^3$:

$$\mathbb{CP}^2 := (\mathbb{C}^3\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{C}^*,$$ $$[Z^0:Z^1:Z^2] \sim [\lambda Z^0: \lambda Z^1: \lambda Z^2],\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb{C}^*.$$

Points are homogeneous coordinates $[Z]$ defined up to nonzero complex rescaling.

Compactness

$\mathbb{CP}^2$ is compact because it is a quotient of the unit sphere $S^5\subset\mathbb{C}^3$ by the free $U(1)$ action (Hopf fibration):

$$S^1 \hookrightarrow S^5 \twoheadrightarrow \mathbb{CP}^2.$$
Dimensions
$$\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{CP}^2 = 2,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{CP}^2 = 4.$$
Simply connected
$$\pi_1(\mathbb{CP}^2)=0.$$

So charge quantization is not from $\pi_1$ (unlike $S^1$ momentum quantization). It comes from representation theory and/or flux topology.

1. Local coordinates (charts) and the Kahler potential

Affine chart $U_0$

On the patch $U_0 = \{Z^0\neq 0\}$ define affine coordinates

$$z^1=\frac{Z^1}{Z^0},\qquad z^2=\frac{Z^2}{Z^0}.$$

Other patches $U_1, U_2$ are obtained by permuting indices. The FS metric is globally well-defined because the transition functions are holomorphic.

Kahler potential

Define $$\rho := 1+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2.$$ The (unit) Fubini--Study Kahler potential is

$$K(z,\bar z) := \log\rho.$$
First derivatives

Compute the basic building blocks explicitly:

$$\partial_{a} \rho = \bar z^{\bar a},\qquad \partial_{\bar b}\rho = z^b.$$ $$\partial_a K = \frac{\partial_a\rho}{\rho} = \frac{\bar z^{\bar a}}{\rho},\qquad \partial_{\bar b}K = \frac{z^b}{\rho}.$$

Here $a,b\in\{1,2\}$ label the complex coordinates.

Second derivatives

Using $\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K = \partial_a\left(\dfrac{z^b}{\rho}\right)$:

$$\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K = \frac{\delta_a^{\ b}}{\rho} - \frac{z^b\,\bar z^{\bar a}}{\rho^2} = \frac{\rho\,\delta_a^{\ b} - z^b\bar z^{\bar a}}{\rho^2}.$$

2. Fubini--Study metric: explicit components

Metric definition

The Kahler metric is $$g_{a\bar b} = \partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K.$$ Using the result above:

$$\boxed{\;g_{a\bar b}(z,\bar z)=\frac{\rho\,\delta_{a\bar b}-\bar z_{\bar b} z_a}{\rho^2}.\;}$$
Matrix form (for $\mathbb{CP}^2$)

Writing $z=(z^1,z^2)$ with $|z|^2=|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2$:

$$g_{a\bar b}=\frac{1}{\rho^2} \begin{pmatrix} \rho-|z^1|^2 & -\bar z^{\bar 1} z^2\\ -\bar z^{\bar 2} z^1 & \rho-|z^2|^2 \end{pmatrix} =\frac{1}{(1+|z|^2)^2} \begin{pmatrix} 1+|z^2|^2 & -\bar z^{\bar 1} z^2\\ -\bar z^{\bar 2} z^1 & 1+|z^1|^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Inverse metric (explicit)

For later use, we compute $g^{a\bar b}$. Using the rank-1 update structure $g = \rho^{-1}(I) - \rho^{-2} (\bar z\otimes z)$, one finds

$$\boxed{\;g^{a\bar b}=\rho\,\left(\delta^{a\bar b}+ z^a\bar z^{\bar b}\right).\;}$$

You can verify directly that $g_{a\bar c}g^{c\bar b}=\delta_a^{\ b}$.

Scaling by a radius $b$

In compactification one introduces a breathing mode $b$ and uses

$$g_{mn} = b^2\,\hat g_{mn},$$ $$g_{a\bar b}=b^2\,\hat g_{a\bar b}.$$

All curvature scalars scale as $R\sim b^{-2}$ while volumes scale as $\mathrm{Vol}\sim b^4$.

3. Determinant, volume form, and total volume

Determinant of $g_{a\bar b}$

We compute $\det(g_{a\bar b})$ explicitly using the matrix determinant lemma. Write $$g = \frac{1}{\rho}\Big(I - \frac{1}{\rho}\bar z\otimes z\Big).$$ Then $$\det(g) = \rho^{-2}\,\det\Big(I - \rho^{-1}\bar z\otimes z\Big).$$ For a rank-1 update, $\det(I+u v^T)=1+v^T u$. Here $u=-\rho^{-1}\bar z$ and $v^T=z^T$.

$$\det\Big(I - \rho^{-1}\bar z\otimes z\Big)=1-\rho^{-1}(z\cdot\bar z)=1-\frac{|z|^2}{\rho}=\frac{1}{\rho}.$$ $$\boxed{\;\det(g_{a\bar b}) = \rho^{-3} = (1+|z|^2)^{-3}.\;}$$
Local volume form

In complex coordinates, the Riemannian volume form is

$$d\mathrm{vol}_4 = \det(g_{a\bar b})\, d^2z^1\,d^2z^2,$$ where $d^2z^a:=d(\mathrm{Re}\,z^a)\,d(\mathrm{Im}\,z^a)$. Thus $$\boxed{\;d\mathrm{vol}_4 = \frac{d^2z^1\,d^2z^2}{(1+|z|^2)^3}.\;}$$
Volume of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ (FS normalization)

A standard fact (and a good consistency check) is that the FS volume is

$$\boxed{\;\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2,\hat g_{FS}) = \frac{\pi^2}{2}.\;}$$

With breathing mode $b$, $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2,g=b^2\hat g)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}b^4$.

Explicit volume integral check

Using the local measure, one can verify the total volume directly in the affine chart. Write $r^2:=|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2$, so $\rho=1+r^2$. In $\mathbb{R}^4\cong\mathbb{C}^2$ we use spherical coordinates. The area of the unit $3$-sphere is $\mathrm{Area}(S^3)=2\pi^2$, hence

$$d^2z^1\,d^2z^2 = d^4x = \mathrm{Area}(S^3)\,r^3dr = 2\pi^2\,r^3dr.$$ $$\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^2}\frac{d^4x}{(1+r^2)^3} =2\pi^2\int_0^{\infty}\frac{r^3}{(1+r^2)^3}\,dr.$$ Substitute $u=r^2$ so $du=2r\,dr$ and $r^3dr=\frac{u}{2}\,du$: $$\mathrm{Vol}=2\pi^2\int_0^{\infty}\frac{r^3}{(1+r^2)^3}\,dr =\pi^2\int_0^{\infty}\frac{u}{(1+u)^3}\,du.$$ Now let $w=1+u$: $$\int_0^{\infty}\frac{u}{(1+u)^3}\,du =\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{w-1}{w^3}\,dw =\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{w^2}-\frac{1}{w^3}\right)dw =\left[-\frac{1}{w}+\frac{1}{2w^2}\right]_{1}^{\infty}=\frac12.$$ Hence $$\boxed{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}.}$$
Derivation via Kahler form

For any Kahler manifold of complex dimension $n$ with Kahler form $\omega$, the volume form satisfies

$$d\mathrm{vol}_{2n} = \frac{\omega^n}{n!}.$$

For $n=2$:

$$\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2} \frac{\omega\wedge\omega}{2}.$$

4. Kahler form, periods, and flux/topology quantization

Kahler form

The Kahler 2-form is

$$\boxed{\;\omega = i\, g_{a\bar b}\, dz^a\wedge d\bar z^{\bar b}.\;}$$
Closure

Because $g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K$:

$$\omega = i\,\partial\bar\partial K,$$ hence $$\boxed{\;d\omega = 0.\;}$$
Integral cohomology

The key topological input for quantization is

$$H^2(\mathbb{CP}^2,\mathbb{Z})\cong \mathbb{Z},\qquad H:=\text{hyperplane class},\qquad \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}H=1.$$
Normalization dictionary (important)

There are two very common normalizations for the Kahler form, related by an overall scale. Both are correct; they just correspond to different metric radii.

FS metric used in your corrected integrated invariants

$$\boxed{\;[\omega/\pi]=H,\qquad \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega=\pi,\qquad \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}.\;}$$

This matches $\mathrm{Vol}(Y=(\mathbb{CP}^2)^3)=\pi^6/8$ and the coefficients used in your corrected $G_6$ component algebra.

Cohomology-period normalization often used for flux quantization

$$\boxed{\;[\omega'/2\pi]=H,\qquad \omega' = 2\,\omega,\qquad \int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega'=2\pi.\;}$$ $$\mathrm{Vol}'(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\int \frac{\omega'\wedge\omega'}{2} = 4\,\int \frac{\omega\wedge\omega}{2} = 2\pi^2.$$

The geometric physics is invariant: choosing $\omega'$ corresponds to scaling the metric by a constant factor. In KK reductions, such a rescaling is absorbed into the breathing radius $b$.

Flux quantization on $\mathbb{CP}^2$

If a $U(1)$ field strength is proportional to the Kahler form, $$F = f\,\omega,$$ then the Dirac quantization condition reads

$$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}F \in \mathbb{Z} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{f}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega \in\mathbb{Z}.$$

In the FS normalization ($\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\omega=\pi$) this becomes

$$\boxed{\;\frac{f}{2}\in \mathbb{Z}.\;}$$

In the $\omega'$ period normalization ($\int\omega'=2\pi$) one gets the more familiar $f\in\mathbb{Z}$.

5. Curvature: Riemann tensor, Ricci, scalar, and scaling with $b$

Kahler curvature formulas

For a Kahler metric $g_{a\bar b}$, the only nonzero Riemann components are of type $(1,1;1,1)$:

$$R_{a\bar b c\bar d} = -\partial_{\bar b}\partial_a g_{c\bar d} + g^{e\bar f}(\partial_{\bar b}g_{c\bar f})(\partial_a g_{e\bar d}).$$
FS Riemann tensor (explicit closed form)

A direct computation (or recognition that $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is a rank-1 Hermitian symmetric space) gives the remarkably simple identity:

$$\boxed{\;\hat R_{a\bar b c\bar d} = \hat g_{a\bar b}\hat g_{c\bar d} + \hat g_{a\bar d}\hat g_{c\bar b}.\;}$$

This is the standard unit FS normalization in which $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is Einstein and has constant holomorphic sectional curvature.

Ricci tensor (step-by-step contraction)

Contract indices:

$$\hat R_{a\bar b} = \hat g^{c\bar d}\,\hat R_{a\bar b c\bar d} = \hat g^{c\bar d}(\hat g_{a\bar b}\hat g_{c\bar d} + \hat g_{a\bar d}\hat g_{c\bar b}).$$ $$= \hat g_{a\bar b}\underbrace{\hat g^{c\bar d}\hat g_{c\bar d}}_{=n} + \underbrace{\hat g^{c\bar d}\hat g_{a\bar d}}_{=\delta_a^c}\hat g_{c\bar b}$$ $$= n\,\hat g_{a\bar b} + \hat g_{a\bar b} = (n+1)\hat g_{a\bar b}.$$

For $\mathbb{CP}^2$ the complex dimension is $n=2$, hence

$$\boxed{\;\hat R_{a\bar b}=3\,\hat g_{a\bar b}.\;}$$

In real indices $(m,n=1,\dots,4)$ this becomes $\hat R_{mn}=6\,\hat g_{mn}$.

Scalar curvature

Using $\hat R = \hat g^{mn}\hat R_{mn}$ and $\hat g^{mn}\hat g_{mn}=4$:

$$\hat R = 6\,\hat g^{mn}\hat g_{mn} = 6\cdot 4 = 24.$$ $$\boxed{\;\hat R = 24.\;}$$
Scaling with breathing radius $b$

Let $g_{mn}=b^2\hat g_{mn}$. Then:

$$\Gamma[g]=\Gamma[\hat g],\qquad R^p{}_{qmn}[g]=R^p{}_{qmn}[\hat g],$$ $$R_{mn}[g]=\frac{6}{b^2}g_{mn},\qquad R[g]=\frac{24}{b^2}.$$
Final curvature package: $$\boxed{\;\mathrm{Ric}(g)=\frac{6}{b^2}g,\qquad R(g)=\frac{24}{b^2}.\;}$$
Integrated invariants (used in your Lovelock reductions)

With $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}b^4$:

$$I_0(b)=\int\sqrt{g}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}b^4,$$ $$I_1(b)=\int\sqrt{g}\,R = \left(\frac{24}{b^2}\right)\left(\frac{\pi^2}{2}b^4\right)=12\pi^2 b^2.$$

For the 4D Gauss--Bonnet density (Euler class), use $\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2)=3$:

$$I_2=\int\sqrt{g}\,\mathcal{G}=32\pi^2\chi(\mathbb{CP}^2)=96\pi^2.$$

6. Isometries: why the symmetry algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$

SU(3) action on homogeneous coordinates

Let $U\in SU(3)$ act linearly on $Z\in\mathbb{C}^3$:

$$Z\mapsto UZ.$$

This respects projective equivalence $Z\sim \lambda Z$, hence defines a well-defined action on $\mathbb{CP}^2$.

Why the FS metric is invariant

Define the global potential in homogeneous variables $$K(Z,\bar Z)=\log(Z^\dagger Z).$$ Under $U\in SU(3)$:

$$Z^\dagger Z \mapsto (UZ)^\dagger(UZ)=Z^\dagger U^\dagger U Z = Z^\dagger Z,$$ hence $K$ is invariant.

Under the projective rescaling $Z\mapsto\lambda Z$:

$$K\mapsto K+\log|\lambda|^2.$$

Adding a holomorphic + antiholomorphic function to $K$ does not change $g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K$. Therefore the metric is globally well-defined and SU(3)-invariant.

Connected isometry group

The full connected isometry group is

$$\mathrm{Isom}_0(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})\cong \mathrm{PSU}(3)=\frac{SU(3)}{\mathbb{Z}_3}.$$

The Lie algebra is

$$\boxed{\;\mathfrak{isom}(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})\cong\mathfrak{su}(3).\;}$$

For KK physics, what matters is the Lie algebra (8 generators, 8 Killing vectors).

Moment map / Killing potentials (explicit)

Choose anti-Hermitian generators $T_A$ of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$, normalized by $$\mathrm{tr}(T_A T_B)=-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{AB}.$$ (Equivalently, $T_A = \frac{i}{2}\lambda_A$ with Gell-Mann matrices $\lambda_A$.)

Define the Killing potentials (components of the moment map)

$$\boxed{\;\mu_A([Z]) := -\frac{Z^\dagger T_A Z}{Z^\dagger Z}.\;}$$

They satisfy the fundamental Hamiltonian identity

$$\boxed{\;d\mu_A = \iota_{K_A}\,\omega,\;}$$

meaning each Killing vector field $K_A$ is the Hamiltonian vector field generated by $\mu_A$ with respect to the symplectic form $\omega$.

Killing vectors in affine coordinates (explicit formula)

On the patch $U_0$ with affine coordinates $z^a=Z^a/Z^0$, an infinitesimal SU(3) transformation $$\delta Z = \epsilon^A T_A Z$$ induces an infinitesimal variation of $z^a$ obtained by differentiating the quotient:

$$\delta z^a = \frac{\delta Z^a}{Z^0} - \frac{Z^a}{(Z^0)^2}\delta Z^0 = \epsilon^A\left((T_A)^a{}_{0} + (T_A)^a{}_{b} z^b - z^a\big((T_A)^0{}_{0} + (T_A)^0{}_{b}z^b\big)\right).$$

Therefore the corresponding Killing vector field is

$$\boxed{\;K_A = (\delta_A z^a)\,\partial_{z^a} + (\delta_A\bar z^{\bar a})\,\partial_{\bar z^{\bar a}},\quad \delta_A z^a:= (T_A)^a{}_{0} + (T_A)^a{}_{b} z^b - z^a\big((T_A)^0{}_{0} + (T_A)^0{}_{b}z^b\big).\;}$$

This is a concrete, coordinate-level construction of all 8 Killing vectors once you pick a basis $T_A$. (The expression is polynomial/rational in $z$.)

Closure of the Lie algebra

Because these vector fields come from a group action,

$$[K_A, K_B] = f_{AB}{}^C K_C,$$

with $f_{AB}{}^C$ exactly the $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ structure constants defined by $$[T_A,T_B]=f_{AB}{}^C T_C.$$

7. Kaluza--Klein: internal isometries become 4D gauge transformations

KK metric ansatz (non-abelian isometry gauging)

Consider an 8D spacetime $M_8 = X_4\times K_4$ with $K_4=\mathbb{CP}^2$. Let $y^m$ be internal coordinates on $K_4$, $x^\mu$ external coordinates on $X_4$. Introduce the 1-forms

$$\eta^m := dy^m + K_A^m(y) A^A_{\mu}(x)\,dx^{\mu}.$$

The KK metric ansatz is

$$\boxed{\;ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu}(x)dx^\mu dx^\nu + g_{mn}(y)\,\eta^m\eta^n.\;}$$
Allowed internal diffeomorphisms

The special diffeomorphisms that preserve the internal metric are the (local) isometries:

$$\delta y^m = -\varepsilon^A(x)\,K_A^m(y).$$

Note the parameter depends on $x$ only (a key KK step): we are gauging the internal rigid symmetry.

Derive the 4D gauge transformation of $A^A_\mu$

We demand that the combination $\eta^m$ transforms covariantly. Compute its variation:

$$\delta \eta^m = d(\delta y^m) + \delta(K_A^m A^A)$$ $$= -d\varepsilon^A\,K_A^m - \varepsilon^A\,dK_A^m + (\delta K_A^m)A^A + K_A^m\,\delta A^A.$$

Use that $\delta K_A^m = -\varepsilon^B [K_B,K_A]^m = -\varepsilon^B f_{BA}{}^C K_C^m$ and that $dK_A^m = (\partial_n K_A^m)dy^n$. Re-express $dy^n = \eta^n - K_B^n A^B$ and require that $\delta\eta^m$ contains no term proportional to $d\varepsilon$. This uniquely fixes $\delta A^A$.

Result (Yang--Mills gauge transformation):
$$\boxed{\;\delta A^A_{\mu} = \partial_{\mu}\varepsilon^A + f_{BC}{}^{A} A^B_{\mu}\varepsilon^C.\;}$$

This is precisely the non-abelian SU(3) gauge transformation law. It is nothing but the statement that gauged internal isometries are 4D gauge symmetry.

Field strength

The corresponding curvature 2-form is

$$\boxed{\;F^A = dA^A + \frac{1}{2}f_{BC}{}^{A}A^B\wedge A^C,\quad F^A_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A^A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A^A_{\mu}+f_{BC}{}^{A}A^B_{\mu}A^C_{\nu}.\;}$$
Gauge kinetic term from higher-dimensional Einstein--Hilbert

Start with 8D pure gravity $$S_{EH} = \frac{1}{2\kappa_8^2}\int d^8x\sqrt{-G}\,R[G].$$ Plugging the KK ansatz and expanding the curvature gives (among other terms) the gauge kinetic piece

$$S_{YM} = -\frac{1}{4\kappa_8^2}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g_4}\,\Big(\mathcal{K}_{AB}\,F^A_{\mu\nu}F^{B\,\mu\nu}\Big),$$ $$\boxed{\;\mathcal{K}_{AB}:=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2} d^4y\sqrt{g_4}\,g_{mn}K_A^m K_B^n.\;}$$

By homogeneity of $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and SU(3) invariance, $\mathcal{K}_{AB}$ must be proportional to $\delta_{AB}$:

$$\mathcal{K}_{AB} = k_{\mathrm{geom}}\,\delta_{AB}.$$

The numerical constant $k_{\mathrm{geom}}$ depends on the chosen normalization of $T_A$ and hence of $K_A$. The scaling with radius is unambiguous.

Scaling of the 4D gauge coupling with $b$ (Jordan frame)

Under $g_{mn}=b^2\hat g_{mn}$, the integrand scales as $$\sqrt{g}\,g_{mn}K_A^mK_B^n \sim (b^4)(b^2)(\text{dimensionless}) = b^6.$$ Thus

$$\mathcal{K}_{AB}(b)=b^6\,\mathcal{K}_{AB}(1),$$ and the Jordan-frame coupling scales as $$\boxed{\;\frac{1}{g_{4,J}^2}\propto \frac{b^6}{\kappa_8^2}.\;}$$

In 4D Einstein frame one further rescales $g_{\mu\nu}$ to absorb internal volume factors; the final power of $b$ can change accordingly. What never changes: the gauge sector is fixed by the Killing algebra and internal geometry.

8. Your physical intuition: what holds, what is subtle, what does not follow

(I) The main KK intuition is correct

Correct statement: internal diffeomorphisms along Killing directions of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})$ become 4D gauge transformations. Since $$\mathfrak{isom}(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})\cong\mathfrak{su}(3),$$ the emergent 4D gauge algebra is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$.

(II) Compactness implies discrete spectra and discrete SU(3) representation sectors

Because $\mathbb{CP}^2$ is compact, the Laplacian has a discrete spectrum: $$-\Delta\,Y_{\lambda}=\lambda Y_{\lambda},$$ giving a KK mass tower $m^2\sim\lambda/b^2$. Moreover, harmonics on a homogeneous space arrange into finite-dimensional irreps of SU(3), with discrete highest-weight labels $(p,q)$.

(III) Compactness does NOT directly quantize glueball masses

Glueball masses are controlled by 4D confinement: $$m_{\mathrm{glue}}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}},$$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ is generated by 4D running of the gauge coupling (dimensional transmutation), not by KK spectral discreteness.

Geometry can still matter hugely: internal volume and normalization determine $g_4$ at the compactification scale, and thus set $\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}$ exponentially. But this is not a simple topological quantization of glueball masses.

Correct distilled conclusion:
$$\boxed{\;\text{FS }\mathbb{CP}^2 \Rightarrow \mathfrak{su}(3)\ \text{KK gauge algebra, and compactness }\Rightarrow\text{discrete KK/rep sectors.}\;}$$
