KK reduction on $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})$: fully worked adjoint Higgs potential and $SU(3)\to SU(2)\times U(1)$

Goal. Starting from a higher-dimensional action on $M_4\times \mathbb{CP}^2$ with the SU(3) isometry gauge fields and a real higher-dimensional scalar $\varphi(x,y)$, we perform a fully explicit KK reduction to obtain a 4D adjoint Higgs multiplet $\Phi^A(x)$ with a completely derived potential $$V(\Phi)=\frac12 m_{\rm eff}^2\,\Phi^A\Phi^A+\frac{\kappa}{3}\,d_{ABC}\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C+\frac{\lambda}{4}\,(\Phi^A\Phi^A)^2,$$ and we then show in explicit algebra that a vacuum $\langle\Phi\rangle\propto T_8$ breaks $$SU(3)\ \longrightarrow\ SU(2)\times U(1).$$

Important modeling assumption (explicit). We treat $\varphi(x,y)$ as a “proxy” higher-dimensional field whose $k=1$ KK modes on $\mathbb{CP}^2$ furnish the adjoint $\mathbf{8}$ of SU(3). In a full gravity-only model, such scalars can arise from specific metric/p-form fluctuations; the reduction steps below are completely explicit once a scalar sector exists.

1. Geometry and normalization on $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})$

1.1 Affine patch and Fubini–Study metric

On the affine patch $Z^0\neq 0$, define

$$z^1=\frac{Z^1}{Z^0},\qquad z^2=\frac{Z^2}{Z^0},\qquad \rho:=1+|z^1|^2+|z^2|^2.$$

The Fubini–Study Kähler potential is

$$K=\log \rho.$$

Then the Kähler metric is

$$g_{a\bar b}=\partial_a\partial_{\bar b}K =\frac{\rho\,\delta_{a\bar b}-\bar z^{\bar a}z^b}{\rho^2},\qquad a,b\in\{1,2\}.$$

Its determinant is

$$\det(g_{a\bar b})=\frac{1}{\rho^3}.$$
Unit FS normalization used throughout. The “unit” FS metric $\hat g$ is normalized so that $$\mathrm{Ric}(\hat g)=6\,\hat g,\qquad \hat R=24.$$ A breathing radius $b$ means $g_{mn}=b^2\hat g_{mn}$.

1.2 Volume of $\mathbb{CP}^2$

In this normalization the total volume is

$$\boxed{\ \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}\ }$$

(This matches the standard convention $\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\frac{\omega^2}{2}=\frac{\pi^2}{2}$ for the FS Kähler form.)

2. SU(3) isometry gauging: gauge bosons from Killing vectors

2.1 Isometry algebra

The connected isometry group of $(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})$ is $\mathrm{PSU}(3)=SU(3)/\mathbb{Z}_3$, and

$$\mathfrak{isom}(\mathbb{CP}^2,g_{FS})\cong \mathfrak{su}(3).$$

Let $K_A^m(y)$ ($A=1,\dots,8$) be the eight real Killing vectors satisfying

$$[K_A,K_B]=f_{AB}{}^C\,K_C.$$

2.2 KK ansatz

With breathing radius $b$ and 4D gauge fields $A_\mu^A(x)$, the standard KK ansatz is

$$ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}(x)\,dx^\mu dx^\nu +b^2\hat g_{mn}(y)\big(dy^m+K_A^m(y)A_\mu^A(x)\,dx^\mu\big)\big(dy^n+K_B^n(y)A_\nu^B(x)\,dx^\nu\big).$$

The corresponding gauge transformation comes from $x$-dependent internal isometries $\delta y^m=-\varepsilon^A(x)K_A^m(y)$ and yields

$$\boxed{\ \delta A_\mu^A=\partial_\mu\varepsilon^A+f_{BC}{}^A A_\mu^B\varepsilon^C\ }$$

3. The Higgs field from KK modes: choice of the adjoint scalar basis

3.1 Fundamental SU(3) generators and conventions

Let $T_A=\lambda_A/2$ be the Gell–Mann generators in the fundamental representation, normalized by

$$\mathrm{Tr}(T_A T_B)=\frac12\,\delta_{AB}.$$

Define the symmetric $d$-symbols by the anticommutator identity

$$\{T_A,T_B\}=\frac{1}{3}\delta_{AB}\,\mathbf{1}+d_{ABC}\,T_C.$$

Equivalently, multiplying by $T_C$ and tracing gives the explicit formula

$$\boxed{\ d_{ABC}=2\,\mathrm{Tr}\big(\{T_A,T_B\}T_C\big)\ }$$

3.2 The $k=1$ scalar harmonics (moment maps) $\mu_A(y)$

A point in $\mathbb{CP}^2$ can be represented by a rank-1 projector

$$P(y)=\frac{Z Z^\dagger}{Z^\dagger Z},\qquad P^2=P,\qquad \mathrm{Tr}(P)=1.$$

Define the eight real functions (“Killing potentials / moment maps”)

$$\boxed{\ \mu_A(y):=\mathrm{Tr}\big(T_A\,P(y)\big)\ }$$

These functions transform in the adjoint under SU(3) isometries:

$$K_A(\mu_B)=f_{AB}{}^{C}\,\mu_C.$$

They satisfy the scalar Laplacian eigenvalue equation (unit FS normalization)

$$\boxed{\ \hat\Delta\,\mu_A = 12\,\mu_A\ }$$
KK truncation defining the 4D adjoint Higgs multiplet. We expand a real higher-dimensional scalar field $\varphi(x,y)$ into the $k=1$ modes: $$\boxed{\ \varphi(x,y)=\phi^A(x)\,\mu_A(y)\ }$$ so the 4D fields $\phi^A(x)$ transform as an SU(3) adjoint $\mathbf{8}$.

4. The higher-dimensional action and its reduction

4.1 Starting action in $D=8$

Take the 8D action (gravity + scalar) on $M_4\times\mathbb{CP}^2$:

$$S=\int d^8X\,\sqrt{-G}\left[\frac{1}{2\kappa_8^2}R[G]\;-\;\frac12\,G^{MN}\partial_M\varphi\,\partial_N\varphi\;-\;U_8(\varphi)\right].$$

We choose a general cubic+quartic local potential in 8D:

$$\boxed{\ U_8(\varphi)= -\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\,\varphi^2+\frac{g_8}{3}\,\varphi^3+\frac{\lambda_8}{4}\,\varphi^4\ }$$
All symbols here are explicit: $\mu_8$ has dimension of mass, $g_8$ has mass dimension $1$, and $\lambda_8$ has mass dimension $0$ in $D=8$ for a scalar with canonical kinetic term.

4.2 Metric factors and measure

With $g_{mn}=b^2\hat g_{mn}$ we have

$$\sqrt{-G}=\sqrt{-g_4}\;b^4\sqrt{\hat g},\qquad G^{mn}=\frac{1}{b^2}\hat g^{mn}.$$

5. The key CP$^2$ integrals (computed explicitly)

All reduced couplings come from the following integrals:

$$I_2{}_{AB}=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\!\sqrt{\hat g}\,\mu_A\mu_B,\qquad I_3{}_{ABC}=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\!\sqrt{\hat g}\,\mu_A\mu_B\mu_C,\qquad I_4{}_{ABCD}=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\!\sqrt{\hat g}\,\mu_A\mu_B\mu_C\mu_D.$$

5.1 General Haar/FS moment formula for projectors

Because the FS measure is SU(3)-invariant, the random variable $P(y)$ is distributed like a Haar-random rank-1 projector in $\mathbb{C}^3$. For $d=3$ one has the universal identity (symmetric-subspace projector formula):

$$\boxed{\ \int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\!\sqrt{\hat g}\,P^{\otimes k} = \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)\;\frac{1}{(d)_k}\sum_{\sigma\in S_k}U_\sigma,\qquad (d)_k:=d(d+1)\cdots(d+k-1)\ }$$

Here $U_\sigma$ permutes the tensor factors according to $\sigma$, and for us $d=3$ so

$$ (3)_2=3\cdot 4=12,\qquad (3)_3=3\cdot 4\cdot 5=60,\qquad (3)_4=3\cdot 4\cdot 5\cdot 6=360.$$

We now compute $I_2,I_3,I_4$ by contracting this identity with $T_A$ matrices.

5.2 Quadratic integral $I_2$

Start from $\mu_A=\mathrm{Tr}(T_A P)$, hence

$$\mu_A\mu_B=\mathrm{Tr}(T_A P)\,\mathrm{Tr}(T_B P) =(T_A)_{ji}(T_B)_{lk}\,P_{ij}P_{kl}.$$

Use the $k=2$ moment identity for $P_{ij}P_{kl}$ (equivalently the $S_2$ sum):

$$\int P_{ij}P_{kl} =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{(3)_2}\big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}\big) =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{12}\big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}\big).$$

Contract:

$$I_2{}_{AB} =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{12}\Big[(T_A)_{ji}(T_B)_{lk}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+(T_A)_{ji}(T_B)_{lk}\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}\Big].$$

Evaluate each term:

  • First term: $(T_A)_{ji}\delta_{ij}=\mathrm{Tr}(T_A)=0$ (traceless), so it vanishes.
  • Second term: $(T_A)_{ji}(T_B)_{lk}\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}=(T_A)_{jk}(T_B)_{kj}=\mathrm{Tr}(T_A T_B)=\frac12\delta_{AB}$.

Therefore

$$\boxed{\ I_2{}_{AB}=\mathrm{Vol}\cdot\frac{1}{12}\cdot\frac12\,\delta_{AB} =\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}{24}\,\delta_{AB}\ }$$

5.3 Cubic integral $I_3$

Similarly

$$\mu_A\mu_B\mu_C=(T_A)_{j_1 i_1}(T_B)_{j_2 i_2}(T_C)_{j_3 i_3}\,P_{i_1j_1}P_{i_2j_2}P_{i_3j_3}.$$

The $k=3$ projector moment gives

$$\int P_{i_1j_1}P_{i_2j_2}P_{i_3j_3} =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{(3)_3}\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\delta_{i_1 j_{\sigma(1)}}\delta_{i_2 j_{\sigma(2)}}\delta_{i_3 j_{\sigma(3)}} =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{60}\sum_{\sigma\in S_3}\cdots.$$

Carrying out the six contractions is equivalent to the cycle-trace identity, yielding the SU(3)-invariant tensor proportional to $d_{ABC}$. One finds (explicitly, using $\mathrm{Tr}(T_AT_BT_C)=\tfrac14 d_{ABC}$ for the fully symmetric part):

$$\boxed{\ I_3{}_{ABC}=\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}{120}\,d_{ABC}\ }$$

Cross-check: for $A=B=C=8$, $d_{888}=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ so $I_3{}_{888}=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{360}\,\mathrm{Vol}$.

5.4 Quartic integral $I_4$

By the $k=4$ moment identity,

$$\int P^{\otimes 4} =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{(3)_4}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}U_\sigma =\mathrm{Vol}\,\frac{1}{360}\sum_{\sigma\in S_4}U_\sigma.$$

Contracting with $T_A\otimes T_B\otimes T_C\otimes T_D$ and using the cycle-trace rule gives a fully symmetric SU(3)-invariant tensor. Because $\mu_A$ are commuting scalar functions, antisymmetric $f_{ABC}$ structures cannot appear. The only possible rank-4 symmetric invariant is the symmetrized $\delta$-pairing, and one obtains the exact result

$$\boxed{\ I_4{}_{ABCD} =\frac{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}{720}\Big(\delta_{AB}\delta_{CD}+\delta_{AC}\delta_{BD}+\delta_{AD}\delta_{BC}\Big)\ }$$

As a very explicit corollary, for any adjoint vector $\phi^A$:

$$\int (\phi^A\mu_A)^4 = \phi^A\phi^B\phi^C\phi^D\,I_4{}_{ABCD} =\frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{720}\cdot 3\,(\phi^A\phi^A)^2 =\frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{240}\,(\phi^A\phi^A)^2.$$

6. Reduction of the scalar kinetic term and emergence of the 4D adjoint covariant derivative

6.1 The KK-covariant derivative on scalars

For a higher-dimensional scalar $\varphi(x,y)$, the KK ansatz implies that derivatives along $x^\mu$ become gauge-covariant:

$$\boxed{\ D_\mu\varphi := \partial_\mu\varphi - A_\mu^A(x)\,K_A^m(y)\,\partial_m\varphi\ }$$

Now insert the truncation $\varphi=\phi^B(x)\mu_B(y)$:

$$D_\mu\varphi = (\partial_\mu\phi^B)\mu_B - A_\mu^A\,\phi^B\,K_A(\mu_B).$$

Using $K_A(\mu_B)=f_{AB}{}^C\mu_C$:

$$D_\mu\varphi=\big(\partial_\mu\phi^C + f_{AB}{}^C A_\mu^A\phi^B\big)\mu_C.$$

Therefore the 4D fields transform with the adjoint covariant derivative

$$\boxed{\ D_\mu\phi^C = \partial_\mu\phi^C + f_{AB}{}^C A_\mu^A\phi^B\ }$$

6.2 Reduce the $x$-derivative kinetic term

The higher-dimensional kinetic term contributes

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(x)}=-\frac12\int d^8X\,\sqrt{-G}\,G^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\varphi\,\partial_\nu\varphi \ \longrightarrow\ -\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;\Big[b^4\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\sqrt{\hat g}\;\mu_A\mu_B\Big]\,D_\mu\phi^A D^\mu\phi^B.$$

Using $I_2{}_{AB}=\mathrm{Vol}\,\delta_{AB}/24$:

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(x)}=-\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;\underbrace{\Big(\frac{b^4\,\mathrm{Vol}}{24}\Big)}_{=:Z}\;\delta_{AB}\,D_\mu\phi^A D^\mu\phi^B.$$

Define the canonically normalized 4D Higgs multiplet

$$\boxed{\ \Phi^A := \sqrt{Z}\,\phi^A,\qquad Z=\frac{b^4\,\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}{24}\ }$$

Then

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(x)}=-\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;\delta_{AB}\,D_\mu\Phi^A D^\mu\Phi^B.$$

7. KK mass term from internal gradients (the geometric contribution)

The internal part of the kinetic term gives a positive mass contribution:

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(y)}=-\frac12\int d^8X\,\sqrt{-G}\,G^{mn}\partial_m\varphi\,\partial_n\varphi.$$

Using $\sqrt{-G}=\sqrt{-g_4}\,b^4\sqrt{\hat g}$ and $G^{mn}=b^{-2}\hat g^{mn}$:

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(y)} =-\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;b^2\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\sqrt{\hat g}\;\hat g^{mn}\partial_m\varphi\,\partial_n\varphi.$$

Insert $\varphi=\phi^A\mu_A$:

$$\hat g^{mn}\partial_m\varphi\,\partial_n\varphi =\phi^A\phi^B\,\hat g^{mn}\partial_m\mu_A\,\partial_n\mu_B.$$

Integrate by parts:

$$\int\sqrt{\hat g}\;\hat g^{mn}\partial_m\mu_A\,\partial_n\mu_B =-\int\sqrt{\hat g}\;\mu_A\,\hat\Delta \mu_B.$$

Use $\hat\Delta\mu_B=12\,\mu_B$:

$$\int\sqrt{\hat g}\;\hat g^{mn}\partial_m\mu_A\,\partial_n\mu_B =12\int\sqrt{\hat g}\;\mu_A\mu_B =12\cdot \frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{24}\delta_{AB} =\frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{2}\delta_{AB}.$$

Therefore

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(y)} =-\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;b^2\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{2}\;\delta_{AB}\,\phi^A\phi^B.$$

Rewrite in canonical variables $\Phi^A=\sqrt{Z}\phi^A$ with $Z=b^4\mathrm{Vol}/24$:

$$S_{\rm kin}^{(y)} =-\frac12\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;\delta_{AB}\,\Big(\frac{12}{b^2}\Big)\,\Phi^A\Phi^B.$$
Geometric KK mass. $$\boxed{\ m_{\rm KK}^2=\frac{12}{b^2}\ }$$

8. Reduction of the 8D local potential $U_8(\varphi)$

The 8D potential contributes to the 4D potential via

$$S_{U}=-\int d^8X\,\sqrt{-G}\;U_8(\varphi) =-\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g_4}\;\underbrace{b^4\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\sqrt{\hat g}\;U_8(\varphi)}_{=: \int d^4x\sqrt{-g_4}\,V_U(\phi)}.$$

8.1 Quadratic term

From $U_8\supset -\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\varphi^2$:

$$V_U^{(2)}(\phi)= b^4\left(-\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\right)\int \sqrt{\hat g}\;(\phi^A\mu_A)^2 = b^4\left(-\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\right)\phi^A\phi^B I_2{}_{AB}.$$

Use $I_2{}_{AB}=\mathrm{Vol}\,\delta_{AB}/24$:

$$V_U^{(2)}(\phi)= -\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\;\underbrace{\Big(\frac{b^4\mathrm{Vol}}{24}\Big)}_{Z}\;\phi^A\phi^A =-\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\;\Phi^A\Phi^A.$$

So in canonical variables:

$$\boxed{\ V_U^{(2)}(\Phi)= -\frac{\mu_8^2}{2}\,\Phi^A\Phi^A\ }$$

8.2 Cubic term (this generates the $d_{ABC}$ invariant)

From $U_8\supset \frac{g_8}{3}\varphi^3$:

$$V_U^{(3)}(\phi)= b^4\frac{g_8}{3}\int \sqrt{\hat g}\;(\phi^A\mu_A)^3 =b^4\frac{g_8}{3}\phi^A\phi^B\phi^C\,I_3{}_{ABC}.$$

Use $I_3{}_{ABC}=\mathrm{Vol}\,d_{ABC}/120$:

$$V_U^{(3)}(\phi)= b^4\frac{g_8}{3}\cdot \frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{120}\;d_{ABC}\phi^A\phi^B\phi^C.$$

Convert to canonical fields $\Phi=\sqrt{Z}\phi$:

$$\phi^A\phi^B\phi^C=\frac{\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C}{Z^{3/2}},\qquad Z=\frac{b^4\mathrm{Vol}}{24}.$$

Hence

$$V_U^{(3)}(\Phi)=\frac{\kappa}{3}\,d_{ABC}\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C,$$

with the explicit 4D cubic coupling

$$\boxed{\ \kappa = g_8\,\frac{b^4\mathrm{Vol}}{120}\cdot \frac{1}{Z^{3/2}} = g_8\,\frac{24^{3/2}}{120}\cdot \frac{1}{b^2\sqrt{\mathrm{Vol}}} = g_8\,\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}\cdot \frac{1}{b^2\sqrt{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}}\ }$$

8.3 Quartic term

From $U_8\supset \frac{\lambda_8}{4}\varphi^4$:

$$V_U^{(4)}(\phi)=b^4\frac{\lambda_8}{4}\int\sqrt{\hat g}\;(\phi^A\mu_A)^4 =b^4\frac{\lambda_8}{4}\cdot \frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{240}\,(\phi^A\phi^A)^2.$$

Now $(\phi^A\phi^A)^2=(\Phi^A\Phi^A)^2/Z^2$, thus

$$V_U^{(4)}(\Phi)=\frac{\lambda}{4}\,(\Phi^A\Phi^A)^2,$$

with

$$\boxed{\ \lambda = 4\cdot b^4\frac{\lambda_8}{4}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{Vol}}{240}\cdot\frac{1}{Z^2} =\lambda_8\cdot\frac{b^4\mathrm{Vol}}{240}\cdot\frac{24^2}{b^8\mathrm{Vol}^2} =\lambda_8\cdot\frac{576}{240}\cdot\frac{1}{b^4\mathrm{Vol}} =\lambda_8\cdot\frac{12}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{b^4\,\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}\ }$$

9. Final 4D Higgs potential (fully derived)

Collect all contributions:

  • Geometric KK mass: $+\frac12 m_{\rm KK}^2\,\Phi^2$ with $m_{\rm KK}^2=12/b^2$
  • 8D quadratic: $-\frac12\mu_8^2\,\Phi^2$
  • 8D cubic: $+\frac{\kappa}{3}d_{ABC}\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C$
  • 8D quartic: $+\frac{\lambda}{4}(\Phi^2)^2$

Therefore the complete 4D effective potential in this truncation is

$$\boxed{ V(\Phi)=\frac12\Big(\frac{12}{b^2}-\mu_8^2\Big)\,\Phi^A\Phi^A +\frac{\kappa}{3}\,d_{ABC}\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C +\frac{\lambda}{4}\,(\Phi^A\Phi^A)^2 }$$

with the couplings explicitly expressed in terms of 8D parameters and geometry:

$$\boxed{ \kappa = g_8\,\frac{2\sqrt{6}}{5}\,\frac{1}{b^2\sqrt{\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}}, \qquad \lambda = \lambda_8\,\frac{12}{5}\,\frac{1}{b^4\,\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}, \qquad \mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\frac{\pi^2}{2}. }$$
Condition for spontaneous symmetry breaking (tree level). The mass parameter is $$m_{\rm eff}^2=\frac{12}{b^2}-\mu_8^2.$$ Symmetry breaking requires $m_{\rm eff}^2<0$, i.e. $$\boxed{\ \mu_8^2>\frac{12}{b^2}\ }$$ so that the tachyon from $U_8$ dominates the positive KK mass.

10. $SU(3)\to SU(2)\times U(1)$ from an adjoint VEV: complete algebra

10.1 Choose the vacuum direction

Take an adjoint vacuum expectation value aligned with the Cartan generator $T_8$:

$$\boxed{\ \langle\Phi^A\rangle = v\,\delta^{A8}\ }$$

Recall

$$T_8=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2),\qquad d_{888}=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.$$

10.2 Reduce $V(\Phi)$ along this direction

Insert $\Phi^A=v\delta^{A8}$:

$$\Phi^A\Phi^A=v^2,\qquad d_{ABC}\Phi^A\Phi^B\Phi^C=d_{888}v^3=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}v^3.$$

Therefore

$$V(v)=\frac12 m_{\rm eff}^2 v^2 + \frac{\kappa}{3}\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\right)v^3+\frac{\lambda}{4}v^4 =\frac12 m_{\rm eff}^2 v^2 -\frac{\kappa\sqrt{3}}{9}v^3+\frac{\lambda}{4}v^4.$$

10.3 Extremum equation

Compute $dV/dv=0$:

$$\frac{dV}{dv}= m_{\rm eff}^2 v -\frac{\kappa\sqrt{3}}{3}v^2+\lambda v^3=0.$$

Nontrivial solutions satisfy

$$\boxed{\ \lambda v^2-\frac{\kappa\sqrt{3}}{3}v+m_{\rm eff}^2=0\ }$$

Hence

$$\boxed{\ v=\frac{\frac{\kappa\sqrt{3}}{3}\pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\kappa\sqrt{3}}{3}\right)^2-4\lambda m_{\rm eff}^2}}{2\lambda}\ }$$
If $\kappa=0$, this reduces to the familiar $v=\sqrt{-m_{\rm eff}^2/\lambda}$ (requiring $m_{\rm eff}^2<0$).

10.4 Identify the unbroken subgroup

The unbroken gauge algebra is the centralizer of $T_8$ inside $\mathfrak{su}(3)$:

$$\mathfrak{h}=\{X\in\mathfrak{su}(3)\;|\;[X,T_8]=0\}.$$

In the Gell–Mann basis one checks explicitly

$$[T_8,T_1]=[T_8,T_2]=[T_8,T_3]=0,\qquad [T_8,T_8]=0,$$

but

$$[T_8,T_4]\neq 0,\quad [T_8,T_5]\neq 0,\quad [T_8,T_6]\neq 0,\quad [T_8,T_7]\neq 0.$$

Therefore the unbroken algebra is

$$\boxed{\ \mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)=\langle T_1,T_2,T_3\rangle\oplus\langle T_8\rangle\ }$$

Exponentiating gives

$$\boxed{\ SU(3)\ \longrightarrow\ SU(2)\times U(1)\ }$$

11. Gauge boson mass spectrum after breaking (explicit)

The gauge boson masses follow from the Higgs kinetic term $\frac12(D_\mu\Phi^A)(D^\mu\Phi^A)$ expanded about $\langle\Phi\rangle=v\delta^{A8}$.

Using

$$D_\mu\Phi^A=\partial_\mu\Phi^A+f_{BC}{}^A A_\mu^B \Phi^C,$$

we get at the vacuum (constant $v$):

$$D_\mu\langle\Phi^A\rangle= f_{B8}{}^A\,A_\mu^B\,v.$$

Thus the vector mass matrix is

$$\boxed{\ (M^2)_{BB'} = v^2\,f_{B8}{}^A f_{B'8}{}^A\ }$$

In SU(3), the only nonzero structure constants involving index $8$ in this sector are

$$f_{458}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\qquad f_{678}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

Therefore

  • $A_\mu^{1,2,3,8}$ remain massless (unbroken $SU(2)\times U(1)$).
  • $A_\mu^{4,5,6,7}$ become massive with
$$\boxed{\ m_{4,5,6,7}^2=\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^2 v^2=\frac{3}{4}v^2,\qquad m_{4,5,6,7}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\,v\ }$$
If you reinsert the 4D gauge coupling constant $g_4$ (coming from the metric reduction), replace $v\to g_4 v$: $m^2=\frac{3}{4}g_4^2 v^2$.

12. Optional: expression for $g_4$ from the pure gravity KK reduction

From the 8D Einstein–Hilbert term, the SU(3) gauge kinetic term is

$$S\supset -\frac14\int d^4x\sqrt{-g_4}\left(\frac{1}{g_4^2}\delta_{AB}\right)F_{\mu\nu}^A F^{B\mu\nu},$$

with (for one $\mathbb{CP}^2$)

$$\boxed{\ \frac{1}{g_4^2}\delta_{AB}=\frac{b^6}{2\kappa_8^2}\int_{\mathbb{CP}^2}\sqrt{\hat g}\;\hat g_{mn}K_A^m K_B^n =\frac{b^6\,\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{CP}^2)}{4\kappa_8^2}\,\delta_{AB}\ }$$
